# **Hippocrates & Galen – Four Temperaments (Humoral Theory of Personality)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Hippocrates (c. 460–370 BCE):** Greek physician, known as the Father of Modern Medicine. Proposed that health depends on the balance of four bodily fluids called **humors**.
- **Galen (129–c. 200 CE):** Greek physician who systematised Hippocrates' humoral theory and connected the predominance of each humor with a personality temperament.
- The theory is one of the earliest systematic **type theories of personality**.
- It influenced Western medicine and personality thought for over 1,000 years.

**Important distinction:**

- Hippocrates → Originated the humoral theory.
- Galen → Linked the four humors to four personality temperaments.

## **2. Four Humors and Four Temperaments**

Essential NET matching question

| Humor       | Bodily source (as given in the theory) | Temperament     | Characteristics                              |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Blood       | Heart                                  | **Sanguine**    | Cheerful, optimistic, enthusiastic, sociable |
| Phlegm      | Lungs                                  | **Phlegmatic**  | Calm, reliable, slow, apathetic              |
| Yellow bile | Liver                                  | **Choleric**    | Ambitious, bold, irritable, quick-tempered   |
| Black bile  | Kidneys                                | **Melancholic** | Sad, anxious, reserved, unhappy              |

**Easy memory trick:**

- Sanguine = Blood = Cheerful.
- Phlegmatic = Phlegm = Calm.
- Choleric = Yellow bile = Angry.
- Melancholic = Black bile = Sad.

## **3. Core Philosophy – Humoral Theory**

### **Hippocrates**

- **Eucrasia:** Balance of the four humors → Health.
- **Dyscrasia:** Imbalance of the four humors → Illness.
- Personality and physical conditions were understood through bodily processes.

### **Galen**

- Elaborated the humoral theory.
- Proposed that the **predominance of a particular humor** produces a particular temperament.
- His classification became influential for more than 1,000 years.

**NET point:** This is an early **biological/physiological approach** to explaining personality.

## **4. Later Developments**

### **A. Immanuel Kant**

- Accepted the four temperament categories.
- Considered them mutually exclusive, with **no overlap** between categories.
- Developed descriptive characteristics for each temperament.

### **B. Wilhelm Wundt**

Wundt described temperament using two dimensions:

1. **Emotional vs. Non-emotional**
2. **Changeable vs. Unchangeable**

| Temperament | Emotionality  | Changeability |
| ----------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| Melancholic | Emotional     | Unchangeable  |
| Choleric    | Emotional     | Changeable    |
| Phlegmatic  | Non-emotional | Unchangeable  |
| Sanguine    | Non-emotional | Changeable    |

**NET trap:** Wundt's two dimensions are not the original four humors. They are a later psychological reformulation.

## **5. Type Theory vs. Trait Theory**

| Type theory                                | Trait theory                                 |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Classifies people into distinct categories | Describes people along continuous dimensions |
| Categorical approach                       | Dimensional approach                         |
| Example: Four temperaments                 | Example: Big Five                            |
| People are assigned to types               | People differ in degree of traits            |

## **6. Contributions**

1. One of the earliest systematic classifications of personality.
2. Introduced a physiological explanation of individual differences.
3. Established enduring temperament terminology.
4. Influenced later temperament classifications, including those of Kant and Wundt.
5. Represents an early attempt to connect biological and psychological characteristics.

## **7. Criticisms**

1. **Lack of empirical evidence:** The connection between bodily humors and personality is rejected by modern science.
2. **Oversimplification:** Four categories cannot adequately explain the complexity of personality.
3. **Incorrect biological assumptions:** Bodily fluids do not determine personality in the proposed manner.
4. **Limited explanatory value:** The theory does not provide scientifically valid mechanisms for personality development.
