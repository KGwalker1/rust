# **William Sheldon – Constitutional Theory (Somatotype Theory of Personality)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **William Herbert Sheldon (1899–1970):** American psychologist and physician who developed the **Somatotype Theory**.
- Proposed a relationship between **body build (physique) and temperament**.
- Extended Kretschmer's constitutional approach using quantitative measurements.
- Unlike Kretschmer, Sheldon rejected discrete body types: every individual possesses all three somatotype components in different proportions.

**Important works:**

- _Varieties of Human Physique_ (1940).
- _Varieties of Temperament_ (1942).
- _Varieties of Delinquent Youth_ (1949).

**NET point:** Sheldon = Somatotypes + 7-point scale + three-digit body profile.

## **2. Three Somatotypes**

Sheldon associated body constitution with the development of the **three embryonic layers**.

Important NET matching topic

| Somatotype     | Embryonic layer | Physical characteristics                              |
| -------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Endomorphy** | Endoderm        | Soft, round, plump; developed digestive system        |
| **Mesomorphy** | Mesoderm        | Muscular, strong bones, athletic build                |
| **Ectomorphy** | Ectoderm        | Thin, linear, fragile; associated with nervous system |

### **Seven-point rating scale**

- Each component is rated from **1 to 7**.
- 1 = Lowest development; 7 = Highest development.
- Somatotype is represented by a **three-digit score**.

Example: **1–6–3**

- 1 = Low endomorphy.
- 6 = High mesomorphy.
- 3 = Moderate ectomorphy.

**Important:** No individual is purely one somatotype. Everyone has all three components in varying proportions.

### **Comparison with Kretschmer**

| Kretschmer         | Sheldon   |
| ------------------ | --------- |
| Pyknic             | Endomorph |
| Athletic           | Mesomorph |
| Leptosome/Asthenic | Ectomorph |

## **3. Three Temperaments**

Sheldon identified three temperament dimensions through correlational analysis of 50 trait descriptors rated on a seven-point scale.

| Temperament      | Associated somatotype | Characteristics                                              |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Viscerotonia** | Endomorphy            | Comfort-loving, sociable, relaxed, enjoys food and affection |
| **Somatotonia**  | Mesomorphy            | Assertive, adventurous, courageous, energetic, risk-taking   |
| **Cerebrotonia** | Ectomorphy            | Reserved, inhibited, private, sensitive, introverted         |

### **Easy memory trick**

- **Endomorph → Viscerotonia → Comfort and social connection.**
- **Mesomorph → Somatotonia → Action and adventure.**
- **Ectomorph → Cerebrotonia → Thought and privacy.**

**NET trap:** Viscerotonia, Somatotonia and Cerebrotonia are temperament dimensions, not separate body types.

## **4. Core Philosophy – Constitution and Correlation**

Sheldon proposed that physique and temperament are correlated because they share an underlying biological basis.

Important ideas:

1. **Common biological basis:** Physical constitution and temperament are related to the development of embryonic layers.
2. **Continuous dimensions:** Individuals possess all three components in different degrees.
3. **Quantitative measurement:** Somatotypes are rated on a seven-point scale.
4. **Environmental influence:** Nutrition and early experiences can also influence physique and temperament.

### **Sheldon vs. Kretschmer**

| Kretschmer                          | Sheldon                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Discrete constitutional types       | Continuous dimensions                     |
| Descriptive/clinical classification | Quantitative measurement                  |
| Pyknic, Leptosome, Athletic         | Endomorph, Mesomorph, Ectomorph           |
| No three-digit scoring system       | Seven-point scale and three-digit profile |

**Most important distinction:** Kretschmer classified people into body types; Sheldon measured the degree to which each person possessed all three components.

## **5. Criticisms and Empirical Status**

Sheldon's personality theory has been extensively criticised.

### **Methodological criticisms**

1. **Subjective ratings:** Somatotypes were assessed visually using photographs, creating observer bias.
2. **Sample bias:** Samples were not sufficiently representative.
3. **Poor standardisation:** Different observers could produce different ratings.

### **Empirical criticism**

- **Hood (1963):** A study involving approximately 10,000 male college freshmen found virtually no relationship between body type and temperament.
- Subsequent studies failed to consistently replicate Sheldon's proposed physique–personality correlations.
- His claim connecting mesomorphy with delinquency was also criticised.

### **Conceptual criticisms**

- **Determinism:** Overemphasised the role of physical constitution.
- **Oversimplification:** Three body components cannot adequately explain complex personality differences.
- **Environmental neglect:** Social and cultural influences were insufficiently considered.

**NET point:** Hood (1963) is particularly important as an empirical criticism of Sheldon's theory.

## **6. Comparison with Other Constitutional Theorists**

| Feature        | Hippocrates/Galen                           | Kretschmer                  | Sheldon                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Basis          | Bodily humors                               | Body constitution           | Embryonic layers                        |
| Classification | 4 temperaments                              | 3 main body types           | 3 somatotypes                           |
| Types          | Sanguine, Phlegmatic, Choleric, Melancholic | Pyknic, Leptosome, Athletic | Endomorph, Mesomorph, Ectomorph         |
| Temperaments   | Based on humor predominance                 | Cycloid, Schizoid           | Viscerotonia, Somatotonia, Cerebrotonia |
| Method         | Humoral classification                      | Clinical observation        | 7-point quantitative rating             |

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed a quantitative somatotype classification system.
2. Improved upon Kretschmer's descriptive approach through numerical ratings.
3. Stimulated research into relationships between physique and behaviour.
4. Influenced later somatotype assessment, particularly the **Heath–Carter method**, used in sports science.
5. Established an important historical connection between constitutional theories and biological approaches to personality.
