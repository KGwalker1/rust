# **Friedman & Rosenman – Type A/Type B Personality Theory**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Meyer Friedman (1910–2001)** and **Ray H. Rosenman (b. 1920):** American cardiologists who proposed the Type A Behavior Pattern.
- Originated from their research on behaviour and cardiovascular disease.
- **1959:** Landmark paper, _Association of Specific Overt Behavior Pattern with Blood and Cardiovascular Findings_, published in _JAMA_.
- Famous observation: Friedman noticed worn upholstery on the front edges of waiting-room chairs, suggesting patients sat restlessly on the edge of their seats.
- Described Type A as an **action-emotion complex**.

**NET focus:** They preferred **Type A Behavior Pattern (TABP)** rather than Type A personality because it describes an observable response pattern, not necessarily a fixed personality trait.

## **2. Core Theory: Type A Behavior Pattern**

TABP refers to a pattern of behaviour involving intense competition, excessive achievement striving and chronic time urgency.

Three defining characteristics:

| Component           | Characteristics                                                                       |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Competitiveness** | Excessive achievement drive, ambition and constant striving                           |
| **Time urgency**    | Impatience, hurry sickness, rapid speech and doing multiple activities simultaneously |
| **Hostility**       | Irritability, cynicism, mistrust and aggression                                       |

Other characteristics include:

- Persistent desire for recognition and advancement.
- Involvement in multiple activities.
- Rapid execution of physical and mental tasks.
- High mental and physical alertness.

Important NET point

**Hostility** was later identified as the most toxic component and the strongest predictor of cardiovascular problems among the Type A characteristics.

## **3. Type B Behavior Pattern**

Type B is characterised by the **relative absence of Type A characteristics**.

Typical features:

- Relaxed and patient.
- Less competitive and less achievement-driven.
- Less concerned with time pressure.
- Able to relax without guilt.
- Reflective and less hurried in decision-making.

**NET distinction:** Type B is generally described through the relative absence of Type A characteristics, rather than as a separate positive behavioural syndrome.

## **4. Western Collaborative Group Study (WCGS)**

The **Western Collaborative Group Study** was the landmark prospective longitudinal investigation of the Type A–coronary heart disease relationship.

Important features:

- Followed thousands of initially healthy men.
- Participants were classified using the **Structured Interview (SI)**.
- Type A individuals were approximately **twice as likely** to develop coronary heart disease (CHD) as Type B individuals.
- Early findings suggested a strong independent association between TABP and CHD.

**NET focus:** WCGS = landmark study connecting Type A behaviour with coronary heart disease.

## **5. Assessment of Type A Behaviour**

### **Structured Interview (SI)**

Gold standard

- Developed by Friedman and Rosenman.
- Assesses both what individuals say and **how they say it**.
- Examines speech speed, volume, hostility, motor tension and impatience.
- Interviewers may deliberately interrupt or speak slowly to elicit Type A responses.

### **Jenkins Activity Survey (JAS)**

- Self-report measure of Type A behaviour.
- Less predictive of CHD than the Structured Interview, according to the supplied notes.

| Measure                       | Nature                               |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Structured Interview (SI)     | Behavioural interview; gold standard |
| Jenkins Activity Survey (JAS) | Self-report questionnaire            |

## **6. Decline of the Broad Type A–CHD Relationship**

Later research failed to consistently replicate the strong association between the overall Type A pattern and coronary heart disease.

- By the mid-1980s, the broad Type A–CHD relationship was increasingly questioned.
- Booth-Kewley and Friedman found only a modest relationship between Type A behaviour and CHD.
- Researchers shifted attention from the entire Type A pattern to specific components, especially hostility.

### **Hostility: The Toxic Component**

- **Cynical hostility** involves mistrust, resentment and chronic anger.
- Later research identified hostility as more strongly associated with cardiovascular risk than competitiveness or time urgency alone.

### **Tobacco Industry Controversy**

- A 2012 _American Journal of Public Health_ analysis examined tobacco industry documents.
- It reported that the tobacco industry was a major funder of Type A research.
- Selected findings were reportedly used to counter concerns about tobacco and health.

## **7. Comparison with Other Type Theorists**

| Theorist                | Basis                            | Types                           |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Hippocrates & Galen     | Bodily humors                    | Four temperaments               |
| Kretschmer              | Body constitution                | Pyknic, Leptosome, Athletic     |
| Sheldon                 | Somatotypes                      | Endomorph, Mesomorph, Ectomorph |
| Spranger                | Cultural values                  | Six value types                 |
| **Friedman & Rosenman** | **Observable behaviour pattern** | **Type A and Type B**           |

Friedman and Rosenman are distinctive because their typology focuses on **behavioural patterns and cardiovascular health**, rather than biological constitution or cultural values.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Connected behavioural patterns with physical health.
2. Contributed to the development of health psychology and behavioural medicine.
3. Introduced the Type A Behavior Pattern concept.
4. Developed the Structured Interview as a behavioural assessment method.
5. Stimulated further research into hostility, stress and cardiovascular disease.

## **9. Criticisms**

- **Replication problems:** The broad Type A–CHD association was not consistently replicated.
- **Heterogeneity:** Competitiveness, time urgency and hostility are not necessarily strongly correlated.
- **Oversimplification:** Dividing individuals into only two behavioural types is restrictive.
- **Measurement issues:** Self-report measures such as JAS were less predictive than the Structured Interview.
- **Research controversy:** Tobacco industry funding raised questions about the research record.
