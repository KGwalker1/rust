### 🎯 White's Effectance Motivation / Competence Motivation

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Robert W. White (1959) proposed that humans possess an innate, **non-homeostatic** motivation to interact effectively with their environment. He called this **effectance motivation**, and the cumulative result of these interactions is **competence** .
- **Key Insight**: White explicitly challenged Hull's drive-reduction theory and Freud's instinct theory. He argued these frameworks could not explain **exploratory, playful, and mastery behaviors** that occur without any biological deficit or external reward .

**2. Key Concepts (Must Know for NET)**

| Concept                   | Meaning                                                                                                             |
| :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Effectance Motivation** | The intrinsic drive to produce an intended effect on the environment and experience a **feeling of efficacy**       |
| **Competence**            | The capacity to interact effectively with the environment; built cumulatively through repeated effectance strivings |
| **Feeling of Efficacy**   | The subjective satisfaction derived from successfully mastering or influencing the environment                      |

**3. How It Works**

- **Mechanism**: Effectance motivation → Exploratory/manipulatory behavior → Intended effect produced → **Feeling of efficacy** → Behavior reinforced → Competence develops over time .
- **Developmental Course**: In infancy, effectance behavior is diffuse (e.g., random shaking). Through experience, it becomes **focused and differentiated**, building an enduring sense of personal competence .
- **Biological Basis**: White argued the need for competence is **biologically fundamental**—observable across species in play and exploration—but distinct from physiological drives like hunger .

**4. Key Distinctions from Other Theories**

- **vs. Hull's Drive Reduction**: Drive theory says behavior aims to **reduce tension**. White says effectance behavior aims to **produce effects** and increase engagement—it is not about restoring homeostasis .
- **vs. Harlow's Manipulation Drive**: Harlow provided the empirical puzzle-monkey evidence. White provided the broader **theoretical framework**, positioning competence as a primary motivational system rivaling biological drives .
- **Legacy**: White's work directly influenced **Deci & Ryan's Self-Determination Theory (SDT)**, where "competence" became one of the three basic psychological needs .

**5. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Operationalization Difficulty**: The "feeling of efficacy" is subjective and hard to measure in laboratory settings compared to concrete drives like hunger .
- **Behaviorist Challenge**: Some argued exploratory behavior could be explained by secondary reinforcement, not an autonomous drive. White countered that the **persistence without external reward** supports an intrinsic system .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **White = Effectance Motivation = Competence**. Link **exploration → feeling of efficacy → competence**. Know that White **challenged drive-reduction theory** by arguing this motivation is **non-homeostatic** (not about reducing tension). This is the direct theoretical bridge from Harlow's monkey experiments to Deci & Ryan's SDT.
