### 🎯 Harlow's Manipulation Drive & Intrinsic Motivation

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Harry Harlow proposed that organisms possess an innate **manipulation drive**—a motivation to actively explore, handle, and solve problems for no external reward (like food or water). This drive is **intrinsic** and self-sustaining, not derived from homeostatic needs .
- **Key Insight**: Harlow argued that this class of motivation is **as primary and important as homeostatic drives** (hunger, thirst). It directly challenged the dominant drive-reduction theories of his time .

**2. The Classic Experiment (Harlow, Harlow & Meyer, 1950)**

- **Subjects**: Rhesus monkeys.
- **Procedure**: Monkeys were given a mechanical puzzle to manipulate for 12–13 days with **no food or water incentive**. A control group had unassembled puzzles in their home cages .
- **Key Findings**:
  - The experimental monkeys **learned to disassemble the puzzle efficiently** without any extrinsic reward .
  - When food (a raisin) was later introduced as a reward, it **disrupted** performance. Monkeys made more errors and were less likely to attempt the puzzle afterward .
- **Interpretation**: The manipulation drive is strong and persistent. Extrinsic rewards can **undermine** intrinsic motivation (an early demonstration of what later became known as the overjustification effect) .

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Manipulation Drive**: The innate tendency to actively engage with and manipulate objects. It is **self-sustaining** and does not depend on internal deficits like hunger .
- **Intrinsic Motivation**: Harlow is credited with coining the term. The behavior is performed for its own sake, not as a means to an end .
- **Challenge to Drive Theory**: Harlow's work provided powerful evidence against the simplistic view that all behavior is aimed at reducing biological tension .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Methodological Flaws**: The original study had very few subjects, short observation periods, and only the experimental group was food-deprived. The disruption effect could have been temporary .
- **Later Career Shift**: Harlow largely abandoned this line of research, later becoming famous for his (controversial) studies on contact comfort and attachment .
