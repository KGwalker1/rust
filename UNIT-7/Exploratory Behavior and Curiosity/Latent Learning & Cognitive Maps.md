### 🎯 Tolman: Latent Learning & Cognitive Maps

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Edward C. Tolman (a behaviorist with a cognitive twist) argued that learning is **not** just a Stimulus-Response (S-R) connection. Organisms learn the **"what leads to what"** (expectations) and build a **cognitive map**—a mental representation of the environment's layout .
- **Key Insight**: Learning can occur **without reinforcement** and remains hidden (latent) until a motivation/reward is introduced to demonstrate it. This directly challenged Hull's drive-reduction and Thorndike's law of effect .

**2. The Classic Experiment (Tolman & Honzik, 1930)**

- **Setup**: Three groups of rats in a complex maze .
  - **Group A (No Reward)**: Ran the maze daily with no food at the end.
  - **Group B (Reward)**: Always received food at the goal.
  - **Group C (Latent)**: No reward for the first 10 days; food introduced on Day 11.
- **Key Findings**:
  - Group C made errors at a rate similar to Group A (no reward) for the first 10 days.
  - **The "Aha" Moment**: As soon as food was introduced on Day 11, Group C's error rate **plummeted**, performing as well as (or better than) Group B almost immediately .
- **Interpretation**: Group C had been learning the maze layout all along; the food merely provided the **incentive** to demonstrate that learning. This is **latent learning** .

**3. Key Concepts (Must Know for NET)**

| Concept             | Meaning                                                                                                                     |
| :------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Latent Learning** | Learning that is not immediately expressed in behavior; it remains "hidden" until a reason (incentive) to perform appears . |
| **Cognitive Map**   | A mental picture/representation of the spatial layout of the environment (the maze) .                                       |
| **Expectancy**      | Tolman argued animals learn **expectations** (e.g., "turning left here leads to food"), not just S-R habits .               |

**4. Criticisms & Caveats**

- **Replication Issues**: Tolman's famous "sunburst maze" shortcut experiment (1946) faced replication failures. Later analyses suggest some results might be explainable by S-R theory or specific experimental conditions .
- **Modern View**: While the strict "cognitive map vs. S-R" dichotomy is debated, the **concept** of cognitive maps is now central to neuroscience (e.g., place cells in the hippocampus) .
