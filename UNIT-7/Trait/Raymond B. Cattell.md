# **Raymond B. Cattell – Factor-Analytic Trait Theory**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Raymond B. Cattell (1905–1998)**: British-American psychologist.
- PhD (1929), University of London, under **Charles Spearman**.
- Applied **factor analysis** to personality.
- Identified **16 source traits**, measured through the **16 PF Questionnaire**.
- Major works: _The Description and Measurement of Personality_ (1946), _Personality_ (1950), _The Scientific Analysis of Personality_ (1965).
- **Approach:** Nomothetic and factor-analytic.

## **2. Multivariate Experimental Method**

Cattell argued that personality should be studied by measuring several variables simultaneously rather than studying one variable in isolation.

**Three data sources:**

| Data       | Meaning         | Examples                              |
| ---------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **L-data** | Life records    | Grades, sick days, peer ratings       |
| **Q-data** | Questionnaire   | Self-reports, 16 PF                   |
| **T-data** | Objective tests | Reaction time, physiological measures |

**Key point:** Cattell used triangulation—validating personality traits through L, Q and T data.

## **3. Surface and Source Traits**

**Surface traits**

- Observable clusters of behaviours.
- Descriptive, situation-dependent.
- Initially identified **35 surface traits**.

**Source traits**

- Underlying, stable and causative personality factors.
- Control and explain surface behaviours.
- Identified **16 source traits** through factor analysis.

**Example:** Talkativeness, cheerfulness and outgoing behaviour may form a surface-trait cluster.

### **Classification of Source Traits**

| Basis   | Types          | Meaning                               |
| ------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Origin  | Constitutional | Biological/genetic                    |
|         | Environmental  | Learning and experience               |
| Content | Ability        | Effectiveness in achieving goals      |
|         | Temperament    | Style and emotional tone              |
|         | Dynamic        | Motivation and direction of behaviour |

**NET trap:** Surface traits = observable; source traits = underlying causes. The 16 PF measures source traits.

## **4. 16 PF Questionnaire**

- Developed by Cattell to measure **16 source traits**.
- Each factor is a bipolar dimension, not a personality type.
- It is based on **Q-data**.

| Factor                  | Low score      | High score    |
| ----------------------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| A – Warmth              | Reserved       | Warm          |
| B – Reasoning           | Concrete       | Abstract      |
| C – Emotional Stability | Reactive       | Stable        |
| E – Dominance           | Deferential    | Dominant      |
| F – Liveliness          | Serious        | Lively        |
| G – Rule-Consciousness  | Expedient      | Dutiful       |
| H – Social Boldness     | Timid          | Venturesome   |
| I – Sensitivity         | Utilitarian    | Sensitive     |
| L – Vigilance           | Trusting       | Suspicious    |
| M – Abstractedness      | Practical      | Imaginative   |
| N – Privateness         | Forthright     | Private       |
| O – Apprehension        | Self-assured   | Apprehensive  |
| Q1 – Openness to Change | Conservative   | Experimenting |
| Q2 – Self-Reliance      | Group-oriented | Self-reliant  |
| Q3 – Perfectionism      | Casual         | Organized     |

## **5. Higher-Order Factors**

Cattell further analysed the 16 primary factors to identify broader, second-order factors.

Examples:

- Extraversion–Introversion.
- Anxiety.
- Tough Poise–Emotionality.
- Independence–Subduedness.
- Superego Strength.

**Important:** Fluid intelligence (**gf**) and crystallized intelligence (**gc**) are treated as **second-order factors** in Cattell's system.

## **6. Dynamic Traits – Dynamic Lattice**

Cattell explained motivation through three interconnected levels:

**ATTITUDES**

Specific readiness to act in a situation

**SENTIMENTS**

Learned motivational patterns

**ERGS**

Innate, constitutional drives

Motivational foundation: Ergs → Sentiments → Attitudes

- **Ergs:** Innate biological drives; Cattell identified 11 primary ergs, including hunger, sex, curiosity, security and gregariousness.
- **Sentiments:** Learned motivational systems organised around important objects or institutions, such as family, career and religion.
- **Attitudes:** Specific interests or readiness to act in a particular situation.

**Subsidiation:** Attitudes serve sentiments, and sentiments serve ergs. This is the hierarchical relationship between the three levels.

**NET trap:** Ergs = innate; sentiments = learned; attitudes = situation-specific.

## **7. Comparison with Other Trait Theorists**

| Theorist           | Approach                    | Main contribution                      |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Allport**        | Idiographic                 | Cardinal, central and secondary traits |
| **Cattell**        | Nomothetic, factor analysis | 16 source traits, 16 PF                |
| **Eysenck**        | Nomothetic, factor analysis | PEN model                              |
| **McCrae & Costa** | Factor analysis             | Big Five (OCEAN)                       |

- Cattell and Eysenck represent the **factor-analytic nomothetic approach**.
- Cattell = 16 source traits; Eysenck = 3 superfactors.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Reduced thousands of personality descriptors to 35 surface traits and 16 source traits.
2. Developed the **16 PF Questionnaire**.
3. Established the importance of factor analysis in personality research.
4. Introduced the L, Q and T data classification.
5. Developed the Dynamic Lattice theory of motivation.
6. Contributed to the understanding of fluid and crystallized intelligence.

## **9. Criticisms**

1. Later studies have not consistently replicated all 16 factors.
2. Over-reliance on factor analysis; results depend partly on analytical choices.
3. Sixteen factors may be less practical than the Big Five.
4. Lacked a comprehensive theory of personality development.
5. English-language trait descriptions may create cultural bias.
6. Dynamic traits received less empirical research than the 16 PF.

## **11. Likely MCQ Traps**

- **35 vs. 16:** Surface traits = 35; source traits = 16.
- **L vs. Q vs. T:** Life records vs. questionnaires vs. objective tests.
- **16 PF:** Measures source traits and uses Q-data.
- **Observable vs. causative:** Surface traits are observable; source traits explain them.
- **Nature vs. nurture:** Ergs are innate; sentiments are learned.
- **Motivational order:** Ergs → Sentiments → Attitudes.
- **Subsidiation:** Attitudes serve sentiments, not the reverse.
- **Higher-order factors:** gf and gc are second-order factors.
- **Allport vs. Cattell:** Idiographic vs. nomothetic.
