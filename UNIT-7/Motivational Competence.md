# **Motivational Competence – Quick Revision Notes**

_UGC-NET Psychology | Unit 7_

## **1. Core Concepts**

- **Competence / Effectance Motivation – Robert W. White (1959):** Innate motivation to interact effectively with and master the environment.
- **Motivational Competence – Hugo Kehr (2004):** Ability to align implicit motives, explicit goals and perceived abilities to sustain motivated action.
- **Self-Efficacy – Albert Bandura (1977):** Belief in one's ability to perform a specific task or behaviour.

## **3. Important Models**

**A. White – Effectance Motivation**

- Intrinsic desire to master and influence the environment.
- Not based on biological drives such as hunger or thirst.
- Produces a sense of competence and efficacy.

**B. Bandura – Self-Efficacy**

Four sources of self-efficacy:

1. Mastery experiences – strongest source.
2. Vicarious experiences – observing others.
3. Verbal persuasion – encouragement and feedback.
4. Physiological and emotional states – interpreting arousal and feelings.

**C. Kehr – 3C Model of Motivational Competence (2004)**

- **Implicit motives:** Unconscious emotional preferences.
- **Explicit goals:** Conscious objectives.
- **Perceived abilities:** Beliefs about one's capabilities.

Motivational problems can arise when these components are incongruent.

**D. Kuhl – Action Control Theory**

- **Action orientation:** Initiating action and recovering from setbacks.
- **State orientation:** Remaining preoccupied with doubts, failures or emotional states.

**E. Gollwitzer – Implementation Intentions**

- Goal intention: "I intend to study Psychology."
- Implementation intention: "If it is 7 PM, then I will study Psychology for one hour."

Implementation intentions use **if–then plans** to reduce the intention–behaviour gap.

## **4. Four Important Dimensions**

| Dimension              | Associated theorist | Key idea                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Motive–goal congruence | Kehr                | Aligning implicit motives with explicit goals |
| Self-efficacy          | Bandura             | Belief in personal capability                 |
| Volitional control     | Kuhl, Gollwitzer    | Maintaining goal-directed action              |
| Adaptive attribution   | Bernard Weiner      | Explaining success and failure constructively |

Additional associations:

- **Carol Dweck:** Growth mindset and mastery goals.
- **Walter Mischel:** Delay of gratification.
- **Bernard Weiner:** Attribution theory—locus, stability and controllability.

## **5. Important NET Comparisons**

| Concept 1                                      | Concept 2                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| White: Innate motivation to master environment | Bandura: Belief in ability to perform                   |
| Self-efficacy: "Can I do it?"                  | Outcome expectancy: "Will doing it produce the result?" |
| Motivation: Goal selection                     | Volition: Goal implementation                           |
| Action orientation: Initiating action          | State orientation: Rumination and hesitation            |
| Goal intention: Intention to achieve           | Implementation intention: Specific if–then plan         |
