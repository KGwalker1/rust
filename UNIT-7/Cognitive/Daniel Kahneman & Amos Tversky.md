**UGC-NET JRF Psychology | December 2026**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Daniel Kahneman (1934–2024)** and **Amos Tversky (1937–1996)** were influential psychologists in judgment and decision-making.
- Their research established the **Heuristics and Biases Approach**.
- **1974:** _Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases_.
- **1979:** _Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk_.
- Kahneman received the **Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences (2002)**. Tversky was not eligible because he had died in 1996.

## **2. Heuristics and Biases (1974)**

**Heuristics:** Mental shortcuts used to make judgments under uncertainty. They simplify decisions but can produce systematic errors called biases.

### **Three Major Heuristics**

| Heuristic                    | Meaning                                                     | Associated bias / example                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Representativeness**       | Judging probability by similarity to a stereotype           | Base-rate neglect, gambler's fallacy, conjunction fallacy |
| **Availability**             | Judging probability by ease of recalling examples           | Overestimating vivid or recent events                     |
| **Anchoring and Adjustment** | Starting with an initial value and adjusting insufficiently | Judgments influenced by arbitrary numbers                 |

**Important examples:**

- Linda problem → Conjunction fallacy (representativeness).
- Ease of recalling plane crashes → Availability.
- Random number influencing an estimate → Anchoring.

## **3. Prospect Theory (1979)**

A **descriptive theory** explaining how people actually make decisions under risk, as opposed to how they should decide under expected utility theory.

### **Four Major Principles**

| Principle                   | Meaning                                                                                             |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Reference dependence**    | Outcomes evaluated as gains or losses relative to a reference point                                 |
| **Loss aversion**           | Losses psychologically matter more than equivalent gains                                            |
| **Diminishing sensitivity** | Additional gains or losses have decreasing marginal impact                                          |
| **Probability weighting**   | Small probabilities are often overweighted; moderate and high probabilities are often underweighted |

### **Certainty Effect**

- People prefer certain outcomes over merely probable outcomes.
- Contributes to risk aversion for gains and risk seeking for losses.

### **Framing Effect (1981)**

- Choices change depending on how equivalent information is presented.
- Gain frame → generally greater risk aversion.
- Loss frame → generally greater risk seeking.

## **4. Expected Utility vs. Prospect Theory**

| Expected Utility Theory               | Prospect Theory                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Normative                             | Descriptive                                      |
| How people should decide              | How people actually decide                       |
| Evaluates final outcomes              | Evaluates gains and losses                       |
| Assumes utility-based rational choice | Includes loss aversion and probability weighting |
