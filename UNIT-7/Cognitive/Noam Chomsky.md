# **Noam Chomsky – Cognitive & Linguistic Theory**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Noam Chomsky (born 1928)**: American linguist, philosopher and cognitive scientist; known as the **Father of Modern Linguistics**.
- His work challenged behaviourist explanations of language and helped establish the cognitive approach.
- **Major works:**
  - _Syntactic Structures_ (1957) – introduced transformational-generative grammar.
  - Review of Skinner's _Verbal Behavior_ (1959) – landmark criticism of behaviourist language theory.
- His central contribution was establishing language as a **biologically based human capacity**.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Mentalism and Innateness**

Chomsky argued that the human mind possesses an innate, rule-governed system for acquiring and using language.

**Key propositions:**

1. **Mentalism:** Psychology must study internal mental processes and representations, not merely observable behaviour.
2. **Innateness:** Humans possess a biologically based language faculty.
3. **Linguistic creativity:** Children produce and understand sentences they have never heard before.
4. **Competence vs. Performance:**

| Concept         | Meaning                                   |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Competence**  | Underlying knowledge of linguistic rules  |
| **Performance** | Actual use of language in real situations |

**NET point:** Competence = knowledge; Performance = actual language behaviour.

## **3. Transformational-Generative Grammar and Universal Grammar**

### **A. Transformational-Generative Grammar (1957)**

- Language is generated through a system of grammatical rules.
- Grammar can generate all and only the grammatical sentences of a language.
- **Deep structure:** Underlying representation of a sentence.
- **Surface structure:** Actual grammatical form expressed in language.
- Transformational rules connect deep and surface structures.

### **B. Universal Grammar (UG)**

**Universal Grammar** refers to the innate, species-specific linguistic principles shared across human languages.

Important concepts:

| Concept                               | Meaning                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Universal Grammar (UG)**            | Innate principles underlying human language                                     |
| **Language Acquisition Device (LAD)** | Hypothetical mechanism enabling children to acquire language                    |
| **Poverty of the stimulus**           | Children acquire complex grammar despite limited and imperfect linguistic input |
| **Principles and parameters**         | Universal principles combined with language-specific settings                   |

**NET distinction:**

- UG = innate linguistic principles.
- LAD = hypothetical acquisition mechanism.
- Poverty of the stimulus = argument supporting innate language capacity.

## **4. Critique of Skinner (1959)**

Chomsky's review of Skinner's _Verbal Behavior_ (1957) became a landmark in the cognitive revolution.

**Major criticisms:**

1. **Novel language production:** Children produce sentences they have never heard, which simple stimulus-response explanations cannot adequately explain.
2. **Lack of negative feedback:** Children acquire grammatical rules even though adults do not consistently correct their grammatical errors.
3. **Reinforcement criticism:** Chomsky argued that Skinner's explanation of reinforcement could become circular.
4. **Behaviourist limitations:** Behaviourism could not adequately explain the creative and rule-governed nature of language.

**NET point:** Chomsky's 1959 review challenged Skinner's behaviourist explanation of language.

## **5. Chomsky Hierarchy**

Chomsky classified formal grammars into four types.

| Type       | Grammar           | Language class         | Automaton                |
| ---------- | ----------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Type-0** | Unrestricted      | Recursively enumerable | Turing machine           |
| **Type-1** | Context-sensitive | Context-sensitive      | Linear-bounded automaton |
| **Type-2** | Context-free      | Context-free           | Pushdown automaton       |
| **Type-3** | Regular           | Regular                | Finite-state automaton   |

**NET point:** The hierarchy connects linguistics with formal language theory and computer science.

## **6. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Theorist             | Approach               | Key concept                                       |
| -------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Chomsky**          | Mentalist and nativist | Universal Grammar, LAD                            |
| **Skinner**          | Behaviourist           | Verbal behaviour, reinforcement                   |
| **Piaget**           | Constructivist         | Language develops alongside cognitive development |
| **George A. Miller** | Information-processing | Magical number 7 ± 2                              |
| **Noam Chomsky**     | Linguistic theory      | Transformational-generative grammar               |

**Important contrast:**

- Chomsky → Language acquisition involves innate linguistic structures.
- Piaget → Language development is related to broader cognitive development.
- Skinner → Language behaviour is explained through learning and reinforcement.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed **transformational-generative grammar**.
2. Proposed **Universal Grammar** and an innate language faculty.
3. Introduced the competence–performance distinction.
4. Criticised behaviourist explanations of language.
5. Contributed to formal language theory through the Chomsky hierarchy.
6. Helped stimulate the cognitive revolution in psychology and linguistics.

## **8. Criticisms**

- **Empirical testing:** Universal Grammar's abstract principles are difficult to test directly.
- **Alternative explanations:** Connectionist and usage-based approaches offer alternative accounts of language acquisition.
- **Social context:** His emphasis on syntax and linguistic competence gives comparatively less attention to social, pragmatic and communicative aspects of language.
- **Theoretical revisions:** Changes from Government and Binding theory to the Minimalist Program make the theory difficult to evaluate as one fixed formulation.
- **Criticism of Skinner:** Behaviourist responses argued that Chomsky's review did not fully address the empirical foundations of behaviour analysis.
