# **Allan Collins & M. Ross Quillian's Hierarchical Network Model — Quick Revision Notes**

**UGC-NET JRF Psychology | December 2026**

## **1. Introduction & Background**

- **Allan M. Collins** and **M. Ross Quillian** proposed the **Hierarchical Network Model of Semantic Memory**.
- Quillian (1965) initially developed a computer-based model of semantic knowledge.
- Collins and Quillian (1969) developed an experimental approach to test how semantic information is organised and retrieved.
- **Key publication:** _Retrieval Time from Semantic Memory_ (1969), published in the _Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior_.
- Their model explained how concepts, categories and properties are organised in a hierarchical network.

**NET association:** Collins and Quillian (1969) = Hierarchical Network Model of Semantic Memory.

## **2. Core Philosophy — Semantic Memory as a Hierarchical Network**

The central proposition is that semantic memory is organised as a network of interconnected concepts, with information arranged hierarchically.

### **Key concepts**

**1. Nodes**

- Represent concepts, objects or categories.
- Example: Canary, Bird and Animal.

**2. Links / Pointers**

- Connect different nodes.
- Represent relationships between concepts.
- Example: Canary belongs to the category Bird.

**3. Hierarchical Organisation**

- Concepts are organised from general categories to specific categories.
- Superordinate categories occupy higher levels.
- More specific concepts occupy lower levels.

**4. Cognitive Economy**

- Properties are stored at the highest applicable level of the hierarchy.
- The same information does not need to be repeated at every lower level.
- This reduces unnecessary duplication of information.

### **Example: Hierarchical Organisation**

**ANIMAL**

Superordinate category

Can breathe; has skin

**BIRD**

Intermediate category

Has wings; can fly (typical property)

**CANARY**

Specific category

Can sing; is yellow

**NET association:** Cognitive economy = Storing a property once at the highest level where it applies.

## **3. Collins and Quillian's Classic Experiment (1969)**

The researchers used the **sentence verification task** to investigate semantic memory.

### **Procedure**

- Participants were presented with sentences.
- They had to decide whether each sentence was true or false.
- Researchers recorded the participants' reaction times.
- The model predicted that reaction time would depend on the number of hierarchical links that needed to be traversed.

### **The Canary Example**

| Sentence               | Hierarchical distance | Predicted reaction time |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| A canary can sing.     | 0 links               | Fast                    |
| A canary is a bird.    | 1 link                | Intermediate            |
| A canary can fly.      | 1 link                | Intermediate            |
| A canary is an animal. | 2 links               | Slower                  |
| A canary has skin.     | 2 links               | Slower                  |

### **Major finding**

- Sentences requiring fewer hierarchical links were generally verified faster.
- Sentences requiring more links took longer.
- This supported the model's prediction that semantic retrieval involves searching through a hierarchical network.

### **Category Size / Levels Effect**

The **category size effect**, also called the levels effect, refers to the increase in reaction time as the number of hierarchical levels that must be traversed increases.

**NET trap:** The canary example is associated with Collins and Quillian (1969), not Collins and Loftus (1975).

## **4. Cognitive Economy — The Most Important Principle**

Cognitive economy is one of the most frequently tested concepts in this model.

### **Explanation**

Properties common to several members of a category are stored at the most general level where they apply.

For example:

- The property _has skin_ is stored at the Animal level.
- The property _has wings_ is stored at the Bird level.
- The property _can sing_ is stored at the Canary level.

This avoids storing the same property separately under every individual category.

**NET association:** Cognitive economy = Avoiding redundant storage of semantic properties.

## **5. Criticisms of the Hierarchical Network Model**

The model faced several empirical challenges.

### **1. Typicality Effect**

- Typical members of a category are recognised faster than atypical members.
- For example, people may verify "A robin is a bird" faster than "A chicken is a bird".
- However, the strict hierarchical model predicts similar reaction times if both members are at the same hierarchical level.
- This challenges the model's assumption that hierarchical distance alone determines retrieval time.

Most important criticism for NET

### **2. Familiarity Effect**

- Familiar statements are often verified faster than unfamiliar statements.
- Reaction time may depend on experience and familiarity, not just hierarchical distance.

### **3. Negative Sentence Problem**

- The original model does not adequately explain how people verify false statements.
- Negative judgments may depend on semantic similarity and the relationship between concepts.

### **4. Cognitive Economy Problem**

- Some frequently encountered properties may be stored or accessed at lower levels rather than exclusively at the highest applicable level.
- This challenges the assumption that properties are always stored according to strict cognitive economy.

**NET association:** Typicality effect = Major criticism of Collins and Quillian's model.

## **6. Revised Model — Collins & Loftus's Spreading Activation Model (1975)**

In response to the limitations of the strict hierarchical model, **Collins and Loftus (1975)** proposed the Spreading Activation Model.

### **Main features**

1. **Flexible network:** Concepts are connected through associations rather than a rigid hierarchy.
2. **Semantic distance:** The strength and closeness of associations influence retrieval.
3. **Spreading activation:** Activating one concept spreads activation to related concepts.
4. **Priming:** Activation of one concept can facilitate the processing of associated concepts.

### **Example**

When a person thinks of the word _doctor_, activation may spread to:

- Nurse
- Hospital
- Medicine
- Patient

Related concepts become easier to access.

### **Comparison: Hierarchical vs. Spreading Activation**

| Dimension      | Collins & Quillian (1969)       | Collins & Loftus (1975)                        |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Model          | Hierarchical Network            | Spreading Activation                           |
| Organisation   | Strict hierarchy                | Flexible associative network                   |
| Retrieval      | Traversal of hierarchical links | Spread of activation                           |
| Main principle | Cognitive economy               | Semantic distance                              |
| Typicality     | Difficult to explain            | Better accommodates typicality                 |
| Key limitation | Rigid hierarchical structure    | Difficult to specify and test all associations |

**NET trap:** Collins and Quillian = Hierarchical Network; Collins and Loftus = Spreading Activation.

## **7. Comparison with Other Semantic Memory Theories**

| Theorist                        | Theory / Concept           | Main contribution                               |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Collins & Quillian (1969)**   | Hierarchical Network Model | Hierarchical organisation and cognitive economy |
| **Collins & Loftus (1975)**     | Spreading Activation Model | Flexible associative network                    |
| **Eleanor Rosch**               | Prototype Theory           | Typicality and category representation          |
| **Smith, Shoben & Rips (1974)** | Feature Comparison Model   | Defining and characteristic features            |

### **Feature Comparison Model**

- Proposed by **Smith, Shoben and Rips (1974)**.
- Explains categorisation through comparison of features.
- Distinguishes between:
  - **Defining features:** Essential properties of a category.
  - **Characteristic features:** Typical but not essential properties.

**NET association:** Smith, Shoben and Rips (1974) = Feature Comparison Model.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Provided one of the earliest influential models of semantic memory organisation.
2. Introduced the idea of hierarchical storage of conceptual knowledge.
3. Explained semantic retrieval through network traversal.
4. Introduced cognitive economy as a principle of knowledge organisation.
5. Inspired later associative network models, including spreading activation.
6. Established sentence verification and reaction time as important experimental methods for studying semantic memory.
