# **Atkinson & Shiffrin's Multi-Store Model of Memory (Modal Model) — Quick Revision Notes**

## **1. Introduction & Background**

- **Richard C. Atkinson (born 1929)** and **Richard M. Shiffrin (born 1942):** American cognitive psychologists who proposed the **Multi-Store Model (MSM)** of memory in 1968.
- Also known as the **Modal Model of Memory**.
- **Key publication:** _Human Memory: A Proposed System and Its Control Processes_ (1968).
- The model explains memory as an information-processing system consisting of three structural stores and several control processes.

**NET association:** Atkinson and Shiffrin (1968) = Multi-Store Model = Sensory Register → STM → LTM.

## **2. Core Philosophy — Structural Features and Control Processes**

Atkinson and Shiffrin distinguished between two major aspects of memory.

### **A. Structural Features**

These are the relatively permanent components of the memory system:

- Sensory register
- Short-term store
- Long-term store

Each store differs in capacity, duration and function.

### **B. Control Processes**

These are flexible strategies used by individuals to regulate memory functioning.

Examples include:

- **Attention:** Selects information for entry into STM.
- **Rehearsal:** Maintains information in STM and can support transfer into LTM.
- **Coding:** Changes how information is represented.
- **Retrieval strategies:** Help recover stored information.

**NET trap:** Structural features are the stores themselves; rehearsal and retrieval strategies are control processes.

## **3. The Three Structural Stores**

### **1. Sensory Register (Sensory Memory)**

- Temporarily holds incoming sensory information in its original sensory form.
- Has a very large capacity but an extremely brief duration.
- Information that receives attention can enter STM.
- Different sensory modalities have different forms of sensory memory.

| Type              | Modality | Approximate duration |
| ----------------- | -------- | -------------------- |
| **Iconic memory** | Visual   | 0.5 second           |
| **Echoic memory** | Auditory | 2–4 seconds          |

**NET association:** Sensory memory → Brief retention of raw sensory information.

### **2. Short-Term Store (STS / STM)**

- Temporarily holds information currently being processed.
- Has limited capacity and duration.
- Information is maintained through rehearsal.
- Information can enter STM from sensory memory or be retrieved from LTM.

| Feature     | Traditional estimate                |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Capacity    | **7 ± 2 items**                     |
| Duration    | **15–30 seconds** without rehearsal |
| Encoding    | Often acoustic, but not exclusively |
| Maintenance | Rehearsal                           |

**Important:** The 7 ± 2 estimate is associated with **George A. Miller (1956)**, not originally established by Atkinson and Shiffrin.

### **3. Long-Term Store (LTS / LTM)**

- Stores knowledge, experiences, skills and information over extended periods.
- Has a potentially unlimited capacity.
- Information may remain available for years or even a lifetime.
- Stored information can be retrieved into STM for current processing.

| Feature   | Characteristic              |
| --------- | --------------------------- |
| Capacity  | Essentially unlimited       |
| Duration  | Relatively permanent        |
| Encoding  | Semantic encoding is common |
| Retrieval | Information returns to STM  |

**NET association:** LTM → Long-duration storage, large capacity and retrieval into STM.

## **4. Flow of Information in the Multi-Store Model**

The model proposes the following general sequence:

**Environmental Input**

Brief storage

**Sensory Register**

Attention

**Short-Term Store (STM)**

Rehearsal

**Long-Term Store (LTM)**

Retrieval: LTM → STM

### **Important mechanisms**

1. **Attention:** Transfers selected information from the sensory register to STM.
2. **Maintenance rehearsal:** Repeats information to keep it available in STM.
3. **Elaborative rehearsal:** Connects information with meaning and existing knowledge, supporting long-term retention.
4. **Retrieval:** Brings information from LTM back into STM.

**Important clarification:** The original model emphasised rehearsal as a control process but did not establish a simple rule that elaborative rehearsal automatically transfers information into LTM. That distinction became especially important in later research.

## **5. Criticisms of the Multi-Store Model**

### **A. Too Much Emphasis on Serial Processing**

- The model presents memory as a sequence of separate stores.
- Actual memory processing can involve interactions between STM and LTM.
- Some information may be processed without following a strictly serial pathway.

### **B. Unit of STM Is Too Simplistic**

- The model treats STM as a relatively unitary store.
- Baddeley and Hitch (1974) proposed that short-term processing involves multiple interacting components.

### **C. Rehearsal Does Not Guarantee Long-Term Retention**

- Repetition alone does not necessarily produce durable memory.
- Craik and Lockhart (1972) emphasised the importance of depth of processing.

### **D. Encoding Is More Flexible**

- The model's traditional emphasis on different encoding characteristics for different stores is overly restrictive.
- Research indicates that acoustic, visual and semantic encoding can occur across memory systems.

### **E. Limited Explanation of Working Memory**

\- The model does not adequately explain how information is actively manipulated during complex cognitive activities such as reasoning and comprehension.

## **6. Comparison with Other Memory Theorists**

| Dimension       | Atkinson & Shiffrin    | Baddeley & Hitch                                             | Craik & Lockhart                                           |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Theory          | Multi-Store Model      | Working Memory Model                                         | Levels of Processing                                       |
| Year            | 1968                   | 1974                                                         | 1972                                                       |
| Main emphasis   | Three memory stores    | Active short-term processing                                 | Depth of processing                                        |
| STM             | Unitary store          | Multiple components                                          | Not treated as a separate structural store in the same way |
| Retention       | Rehearsal and transfer | Active maintenance and manipulation                          | Deep processing improves retention                         |
| Key association | Sensory, STM, LTM      | Phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive | Shallow vs. deep processing                                |

**NET memory trick:**

- Atkinson and Shiffrin = Three stores.
- Baddeley and Hitch = Working memory components.
- Craik and Lockhart = Depth of processing.

## **7. Contributions**

1. **Information-processing approach:** Established an influential systematic framework for understanding memory.
2. **Three-store distinction:** Distinguished sensory memory, STM and LTM.
3. **Control processes:** Highlighted the active role of attention, rehearsal and retrieval strategies.
4. **Foundation for later research:** Stimulated the development of working memory and levels-of-processing approaches.
5. **Educational importance:** Continues to provide a foundational introduction to human memory.

## **9. Likely NET MCQ Traps**

- **Atkinson and Shiffrin (1968):** Multi-Store Model.
- **George Miller (1956):** Magical number 7 ± 2.
- **Ulric Neisser (1967):** Popularised the term _cognitive psychology_ and made major contributions to memory research.
- **Attention:** Sensory register → STM.
- **Rehearsal:** Maintains STM information and supports LTM transfer.
- **Iconic:** Visual sensory memory.
- **Echoic:** Auditory sensory memory.
- **Baddeley and Hitch:** Working Memory Model, not Multi-Store Model.
- **Craik and Lockhart:** Levels of Processing, not Multi-Store Model.
- **Structural features:** Relatively permanent memory stores.
- **Control processes:** Flexible strategies used to regulate memory.
- **Main criticism:** The model oversimplifies STM and gives excessive emphasis to serial processing and rehearsal.
