# **Peter Wason – Psychology of Reasoning**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology | Quick Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Peter Cathcart Wason (1924–2003):** British cognitive psychologist; major founder of modern psychology of reasoning.
- Influenced by **Karl Popper** (falsificationism) and **Jean Piaget**.
- Associated with **confirmation bias**, the **Wason Selection Task (1966)** and the **2-4-6 Problem (1960)**.
- Co-authored _Psychology of Reasoning: Structure and Content_ (1972) with **P. N. Johnson-Laird**.

## **2. Confirmation Bias**

**Confirmation bias:** The tendency to seek, select and interpret evidence that supports an existing hypothesis rather than evidence that could disprove it.

- People often test hypotheses using confirming examples.
- They neglect potentially falsifying evidence.
- Wason demonstrated that reasoning errors can be systematic rather than random.

## **3. Wason Selection Task (1966)**

Also called the Four-Card Problem.

**Rule:** If a card has a vowel on one side, it must have an even number on the other side.

Cards: **A, D, 4, 7**

Correct answer: A and 7

- **A (P):** Must check whether the other side is even.
- **7 (not-Q):** Must check whether the other side is a vowel; otherwise, the rule is violated.
- **D (not-P):** Cannot falsify the rule.
- **4 (Q):** Cannot falsify the rule, even if the other side is a consonant.

**Key findings:**

- Correct logical selection: **P and not-Q**.
- Common incorrect selection: **P and Q**.
- The task demonstrates difficulty in falsification and confirmation bias.
- Realistic or deontic contexts (e.g., age restrictions) often improve performance.

**NET trap:** The logically correct answer is not the two cards mentioned in the rule. It is the cards that could falsify it.

## **4. The 2-4-6 Problem (1960)**

- Participants are given the sequence **2-4-6** and asked to discover the hidden rule.
- They generate other three-number sequences and receive feedback.
- Actual rule: **Any ascending sequence of numbers**.

**Key finding:** Participants often test only examples that confirm their initial hypothesis, such as sequences of even numbers increasing by two.

**NET association:** 2-4-6 Problem → Confirmation bias in hypothesis testing.

## **5. THOG Problem**

- A reasoning task developed by Wason to study **combinational reasoning**.
- Uses four cards combining two colours (white/black) and two shapes (diamond/circle).
- Participants identify which cards satisfy a given logical condition.
- Demonstrates difficulties in combining and evaluating logical possibilities.

**NET association:** THOG → Combinational reasoning.
