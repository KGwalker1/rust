# **George A. Miller – Cognitive Psychology & Psycholinguistics**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **George Armitage Miller (1920–2012)**: American psychologist; one of the founders of cognitive psychology and a pioneer of **psycholinguistics**.
- PhD in Experimental Psychology, Harvard (1946); research focused on speech intelligibility in noise.
- Influenced by **Claude Shannon's information theory**.
- In **1960**, co-founded the **Center for Cognitive Studies at Harvard** with **Jerome Bruner**.
- **Major works:**
  - _Language and Communication_ (1951) – foundational work in psycholinguistics.
  - “The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two” (1956).
  - _Plans and the Structure of Behavior_ (1960), with Galanter and Pribram.
- Received the **National Medal of Science (1991)**.

## **2. Cognitive Revolution & Information Processing**

Miller helped move psychology beyond behaviourism by demonstrating that internal mental processes could be studied scientifically.

**Key propositions:**

1. **Information-processing approach:** The mind receives, processes, stores and retrieves information.
2. **Information theory:** Applied Shannon's concepts, including bits and channel capacity, to human cognition.
3. **Measurable mental processes:** Memory and information-processing limits can be quantified.
4. **Language:** Language makes internal psychological processes observable and communicable.

## **3. The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two (1956)**

Very important for NET

Miller's most famous contribution, published in _Psychological Review_ (1956).

**Main finding:** Human immediate memory span is approximately:

\\[ 7 \pm 2 \\]

That is, approximately **5–9 items**.

His paper discussed three types of capacity findings:

1. **Absolute judgment:** People could associate approximately 4–10 labels with continuous stimuli, such as loudness and line length.
2. **Immediate numerical estimation:** People could rapidly identify up to approximately six dots.
3. **Short-term memory span:** People could retain approximately 5–9 items.

**Chunking:**

- Grouping separate pieces of information into meaningful units called **chunks**.
- Helps overcome immediate-memory limitations through recoding and organisation.
- Example: Remembering 194719481950 as three meaningful year-chunks rather than ten individual digits.

**Important caveat:**

- Miller did not claim that seven is a universal, fixed law.
- He called the similarity across different tasks a **“pernicious, Pythagorean coincidence.”**
- Later research, including Cowan's work, suggested a smaller capacity estimate of approximately **4 ± 1 chunks**.

## **4. Psycholinguistics**

Miller was a pioneer of psycholinguistics, applying information theory and experimental methods to language.

- _Language and Communication_ (1951) – foundational text.
- **Speech perception:** Developed research using confusion matrices for spoken consonants.
- **Sentence meaning:** Research with Healy (1970) suggested that verbs play a major role in determining sentence meaning.
- **Chomsky collaboration:** Worked with Noam Chomsky on formal analysis of natural language for the _Handbook of Mathematical Psychology_ (1963).

**NET point:** Miller = psycholinguistics; Chomsky = transformational-generative grammar.

## **5. WordNet (1985/1986 onward)**

- Developed by Miller and colleagues at **Princeton University**.
- A large electronic **lexical database of English**, not a personality test or psychological inventory.

**Structure:**

- Organises nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs into synonym sets called **synsets**.
- Synsets represent lexicalised concepts.
- Semantic relations connect them.

| Relation | Meaning                    | Example       |
| -------- | -------------------------- | ------------- |
| Synonymy | Similar meaning            | Big – Large   |
| Hyponymy | Specific type of something | Canary – Bird |
| Meronymy | Part–whole relationship    | Wheel – Car   |

**Applications:** Natural Language Processing (NLP), search engines, translation and cognitive science.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Helped establish cognitive psychology and the information-processing approach.
2. Pioneered psycholinguistics through _Language and Communication_ (1951).
3. Proposed the **7 ± 2** immediate-memory capacity estimate.
4. Popularised **chunking** as a way to organise information.
5. Developed WordNet, an important lexical database.
6. Applied information theory to human cognition.

## **8. Criticisms and Limitations**

- **7 ± 2 is approximate:** Later research suggested lower memory capacity, such as 4 ± 1 chunks.
- **Mechanistic approach:** The information-processing metaphor may underemphasise emotion, embodiment and context.
- **WordNet limitation:** It is a lexical database, not a complete explanation of human language processing.
