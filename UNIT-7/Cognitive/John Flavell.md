# **John Flavell – Developmental Cognitive Theory**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology | Quick Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **John H. Flavell (1928–2025):** American developmental psychologist, widely recognised as a pioneer of metacognition research.
- Known for **metacognition, metamemory, appearance–reality distinction and visual perspective-taking**.
- _The Developmental Psychology of Jean Piaget_ (1963).
- **“What Is Memory Development the Development Of?” (1971)** – associated with metamemory.
- **“Metacognition and Cognitive Monitoring” (1979)** – foundational paper on metacognition.

## **2. Metacognition**

**Metacognition = Thinking about thinking.**

It refers to knowledge and regulation of one's own cognitive processes.

Two major aspects:

1. **Metacognitive awareness:** Knowing one's cognitive strengths, weaknesses and processes.
2. **Metacognitive regulation:** Monitoring and controlling one's thinking and learning.

Examples: Recognising that a topic is difficult, selecting a suitable study strategy and checking whether it has been understood.

## **3. Metamemory (1971)**

**Metamemory:** Knowledge about one's own memory processes and abilities.

- Understanding how memory works.
- Knowing which information is difficult to remember.
- Awareness of effective memory strategies.
- Monitoring one's own remembering and forgetting.

**NET association:** Flavell (1971) → Metamemory.

## **4. Flavell's Model of Metacognition (1979)**

Flavell proposed four interacting components:

| Component                     | Meaning                                             |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Metacognitive knowledge**   | Knowledge about person, task and strategy variables |
| **Metacognitive experiences** | Conscious experiences during cognitive activities   |
| **Goals/tasks**               | Objectives of cognitive activity                    |
| **Actions/strategies**        | Methods used to achieve cognitive goals             |

### **Three types of metacognitive knowledge**

- **Person variables:** Knowledge about one's own and others' cognitive abilities.
- **Task variables:** Knowledge about task difficulty and demands.
- **Strategy variables:** Knowledge of strategies suitable for different tasks.

Important NET point

Remember: **Person – Task – Strategy**.

## **5. Appearance–Reality Distinction (1983)**

Associated with **Flavell, Flavell and Green**.

- Children are shown objects that appear to be one thing but are actually something else.
- Example: A sponge painted to look like a rock.
- They are asked what the object looks like and what it really is.

**Finding:**

- Many 3-year-olds struggle to distinguish appearance from reality.
- Most 5-year-olds succeed.

**Significance:** Understanding that an object's appearance and actual identity can differ; important for representational understanding and Theory of Mind.

## **6. Visual Perspective Taking**

Flavell distinguished two levels:

| Level       | Approximate age | Understanding                                  |
| ----------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Level 1** | 2–3 years       | Others may not see what I can see              |
| **Level 2** | 4–5 years       | Two people can see the same object differently |

Example:

- Level 1: Understanding that another person cannot see an object hidden from their view.
- Level 2: Understanding that the same object may look different from another person's viewpoint.

**NET association:** Level 1 = access to seeing; Level 2 = different visual perspectives.
