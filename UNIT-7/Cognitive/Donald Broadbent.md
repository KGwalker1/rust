# **Donald Broadbent – Information-Processing Theory & Filter Model of Attention**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Donald Eric Broadbent (1926–1993):** British psychologist; pioneer of cognitive psychology and selective attention research.
- Studied under **Frederic Bartlett** at Cambridge.
- Served in the RAF (1944–47), where he observed attentional difficulties in handling multiple radio signals.
- Joined the **Medical Research Council's Applied Psychology Unit (APU)** in 1949; Director from 1958–1974.
- Major works:
  - _Perception and Communication_ (1958) – foundational text of modern cognitive psychology.
  - _Decision and Stress_ (1971) – later revision incorporating signal detection theory.

**NET keyword:** Broadbent = Information-processing approach + Filter model of attention.

## **2. Information-Processing Approach**

Broadbent viewed the human mind as a **limited-capacity communication channel** that processes information through sequential stages.

Key ideas:

1. **Information-processing:** Inspired by Shannon's communication theory; the mind receives, processes and transmits information.
2. **Limited capacity:** The brain cannot consciously process all incoming information simultaneously.
3. **Bottleneck:** Selective attention manages the flow of information into limited-capacity processing.
4. **Box-and-arrow models:** Represent cognitive processes as sequential stages and information flows.

**NET connection:** Broadbent applied communication-engineering concepts to human cognition.

## **3. Filter Model of Attention (1958)**

Also called the **Bottleneck Theory** or **Early Selection Model**.

### **Three stages**

**1. Sensory Store (S-store)**

Briefly holds all incoming sensory information.

**2. Selective Filter**

Selects information using physical features such as pitch, location, intensity and ear of input.

**3. Limited-Capacity Channel**

Selected information receives conscious processing; unattended information is blocked.

**Defining feature:** The filter selects information based on **physical characteristics, not meaning**.

### **Empirical Evidence**

**A. Dichotic listening – Cherry's shadowing task**

- Participants hear different messages in each ear.
- They repeat the attended message while ignoring the other.
- They can detect physical changes in the unattended message, such as a change in pitch.
- They generally fail to report semantic changes, such as a language change.

**B. Split-span experiment – Broadbent**

- Digits are presented simultaneously to both ears.
- Participants recall digits ear-by-ear rather than in their original presentation order.
- Broadbent interpreted this as evidence for separate channels and selective processing.

**NET trap:** Cherry developed the shadowing procedure; Broadbent developed the filter model and used dichotic-listening evidence.

## **4. Early vs. Late Selection Debate**

Broadbent's model was challenged by findings that unattended information can sometimes be processed for meaning.

| Researcher                   | Contribution                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Broadbent (1958)**         | Early selection; physical-feature filter                                  |
| **Moray (1959)**             | Demonstrated recognition of personally significant unattended information |
| **Gray & Wedderburn (1960)** | Demonstrated meaning-based recall across ears                             |
| **Treisman (1960)**          | Proposed attenuation theory                                               |
| **Deutsch & Deutsch (1963)** | Late selection; semantic analysis before response selection               |

### **Treisman's Filter-Attenuation Theory**

- The filter does not completely block unattended information.
- Instead, it **reduces or attenuates** its strength.
- Important or meaningful information can still reach awareness.
- Thus, unattended information may receive some semantic processing.

**Key distinction:**

- Broadbent = all-or-none early filter.
- Treisman = attenuation of unattended information.
- Deutsch & Deutsch = late selection after semantic analysis.

## **5. Later Work – _Decision and Stress_ (1971)**

- Broadbent revised his earlier account and acknowledged a more flexible role for meaning in selection.
- Incorporated **Signal Detection Theory**, associated with Tanner and Swets.
- Examined vigilance, decision-making, noise and stress.
- Investigated individual differences in responses to stress.

**NET keyword:** _Decision and Stress_ (1971) → Signal Detection Theory.

## **6. Applied Contributions**

Broadbent was a pioneer of applied cognitive psychology.

- **Noise and performance:** Studied how noise affects attention and vigilance.
- **Human factors:** Applied attention research to radar operation, air-traffic control and gunnery systems.
- **Cognitive Failures Questionnaire (CFQ):** Associated with Broadbent and colleagues; measures everyday cognitive failures such as absent-mindedness and attentional lapses.

## **7. Comparison with Other Attention Theories**

| Feature          | Broadbent         | Treisman           | Deutsch & Deutsch        |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Model            | Filter theory     | Attenuation theory | Late-selection theory    |
| Selection        | Early             | Intermediate       | Late                     |
| Unattended input | Blocked           | Weakened           | Semantically analysed    |
| Main principle   | Physical features | Attenuation        | Meaning before selection |

**Remember:** Broadbent's filter is based on physical characteristics; Treisman's attenuation allows important unattended information to receive further processing.

## **8. Major Contributions**

1. Established the information-processing approach in cognitive psychology.
2. Developed the early-selection filter model.
3. Introduced bottleneck and limited-capacity explanations of attention.
4. Encouraged experimental investigation of selective attention.
5. Applied cognitive research to real-world human-performance problems.

## **9. Criticisms**

1. **Rigid selection:** The all-or-none filter cannot adequately explain semantic processing of unattended information.
2. **Meaning ignored:** Research by Gray and Wedderburn challenged the claim that selection depends only on physical features.
3. **Attenuation alternative:** Treisman offered a more flexible explanation.
4. **Limited role of memory:** The original model gave insufficient importance to long-term memory and prior knowledge.
