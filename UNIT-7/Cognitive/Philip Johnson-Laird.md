# **Philip Johnson-Laird – Mental Model Theory of Reasoning**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology | Quick Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Philip Johnson-Laird (born 1936):** British cognitive psychologist and major contributor to the psychology of reasoning.
- Proposed the **Mental Model Theory of Reasoning**.
- Key works:
  - _Language and Perception_ (1976), with George Miller.
  - _Mental Models_ (1983).
  - _Deduction_ (1991), with Ruth Byrne.
- Collaborated with **Peter Wason** on _Psychology of Reasoning: Structure and Content_ (1972).

## **2. Mental Model Theory**

**Central idea:** People reason by constructing and manipulating mental representations of possible situations rather than simply applying formal logical rules.

Three major principles:

1. **Possibility representation:** A mental model represents a possible state of affairs consistent with the premises.
2. **Iconicity:** The structure of a mental model corresponds to the structure of the situation it represents.
3. **Principle of truth:** People primarily represent what is true and tend to neglect what is false.

**NET association:** Mental Model Theory → Reasoning through possibilities.

## **3. Three Stages of Reasoning**

| Stage                         | Explanation                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Model construction**     | Construct a mental model based on the premises.                                |
| **2. Conclusion formulation** | Draw a possible conclusion from the model.                                     |
| **3. Validation**             | Search for alternative models or counterexamples that disprove the conclusion. |

**Key prediction:** The more mental models required, the more difficult the reasoning task.

Important NET point

A conclusion is valid when **no counterexample can be found** in which the premises are true but the conclusion is false.

## **4. Principle of Truth and Illusory Inferences**

- People tend to represent true possibilities while neglecting false possibilities.
- This reduces cognitive effort but can produce systematic reasoning errors.
- **Illusory inferences:** Conclusions that appear logically valid but are actually invalid.

**NET association:** Illusory inferences → Principle of truth.

## **5. Mental Model Theory vs Mental Logic Theory**

| Mental Model Theory                                        | Mental Logic Theory                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Associated with Johnson-Laird                              | Associated with formal-rule approaches      |
| Uses mental representations of possibilities               | Uses formal rules of inference              |
| Errors arise partly from limited models and working memory | Errors arise from difficulty applying rules |
| Content and meaning influence reasoning                    | Emphasises logical rules                    |

**Important:** Johnson-Laird's theory challenges the idea that human reasoning depends primarily on an innate system of formal logical rules.

## **6. Comparison with Other Reasoning Theorists**

| Theorist               | Major contribution                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Johnson-Laird**      | Mental Model Theory                              |
| **Peter Wason**        | Confirmation bias, Selection Task, 2-4-6 Problem |
| **Kahneman & Tversky** | Heuristics, biases, Prospect Theory              |
| **Herbert Simon**      | Bounded rationality and satisficing              |
