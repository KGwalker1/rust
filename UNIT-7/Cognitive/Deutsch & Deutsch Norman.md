# **Deutsch & Deutsch / Norman's Late Selection Theory of Attention**

UGC-NET JRF Revision Notes

High-yield topic

## **1. Introduction**

- **Deutsch and Deutsch (1963)** proposed the **Late Selection Theory of Attention**.
- They argued that all incoming stimuli, including unattended information, undergo **complete semantic processing** before attention selects relevant information.
- **Donald Norman (1968)** later modified and extended the theory by introducing the concept of **Pertinence**.

**Remember:**

- Deutsch & Deutsch = Originators of Late Selection Theory.
- Norman = Pertinence and refinement of the theory.

## **2. Main Assumptions of Late Selection Theory**

1. All incoming sensory information, whether attended or unattended, receives complete perceptual and semantic analysis.
2. Selection does not occur at the initial perceptual stage.
3. Selection occurs at the **response selection stage**, after meaning has been processed.
4. Important and relevant information receives priority for further processing and conscious response.
5. Unattended information can influence behaviour if it is sufficiently important.

**Example:** During a conversation at a party, you may suddenly hear someone mentioning your name, even when you are concentrating on another conversation. This is called the **Cocktail Party Effect**.

## **3. Deutsch & Deutsch's Original Model (1963)**

**Key concepts:**

- **Complete semantic processing:** Both attended and unattended stimuli are analysed for meaning.
- **Importance:** Important information receives priority for further processing and response.
- **Displacement:** When more important information appears, it can displace previously selected information.

**NET focus:** Deutsch & Deutsch proposed that selection occurs after semantic analysis, not before it.

## **4. Norman's Reformulation (1968)**

Donald Norman extended the late selection model through the concept of **Pertinence**.

### **A. Pertinence**

Pertinence refers to the **momentary psychological relevance or importance of information** based on an individual's current goals, expectations and context.

Example: A student studying in a crowded room may ignore ordinary conversations but immediately notice someone mentioning an upcoming examination.

### **B. Two Types of Activation**

Norman discussed two forms of activation:

1. **Temporary excitation:** Temporary activation of information currently being processed.
2. **Permanent excitation:** Relatively lasting activation of information stored in long-term memory.

### **C. Retrieval Mechanism**

Norman also discussed how information is retrieved from memory and how the relevance of retrieved information guides further processing.

**NET focus:** Pertinence is the most important term associated with Norman (1968).

## **5. Comparison of Attention Theories**

| Feature                | Broadbent                        | Treisman     | Deutsch & Deutsch | Norman                  |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| Year                   | 1958                             | 1960         | 1963              | 1968                    |
| Theory                 | Filter                           | Attenuation  | Late selection    | Modified late selection |
| Selection              | Early                            | Intermediate | Late              | Late                    |

Important sequence for NET

**Broadbent → Treisman → Deutsch & Deutsch → Norman**

- Broadbent: Early selection.
- Treisman: Attenuation.
- Deutsch & Deutsch: Late selection.
- Norman: Pertinence-based late selection.

## **6. Criticisms of Late Selection Theory**

1. **Limited processing capacity:** Processing every stimulus completely may overload the cognitive system.
2. **Mixed research evidence:** Not all studies demonstrate complete semantic processing of unattended information.
3. **Complexity:** Norman's model is difficult to test in its entirety.
4. **Individual differences and task demands:** The extent of unattended processing varies with the situation.

### **Modern Development: Lavie's Load Theory (1995)**

- Proposed by **Nilli Lavie**.
- Explains that the stage of attentional selection depends on perceptual load.

| Condition            | Selection       |
| -------------------- | --------------- |
| High perceptual load | Early selection |
| Low perceptual load  | Late selection  |

**Remember:** High load leaves fewer resources for processing irrelevant information, whereas low load allows more processing of unattended stimuli.
