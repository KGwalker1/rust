# **Allen Newell & Herbert A. Simon – Information Processing Theory**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Allen Newell (1927–1992)** and **Herbert A. Simon (1916–2001)**: Pioneers of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the information-processing approach to human cognition.
- Associated with **RAND Corporation** and **Carnegie Mellon University**.
- Their work contributed to the development of both cognitive psychology and AI.
- **Major works:**
  - _Logic Theorist_ (1955–56) – first AI program.
  - _General Problem Solver (GPS)_ (1957/1959, with J. C. Shaw).
  - _Human Problem Solving_ (1972) – information-processing model of human reasoning.
- **Awards:**
  - ACM A. M. Turing Award (1975), jointly.
  - Herbert Simon received the **Nobel Prize in Economics (1978)** for bounded rationality.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Information-Processing Approach**

Their central proposition was that human cognition can be understood as a **symbolic information-processing system**.

**Key propositions:**

1. **Mind as an information-processing system:** The mind receives, processes, stores and retrieves information.
2. **Computer simulation:** Computer programs can simulate human problem-solving and provide testable models of cognition.
3. **Heuristics:** Humans frequently use shortcuts and rules of thumb rather than exhaustively examining every possible solution.
4. **Symbol manipulation:** Human reasoning can be modelled as the manipulation of symbols according to rules.

## **3. Physical Symbol System Hypothesis (PSSH)**

Very important for NET

Proposed by **Newell and Simon**.

**Definition:** A physical symbol system possesses the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action.

- **Necessary:** Symbol manipulation is required for intelligence.
- **Sufficient:** A physical symbol system can produce intelligent behaviour.
- Both human thinking and computer operations can be understood through symbol manipulation.

**Examples:**

| System        | Symbols                    | Processes            |
| ------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Formal logic  | Logical symbols            | Rules of deduction   |
| Chess         | Pieces and board positions | Legal moves          |
| Human thought | Mental representations     | Cognitive operations |

**NET trap:** Remember both words – **necessary and sufficient**.

## **4. Problem-Solving Framework**

### **A. Problem Space**

A problem is represented through different states and possible operations.

| Component               | Meaning                                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Initial state**       | Starting condition                         |
| **Goal state**          | Desired solution                           |
| **Intermediate states** | Conditions between initial and goal states |
| **Operators**           | Permissible actions or moves               |

**Problem space** = Initial state + Goal state + Intermediate states + Operators.

### **B. Means-End Analysis**

- A problem-solving strategy that reduces the difference between the **initial state and goal state**.
- Involves identifying differences and creating **subgoals**.
- The General Problem Solver used this strategy.
- Subgoals help break complex problems into manageable steps.

### **C. Think-Aloud Protocols**

- Developed by Newell and Simon to study human problem-solving.
- Participants verbalise their thoughts as they solve a problem.
- Helps researchers identify the information people attend to and the strategies they use.
- Focuses on thoughts occurring during the task rather than retrospective explanations.

## **5. Major Programs and Contributions**

| Program / Concept                         | Main contribution                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Logic Theorist (1955–56)**              | First AI program; proved logical theorems       |
| **General Problem Solver (1957/1959)**    | Used heuristics and means-end analysis          |
| **Information Processing Language (IPL)** | Introduced important list-processing techniques |
| **Soar (1990)**                           | Newell's unified cognitive architecture         |

**Logic Theorist:**

- Developed by Newell, Simon and J. C. Shaw.
- Presented at the Dartmouth Conference (1956), associated with the birth of AI.

**General Problem Solver (GPS):**

- Attempted to imitate human problem-solving.
- Used general heuristics rather than exhaustive algorithms.
- Limitation: Could solve only relatively simple problems because it lacked specialised knowledge.

**IPL:**

- Developed by Newell, Simon and Shaw.
- Pioneered list processing.
- Linked-list structures were originally called **NSS memory**, after their inventors.

## **6. Bounded Rationality and Satisficing – Herbert Simon**

### **A. Bounded Rationality**

- Simon rejected the assumption that humans always make perfectly rational, optimal decisions.
- Human decision-making is limited by:
  - Limited cognitive capacity.
  - Incomplete information.
  - Limited time and attention.
  - Complexity of the environment.
- **Procedural rationality:** Evaluating the procedures used to make decisions within these limitations.

### **B. Satisficing**

- A decision-making strategy of accepting an option that is **good enough**, rather than searching endlessly for the optimal option.
- The decision-maker establishes an **aspiration level**.
- Search stops when an available option meets the required level.

| Optimising                                               | Satisficing                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Searches for the best possible option                    | Searches for an acceptable option |
| Assumes extensive information and computational capacity | Recognises human limitations      |
| Associated with classical rational-choice models         | Associated with Herbert Simon     |

**NET distinction:** Bounded rationality = limitations on decision-making; satisficing = strategy used under those limitations.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Developed the information-processing approach to human cognition.
2. Created **Logic Theorist**, an early AI program.
3. Developed the **General Problem Solver**.
4. Proposed the Physical Symbol System Hypothesis.
5. Developed problem-space and means-end analysis frameworks.
6. Popularised think-aloud protocols in problem-solving research.
7. Simon introduced bounded rationality and satisficing.
8. Contributed to list processing through IPL.

## **9. Criticisms**

- **GPS limitations:** Too general; unable to handle complex problems requiring specialised knowledge.
- **PSSH criticism:** Connectionist and embodied approaches challenge the idea that symbol manipulation is necessary for all intelligence.
- **Think-aloud limitations:** Verbal reports may not capture unconscious or automatic cognitive processes.
- **Mechanistic approach:** Computer-based models may underrepresent emotion, creativity and social cognition.
- **Limited scope:** Their framework focused heavily on reasoning and problem-solving.
