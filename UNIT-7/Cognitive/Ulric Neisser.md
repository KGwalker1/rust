# **Ulric Neisser – Cognitive & Ecological Theory**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Ulric Neisser (1928–2012)**: German-born American psychologist, known as the **Father of Cognitive Psychology**.
- Studied at Harvard; academic advisor **George Miller**. Influenced by information theory and Oliver Selfridge.
- PhD in Experimental Psychology, Harvard (1956).
- **Major works:**
  - _Cognitive Psychology_ (1967) – named and unified the field; landmark of the cognitive revolution.
  - _Cognition and Reality_ (1976) – shift towards the ecological approach.
  - “Five Kinds of Self-Knowledge” (1988).
  - _Intelligence: Knowns and Unknowns_ (1996) – APA Task Force report.

## **2. Cognitive Revolution**

Neisser helped establish the scientific study of internal mental processes, challenging behaviourism.

**Key propositions:**

1. Cognition is scientifically measurable.
2. Mind functions as an **information-processing system** – transforms, reduces, elaborates, stores and retrieves information.
3. Cognition is **active and constructive**, not passive.
4. Memory involves reconstruction, influenced by **Frederic Bartlett**.

## **3. Ecological Turn (1976)**

Neisser became critical of artificial laboratory-based information-processing research and moved towards **James J. Gibson's ecological approach**.

- **Ecological validity:** Study cognition in natural, real-world contexts.
- **Watergate study:** Examined memory through testimony concerning the Watergate scandal.
- **Inattentional blindness:** Developed an experimental paradigm demonstrating failure to notice unexpected stimuli when attention is focused elsewhere.

**NET point:** Neisser's later approach emphasised real-world cognition rather than artificial laboratory tasks.

## **4. Five Kinds of Self-Knowledge (1988)**

Neisser proposed five forms of self-knowledge:

| Self                   | Basis                           | Meaning                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ecological self**    | Direct perception               | Self as an active agent in the immediate physical environment                |
| **Interpersonal self** | Direct perception               | Self as socially engaged through emotional and communicative signals         |
| **Extended self**      | Memory and anticipation         | Self across past, present and future                                         |
| **Private self**       | Conscious experience            | Awareness that one's conscious experiences are personally one's own          |
| **Conceptual self**    | Social assumptions and theories | Self-concept shaped by socially based beliefs about oneself and human nature |

**Important distinctions:**

- Ecological and interpersonal selves are directly perceived and present in preverbal infancy.
- Extended self = memory of the past and anticipation of the future.
- Conceptual self = socially shaped self-concept.
- The five selves are usually integrated, not experienced separately.
- They differ in developmental histories and accuracy of self-knowledge.

## **5. APA Intelligence Report (1996)**

- Neisser chaired the APA Task Force that produced \*\* _Intelligence: Knowns and Unknowns_ (1996)\*\*.
- It examined IQ heritability, group differences and environmental influences.
- Prepared in response to controversy surrounding _The Bell Curve_ (1994), by Herrnstein and Murray.
- Criticism: Some argued that the report avoided important social and political controversies, including issues involving socioeconomic status and race.

## **6. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Theorist          | Main approach          | Key concept                                              |
| ----------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Neisser**       | Cognitive → Ecological | Information processing, five selves, ecological validity |
| **Gibson**        | Ecological             | Direct perception, affordances                           |
| **Bartlett**      | Constructive memory    | Schema, reconstructive memory                            |
| **Skinner**       | Radical behaviourism   | Reinforcement, observable behaviour                      |
| **George Miller** | Information processing | Working memory, “magical number seven”                   |

## **7. Contributions**

1. Established cognitive psychology through _Cognitive Psychology_ (1967).
2. Legitimated scientific investigation of internal mental processes.
3. Proposed five kinds of self-knowledge.
4. Promoted ecological validity in cognitive research.
5. Developed an experimental paradigm for inattentional blindness.
6. Chaired the APA intelligence report (1996).

## **8. Criticisms**

- **Self-criticism:** Neisser later considered information-processing research too artificial and insufficiently connected to real-world cognition.
- **Five selves:** Their boundaries are theoretical and they are rarely experienced independently.
- **Intelligence report:** Criticised for allegedly avoiding major social and political controversies.
- **Limited personality theory:** His work is primarily cognitive rather than a comprehensive theory of personality.
