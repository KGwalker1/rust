## **1. Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)**

The **Hypothalamus** plays a major role in regulating autonomic responses associated with emotion.

| Function    | Sympathetic (SNS)          | Parasympathetic (PNS)                     |
| ----------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Heart       | Increases heart rate       | Decreases heart rate                      |
| Respiration | Bronchodilation            | Bronchoconstriction                       |
| Skin        | Increases sweating and GSR | Little direct control of eccrine sweating |
| Pupils      | Mydriasis (dilation)       | Miosis (constriction)                     |
| Digestion   | Inhibits digestion         | Stimulates digestion                      |
| Salivation  | Reduces watery salivation  | Stimulates salivation                     |
| Metabolism  | Glycogenolysis             | Energy storage                            |

**Important terms:**

- Mydriasis = Pupil dilation.
- Miosis = Pupil constriction.
- Tachycardia = Increased heart rate.
- Bradycardia = Decreased heart rate.
- GSR/EDA = Increased skin conductance due to sweating.

**ANS specificity debate:**

- **Walter Cannon:** Emotions share a broadly similar sympathetic arousal pattern.
- **Ekman, Levenson & Friesen (1983):** Different emotions can produce distinct autonomic patterns.

## **2. Endocrine and Neurochemical Correlates**

**A. Sympathetic-Adrenomedullary (SAM) Axis**

Rapid response

Hypothalamus → Sympathetic nervous system → Adrenal medulla → Epinephrine and norepinephrine.

- Epinephrine = Adrenaline.
- Norepinephrine = Noradrenaline.
- Prepares the body for fight-or-flight responses.

**Funkenstein's Hypothesis (1955):**

- Anger-out → Higher norepinephrine.
- Anger-in, fear and anxiety → Higher epinephrine.

**B. Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis**

Sustained response

Hypothalamus (CRH) → Anterior pituitary (ACTH) → Adrenal cortex (Cortisol).

- Cortisol mobilises energy.
- Helps maintain prolonged stress responses.
- Suppresses certain immune and inflammatory activities.

**C. Important Neurotransmitters**

| Neurotransmitter | Emotional Function                                          |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Serotonin (5-HT) | Mood regulation and impulse control                         |
| Dopamine         | Reward, motivation and positive reinforcement               |
| GABA             | Inhibitory neurotransmission; reduces neuronal excitability |
| Norepinephrine   | Alertness, arousal and vigilance                            |

Sequence:

Hippocampus → Fornix → Mammillary bodies → Anterior thalamus → Cingulate gyrus → Parahippocampal region → Hippocampus.

**NET point:** The amygdala was not part of the original Papez circuit.

**B. Limbic System – Paul MacLean (1952)**

- Expanded the classical limbic framework.
- Included structures such as the amygdala and septal area.
- Associated with the Triune Brain model:
  1. Reptilian complex
  2. Paleomammalian brain (limbic system)
  3. Neomammalian brain (neocortex)

**C. Amygdala – Fear and Emotional Memory**

- Important in threat detection, fear conditioning and emotional memory.
- **Klüver-Bucy Syndrome (1939):** Bilateral temporal lobe lesions in monkeys.

Symptoms:

- Psychic blindness (visual agnosia).
- Placidity and reduced fear.
- Hyperorality.
- Hypersexuality.

**D. LeDoux – Dual-Pathway Model of Fear**

| Low Road                 | High Road                            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Thalamus → Amygdala      | Thalamus → Sensory cortex → Amygdala |
| Fast and crude           | Slower and more detailed             |
| Rapid defensive response | Detailed stimulus evaluation         |
| Less cortical processing | Greater cortical processing          |

**E. Hypothalamus and Emotional Expression**

- **Walter Hess:** Electrical stimulation of the hypothalamus produced emotional and autonomic responses.
- **Philip Bard (1928):** Proposed the hypothalamic theory of emotion.
- **Sham Rage:** Decorticate animals displayed uncoordinated rage responses when the posterior hypothalamus remained intact.

**F. Prefrontal Cortex – Richard Davidson**

| Hemisphere              | Association                               |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Left prefrontal cortex  | Approach motivation and positive affect   |
| Right prefrontal cortex | Withdrawal motivation and negative affect |

**G. Damasio – Somatic Marker Hypothesis**

- Bodily signals influence decision-making.
- Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) integrates emotional information into decisions.
- Demonstrated through the **Iowa Gambling Task**.
- Associated with the case of Phineas Gage.

**H. Insula**

- Interoceptive awareness (perception of internal bodily states).
- Strongly associated with disgust processing.

## **4. Psychophysiological Measurement**

| Instrument     | Measures                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| GSR / EDA      | Skin conductance and sweating              |
| ECG / EKG      | Heart rate and cardiac electrical activity |
| EMG            | Muscle electrical activity                 |
| EEG            | Brain electrical activity                  |
| Pneumograph    | Respiration rate and depth                 |
| Plethysmograph | Changes in blood volume                    |
| Pupillometry   | Pupil diameter                             |

**Facial EMG:**

- Zygomaticus major → Smiling / positive affect.
- Corrugator supercilii → Frowning / negative affect.

**HRV (Heart Rate Variability):**

- Variation in time between successive heartbeats.
- Higher resting HRV is often associated with better autonomic flexibility and emotion regulation.

## **5. Polygraph – Lie Detector**

- Records physiological changes associated with autonomic arousal.
- Does not directly detect lies.

Common measurements:

1. Skin conductance (GSR).
2. Blood pressure and pulse.
3. Respiration.

**Important methods:**

- Control Question Test (CQT).
- Guilty Knowledge Test (GKT), also called Concealed Information Test (CIT).
- **David Lykken:** Associated with the Guilty Knowledge Test.

## **7. Most Important NET Facts**

1. **SNS:** Fight-or-flight; increases heart rate and sweating.
2. **PNS:** Rest-and-digest; promotes digestion and reduces heart rate.
3. **SAM axis:** Adrenal medulla releases catecholamines.
4. **HPA axis:** Adrenal cortex releases cortisol.
5. **Papez (1937):** Classical emotional circuit.
6. **MacLean (1952):** Limbic system and Triune Brain.
7. **LeDoux:** Low road and high road of fear.
8. **Bard:** Sham rage.
9. **Klüver-Bucy:** Placidity, hyperorality and hypersexuality.
10. **Damasio:** Somatic Marker Hypothesis.
11. **GSR:** Measures sympathetic-related electrodermal activity.
12. **Polygraph:** Measures physiological arousal, not lies directly.

**MCQ caution:** The Papez circuit and MacLean's limbic system are not identical. Also, autonomic responses are not perfectly unique to each emotion; physiological patterns can overlap.
