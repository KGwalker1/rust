Kohlberg's theory of moral development is a stage theory proposing that moral reasoning develops through **three levels and six stages**, with each stage representing a qualitatively different way of thinking about right and wrong. The theory was developed by **Lawrence Kohlberg (1927–1987)**, an American developmental psychologist who built on Piaget's work.

### The Three Levels and Six Stages

Kohlberg assessed moral reasoning using **moral dilemmas**—most famously the **Heinz dilemma** (a man must decide whether to steal an overpriced drug to save his dying wife). What mattered was not the answer (yes/no) but the **reasoning** behind it.

| Level                 | Stage       | Orientation                      | Key Reasoning                                                             |
| :-------------------- | :---------- | :------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pre-conventional**  | **Stage 1** | Punishment & Obedience           | Avoid punishment; obey authority                                          |
|                       | **Stage 2** | Individualism & Exchange         | Satisfy one's own needs; "what's in it for me?"                           |
| **Conventional**      | **Stage 3** | Good Interpersonal Relationships | Gain approval; be a "good boy/good girl"; live up to others' expectations |
|                       | **Stage 4** | Maintaining Social Order         | Fulfill one's duty; uphold laws and social order                          |
| **Post-conventional** | **Stage 5** | Social Contract                  | Laws are social contracts that can be changed for the greater good        |
|                       | **Stage 6** | Universal Ethical Principles     | Abstract, universal principles of justice and human dignity override laws |

### Key Features for NET Preparation

- **Invariant Sequence**: Stages unfold in a fixed order; individuals do not skip stages, though progress may stop at any point.
- **Few Reach the Highest Stages**: Less than one-fourth of adults reach Stage 5, and very few reach Stage 6.
- **Focus on Reasoning, Not Behavior**: The theory assesses _how_ one justifies a moral choice, not _what_ the choice is.
- **Mnemonic**: "Pre-Coaching Post-exam" + **PI → GM → SU** (Pre: Punishment/Individualism; Conventional: Good/Maintaining; Post: Social contract/Universal).

### Criticisms (High-Yield for NET)

- **Gender Bias**: Carol Gilligan argued the theory was based on male samples and undervalued an "ethic of care" (associated with females) in favor of an "ethic of justice".
- **Cultural Bias**: The emphasis on individual rights and universal principles reflects Western individualistic values; collectivist cultures may prioritize social harmony and duty differently.
- **Reasoning ≠ Action**: The "judgment-action gap"—knowing the "right" reasoning does not guarantee moral behavior.
