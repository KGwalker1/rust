## 2. Freedom and the three escape mechanisms

Fromm argued that modern freedom can leave people feeling isolated, powerless, and anxious. Freedom from traditional restrictions does not automatically provide freedom to live creatively and spontaneously.

| Concept      | Meaning                                             | NET clue         |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Freedom from | Liberation from external restrictions               | Negative freedom |
| Freedom to   | Capacity for spontaneous, creative, loving activity | Positive freedom |

Pasted text.txt

### Three ways people escape freedom

### 1. Authoritarianism

Escaping powerlessness through submission to authority (masochism) or domination of others (sadism). Both may coexist in the same person.

### 2. Destructiveness

Destroying people or things that make one feel powerless or unable to control the world.

### 3. Automaton conformity

Adopting society’s opinions, tastes, and personality, losing one's authentic identity and developing a pseudo-self.

Pasted text.txt

MCQ traps:

- Authoritarianism includes both submission and domination.
- Automaton conformity is presented as the most common escape in modern democratic consumer society.
- The healthy alternative is positive freedom through spontaneous, productive activity.

## 3. The five existential needs

Fromm proposed five needs arising from the human condition, rather than simply from physiological drives.

| Need                              | Core meaning                                                       | Productive satisfaction                |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Relatedness                       | Overcoming separateness                                            | Love and brotherhood                   |
| Transcendence                     | Rising above passive existence                                     | Creativity and productive work         |
| Rootedness                        | Feeling at home in the world                                       | Solidarity and love                    |
| Sense of identity                 | Knowing “I am I”                                                   | Individuality and spontaneous activity |
| Frame of orientation and devotion | Understanding the world and finding something to devote oneself to | Rational goals and productive values   |

Pasted text.txt

Memory aid: R–T–R–I–F — Relatedness, Transcendence, Rootedness, Identity, Frame of orientation.

NET trap: Do not substitute Maslow’s needs, such as safety, for Fromm’s five existential needs.

## 4. Character orientations — high-yield topic

Fromm described four nonproductive orientations and one productive orientation.

| Orientation  | Core attitude                                          | Remember it as          |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| Receptive    | Good things come from outside; wait to receive         | “Give it to me.”        |
| Exploitative | Good things come from outside but must be taken        | “I’ll take it.”         |
| Hoarding     | Security comes from keeping and saving                 | “I’ll keep it.”         |
| Marketing    | Self-worth depends on marketability and exchange value | “I must sell myself.”   |
| Productive   | Uses human powers creatively and spontaneously         | “I’ll create and grow.” |

Pasted text.txt

The marketing orientation is especially associated with modern consumer capitalism. The productive orientation is the healthy ideal, not a rigid category that a person must belong to exclusively.

### Biophilia versus necrophilia

- Biophilia: love of life, growth, and living things.
- Necrophilia: in Fromm’s characterological sense, attraction to death, destruction, decay, and mechanical control; it does not simply mean sexual attraction to death.

## 5. Social character

Social character is the set of character traits shared by members of a society that makes them want to do what they must do to meet that society’s economic and social demands.

For example, a capitalist economy may encourage punctuality, discipline, acquisition, and competition.

Fromm used this concept to connect:

Economic structure → Social character → Ideology and behaviour

In The Sane Society (1955), he questioned whether an entire society could be sick. His idea of the pathology of normalcy suggests that behaviour considered normal by society may itself be psychologically unhealthy.&#x20;

## 6. Productive love and work

In The Art of Loving (1956), Fromm treated love as an art requiring knowledge, effort, and practice.

| Element of mature love | Meaning                                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Care                   | Concern for the other person's growth       |
| Responsibility         | Readiness to respond to the other's needs   |
| Respect                | Allowing the other to develop independently |
| Knowledge              | Understanding the other deeply              |

Pasted text.txt

Remember: Care – Responsibility – Respect – Knowledge (CRRK).

Fromm also viewed productive work as creative self-expression. Work becomes alienated when people function merely as tools of production rather than expressing their human capacities.

## 8. Dreams, psychopathology and therapy

- Dreams: In The Forgotten Language (1951), Fromm emphasized universal symbols expressing shared human experiences. His approach differed from Freud’s emphasis on dreams as wish fulfilment and from Jung’s emphasis on archetypes.
- Neurosis: Can arise from unsuccessful attempts to satisfy existential needs in nonproductive ways.
- Therapy: Aims to restore productive love, creative work, spontaneity, and positive freedom. Fromm emphasized the patient's social context and the therapist as a real person rather than merely a blank screen.

## 10. Quick NET revision

- Freud + Marx = Fromm's humanistic psychoanalysis.
- Three escape mechanisms = authoritarianism, destructiveness, automaton conformity.
- Five existential needs = relatedness, transcendence, rootedness, identity, frame of orientation and devotion.
- Four nonproductive orientations = receptive, exploitative, hoarding, marketing.
- Healthy orientation = productive.
- Life-loving character = biophilia.
- Death- and destruction-oriented character = necrophilia.
- Four elements of mature love = care, responsibility, respect, knowledge.
- Social character = shared traits that help society's economic system function.
- The Sane Society = pathology of normalcy.
- The Forgotten Language = dreams and universal symbols.
- To Have or to Be? = having versus being.
