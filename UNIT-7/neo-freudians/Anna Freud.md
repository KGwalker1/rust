# Anna Freud: Ego Psychology & Child Psychoanalysis

UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Quick Revision Notes

## 1. Introduction

- Anna Freud (1895–1982): Youngest daughter of Sigmund Freud; founder of child psychoanalysis and a major figure in ego psychology.
- Major work: The Ego and the Mechanisms of Defence (1936).
- Child analysis book: Introduction to the Technique of Child Analysis (1927).
- Other works: War and Children (1943), Infants Without Families (1943), Normality and Pathology in Childhood (1965).
- Institution: Hampstead Child Therapy Course and Clinic (1952), later associated with the Anna Freud Centre.
- Collaborator: Dorothy Burlingham.
- Training analyst: Sigmund Freud, her father.

## 2. Ego Psychology

- Main focus: The ego and its functions, rather than only the id and instinctual drives.
- Ego functions: Perception, memory, thinking, judgment, reality testing, movement and emotional regulation.
- Two-front battle: The ego defends against internal instinctual demands (id) and external reality.
- Ego assessment: Ego functions can be observed directly, especially in children.
- Influence: Heinz Hartmann, Erik Erikson, David Rapaport and other ego psychologists.

## 3. Defense Mechanisms

Defense mechanisms are largely unconscious ways of protecting the ego from anxiety and conflict.

| Mechanism                         | Simple meaning                                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Repression                        | Pushing distressing thoughts out of awareness.                                  |
| Regression                        | Returning to an earlier pattern of behaviour.                                   |
| Reaction formation                | Expressing the opposite of an unacceptable impulse.                             |
| Projection                        | Attributing one's own feelings to others.                                       |
| Introjection                      | Internalizing another person's qualities or values.                             |
| Isolation                         | Separating an idea from its emotion.                                            |
| Undoing                           | Trying to cancel an unacceptable thought or act.                                |
| Turning against the self          | Directing hostility towards oneself.                                            |
| Reversal                          | Changing an impulse into its opposite direction or role.                        |
| Sublimation                       | Channeling unacceptable impulses into socially acceptable activities.           |
| Identification with the aggressor | Adopting the characteristics of a threatening person.                           |
| Altruistic surrender              | Giving up one's own ambitions and living through another person's achievements. |
| Denial in fantasy                 | Escaping an unpleasant reality through imagination.                             |
| Denial in word and act            | Rejecting reality through words or behaviour.                                   |
| Restriction of the ego            | Limiting activities to avoid anxiety-provoking situations.                      |

NET points:

- Anna Freud systematically organized and expanded the study of defense mechanisms.
- Identification with the aggressor and altruistic surrender are especially associated with her.
- Repression is classically considered a fundamental defense.
- Sublimation is generally considered a mature defense.
- Defenses vary in their developmental appropriateness; avoid assuming every defense fits a rigid age category.

## 4. Child Psychoanalysis

- Established: A systematic approach to analyzing children, especially through her 1927 work.
- Techniques: Play, drawings, stories, fantasy and observation of behaviour.
- Difference from adult analysis: Children do not use free association in the same way as adults.
- Analyst's role: Observer, therapist and sometimes educator.
- Treatment goal: Reduce symptoms and restore healthy developmental progress.
- Parents' role: Parent guidance is important because children depend on their caregivers.
- Transference: The child's feelings towards the analyst must be understood alongside ongoing relationships with parents.

## 5. Wartime Nurseries

- Anna Freud and Dorothy Burlingham studied children separated from their families during World War II.
- Their books include War and Children and Infants Without Families (1943).
- Key findings: Separation can cause distress, and stable, consistent caregiving is important for development.
- Their observations contributed to later discussions of maternal deprivation and attachment, associated particularly with John Bowlby and James Robertson.

## 6. Developmental Lines (1965)

Anna Freud described five developmental lines in Normality and Pathology in Childhood.

1. Dependency to emotional self-reliance: Moving from dependence on caregivers towards independence.
2. Suckling to rational eating: Developing mature eating habits.
3. Wetting and soiling to bladder and bowel control: Achieving toilet control.
4. Irresponsibility to responsibility in body management: Learning to care for one's body.
5. Egocentricity to companionship and work: Moving towards cooperation, relationships and productive activity.

Main principle: Normal development involves progress across different lines. Uneven development, fixation or regression may indicate difficulties.

## 7. Diagnostic Profile

- Developed at the Hampstead Clinic.
- Systematically assessed a child's development.
- Included ego functions, drives, superego, object relations and developmental lines.
- Helped identify strengths, delays and developmental difficulties.

## 8. Anna Freud–Melanie Klein Controversy

| Anna Freud                                                | Melanie Klein                                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Emphasized ego functions and external reality.            | Emphasized unconscious fantasy and internal object relations.                  |
| Followed a more traditional Freudian view of development. | Proposed that important Oedipal processes begin in infancy.                    |
| Used observation, developmental assessment and play.      | Treated play as a form of free association.                                    |
| Focused strongly on ego defenses.                         | Emphasized primitive defenses such as splitting and projective identification. |

- Controversial Discussions: British Psychoanalytical Society, 1943–1944.
- Splitting and projective identification: Particularly associated with Melanie Klein.

## 9. Beyond the Best Interests of the Child (1973)

- Written with Joseph Goldstein and Albert Solnit.
- Emphasized continuity of care and the child's psychological relationships in custody decisions.
- Associated with the concept of the psychological parent.

## 10. Major Contributions

1. Developed ego psychology and expanded the study of defenses.
2. Established child psychoanalysis as a distinct clinical approach.
3. Studied separation and caregiving through wartime nurseries.
4. Proposed five developmental lines.
5. Developed systematic child assessment at the Hampstead Clinic.
6. Influenced child psychotherapy and custody discussions.

## 11. Criticisms

- Remained close to classical Freudian theory.
- Defense mechanisms describe coping processes but do not always explain their causes.
- Traditional psychoanalytic treatments may be lengthy and have less empirical support for some conditions than evidence-based alternatives.
- Later research showed that the effects of separation depend on the quality of substitute care and other circumstances.
