# UGC-NET JRF Psychology: Karen Horney's Theory of Personality

Short, exam-focused memory notes based on your source, with key concepts, comparison tables, and common MCQ traps.

## 1. Introduction and background

| Feature              | Key fact                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Theorist             | Karen Danielsen Horney (1885–1952)                                                                                                                                 |
| School               | Psychoanalytic Social Theory; Neo-Freudian                                                                                                                         |
| Main emphasis        | Culture, social relationships, childhood experiences                                                                                                               |
| Emigrated to the USA | 1932                                                                                                                                                               |
| Founded              | American Institute for Psychoanalysis, 1941                                                                                                                        |
| Major works          | The Neurotic Personality of Our Time (1937); New Ways in Psychoanalysis (1939); Self-Analysis (1942); Our Inner Conflicts (1945); Neurosis and Human Growth (1950) |

Core idea: Personality and neurosis are shaped substantially by social and cultural conditions, rather than biological instincts alone.

## 2. Horney vs Freud

| Freud                                                          | Horney                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emphasized biological instincts and sexuality                  | Emphasized social and cultural influences                     |
| Neurosis linked to infantile sexuality and the Oedipus complex | Neurosis linked to disturbed early relationships              |
| Penis envy viewed through biological development               | Penis envy reinterpreted as envy of male status and privilege |
| Emphasized instinctual conflict                                | Emphasized basic anxiety and interpersonal conflict           |

## 3. Basic evil, basic hostility and basic anxiety

This is one of Horney's most important concepts.

Basic Evil

Parental rejection, indifference, humiliation, unfairness

Basic Hostility

Repressed anger toward parents

Basic Anxiety

Feeling isolated and helpless in a potentially hostile world

| Concept         | Meaning                                                |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Basic evil      | Parental behaviors that undermine the child's security |
| Basic hostility | Resentment toward parents                              |
| Basic anxiety   | Feeling lonely, helpless and unsafe in a hostile world |

Memory chain: Basic evil → Basic hostility → repression → Basic anxiety.

NET point: Basic anxiety is the foundation of neurosis in Horney's theory.

## 4. The ten neurotic needs

Horney described ten neurotic needs in Our Inner Conflicts (1945).

| No. | Neurotic need                           | Key idea                                 |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Affection and approval                  | Need to please everyone                  |
| 2   | A partner who will take over one's life | Dependence on a powerful partner         |
| 3   | Restricting life within narrow borders  | Avoiding demands and attention           |
| 4   | Power                                   | Controlling others                       |
| 5   | Exploiting others                       | Taking advantage of people               |
| 6   | Social recognition and prestige         | Seeking status and admiration            |
| 7   | Personal admiration                     | Seeking admiration for an idealized self |
| 8   | Ambition and personal achievement       | Need to outperform others                |
| 9   | Self-sufficiency and independence       | Avoiding dependence on others            |
| 10  | Perfection and unassailability          | Avoiding mistakes and criticism          |

Healthy vs neurotic needs

- Healthy: Flexible, appropriate and consciously managed.
- Neurotic: Compulsive, rigid, excessive and driven by anxiety.

## 6. Horney's theory of the self

From Neurosis and Human Growth (1950).

| Concept                | Meaning                                                                     |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Real self              | Genuine potential for healthy growth                                        |
| Idealized self         | Unrealistic, glorified image of what one believes one should be             |
| Despised self          | Devalued image of oneself, associated with shame and self-hatred            |
| Tyranny of the shoulds | Rigid internal demands to be perfect, powerful, loved or successful         |
| Externalization        | Experiencing one's own inner conflicts as if they originate outside oneself |
| Self-hatred            | Hostility toward oneself when reality fails to match the idealized self     |

Key principle: A greater discrepancy between the real self and idealized self is associated with more severe neurosis.

### The neurotic process

- Healthy growth: Realization of the real self.
- Neurotic growth: Pursuit of an idealized self.
- Search for glory: Perfectionism, neurotic ambition and vindictive triumph.

## 7. Feminine psychology

| Concept                | Horney's position                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Critique of penis envy | Envy may concern male social status and privilege rather than anatomy                |
| Womb envy              | Men may envy women's capacity to bear and nurture children                           |
| Flight from womanhood  | Cultural insecurity and gender-role expectations may contribute to women's conflicts |

NET one-marker: Womb envy — Karen Horney.

Horney challenged Freud's account of female development and emphasized the effects of cultural devaluation of women.

## 8. Therapy

| Goal or method                      | Key point                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main goal                           | Reduce basic anxiety and revive the real self                                    |
| Self-analysis                       | Individuals can examine their own conflicts with guidance                        |
| Free association and dream analysis | Retained from psychoanalysis                                                     |
| Present focus                       | Emphasizes current relationships and conflicts while exploring childhood origins |
| Therapist's role                    | Genuine human relationship and mutual respect                                    |
| Desired outcome                     | Wholeheartedness in feeling, thinking, wanting and deciding                      |

## 9. Comparison with Freud, Adler and Rogers

| Theorist | Key concept                                          | Main explanation of difficulties                    |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Freud    | Oedipus complex, libido                              | Instinctual conflict and repression                 |
| Adler    | Inferiority, striving for superiority                | Inferiority feelings and lack of social interest    |
| Horney   | Basic anxiety, neurotic trends                       | Disturbed early relationships and social conditions |
| Rogers   | Real self, ideal self, unconditional positive regard | Incongruence between self-concept and experience    |

Important connection: Horney's real-self/idealized-self discrepancy resembles Rogers' concept of incongruence.

## 10. Contributions and criticisms

| Contributions                                             | Criticisms                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Developed Psychoanalytic Social Theory                    | Concepts are difficult to operationalize                                      |
| Emphasized culture and relationships                      | May underemphasize biological factors                                         |
| Introduced basic anxiety and three neurotic trends        | Theory relies heavily on clinical observation                                 |
| Challenged Freud's theory of female psychology            | No comprehensive developmental-stage model                                    |
| Developed the real, idealized and despised self framework | Neurotic trends describe coping patterns more than they explain their origins |
