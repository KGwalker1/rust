# **Socially Acquired Motives – nAch, nAff and nPow**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology | Unit 7 & Unit 9 | Quick Revision Notes

## **1. Theoretical Foundations**

- **Henry Murray (1938):** Identified psychogenic needs in his theory of Personology. Introduced the concepts of needs and environmental press. Co-developed the **TAT with Christiana Morgan**.
- **David C. McClelland (1961):** Developed the Acquired Needs Theory, focusing on achievement, affiliation and power.
- These motives are learned through socialisation and cultural experiences. Individuals possess all three in varying degrees.

**NET distinction – Implicit vs Explicit motives (McClelland, Koestner & Weinberger, 1989):**

| Implicit motives                                  | Explicit motives                            |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Measured through projective methods such as TAT   | Measured through self-report questionnaires |
| Often operate outside conscious awareness         | Conscious, self-attributed motives          |
| Predict spontaneous, long-term behavioural trends | Predict deliberate, immediate choices       |

## **2. Three Major Social Motives**

### **A. Need for Achievement (nAch)**

**Definition:** Desire to accomplish challenging tasks and achieve standards of excellence.

Characteristics of high-nAch individuals:

- Prefer moderately difficult tasks and calculated risks.
- Take personal responsibility for outcomes.
- Seek immediate, specific performance feedback.
- Prefer challenging but achievable goals.
- Value accomplishment over external praise.

**Important theorists:**

- **McClelland:** Achievement motivation and Achievement Motivation Training (AMT).
- **John Atkinson:** Expectancy-Value Model of achievement motivation.
- **Matina Horner (1968):** Fear of Success (FOS) – anticipated negative social consequences of success may inhibit achievement behaviour.

### **B. Need for Affiliation (nAff)**

**Definition:** Desire to establish and maintain friendly, harmonious interpersonal relationships.

Characteristics:

- Prefer cooperation and social acceptance.
- Seek approval and belongingness.
- Avoid interpersonal conflict.
- May hesitate to make unpopular decisions.

**Important theorists:**

- **Stanley Schachter (1959):** Demonstrated that anxiety can increase the desire to affiliate with others facing the same stressful situation.
- **Leon Festinger (1954):** Social Comparison Theory helps explain why people seek others in uncertain situations.
- **Dan McAdams:** Distinguished need for intimacy from need for affiliation.

NET association

Schachter → Anxiety and affiliation → Electric shock experiment.

### **C. Need for Power (nPow)**

**Definition:** Desire to influence, control or have an impact on other people and the environment.

Characteristics:

- Desire for leadership and influence.
- Interest in status, prestige and authority.
- Enjoy persuasive and competitive situations.

**Two forms of power (McClelland):**

| Personalised power           | Socialised power                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Power for personal dominance | Power for collective goals       |
| Self-centred influence       | Organisational or social welfare |
| Dominance over others        | Empowering and guiding others    |

**Effective leadership:** McClelland and Burnham associated effective managerial leadership with:

- High need for power.
- Low need for affiliation.
- High activity inhibition (self-control).
- Socialised rather than personalised use of power.

## **3. Atkinson's Expectancy-Value Model (1957)**

John W. Atkinson formalised achievement motivation mathematically.

### **A. Tendency to Approach Success**

\\[ T_s=M_s\times P_s\times I_s \\]

- \\(M_s\\) = Motive to achieve success.
- \\(P_s\\) = Subjective probability of success.
- \\(I_s\\) = Incentive value of success.

\\[ I_s=1-P_s \\]

### **B. Tendency to Avoid Failure**

\\[ T\_{af}=M\_{af}\times P_f\times I_f \\]

- \\(M\_{af}\\) = Motive to avoid failure.
- \\(P_f\\) = Probability of failure.
- \\(I_f\\) = Negative incentive value of failure.

In the classic model:

\\[ P_f=1-P_s,\qquad I_f=-P_s \\]

### **C. Resultant Achievement Motivation**

\\[ T_a=T_s-T\_{af} \\]

\\[ T_a=(M_s-M\_{af})P_s(1-P_s) \\]

**Key findings:**

- Success-oriented individuals prefer moderately difficult tasks.
- The tendency to approach success is greatest when \\(P_s=0.50\\).
- Failure-avoidant individuals may choose very easy or very difficult tasks to protect themselves from the psychological consequences of failure.

Important NET formula

Remember: **Moderate difficulty = maximum tendency to approach success**, under the classic model.

## **4. Measurement Tools**

| Tool                            | Developer / Association      | Purpose                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **TAT**                         | Murray and Christiana Morgan | Projective assessment of motives              |
| **Achievement imagery scoring** | McClelland and colleagues    | Assessment of implicit achievement motivation |
| **EPPS**                        | Allen L. Edwards             | Self-report assessment of 15 needs            |
| **PRF**                         | Douglas N. Jackson           | Objective personality assessment              |

**Important distinction:** TAT measures implicit motives through imaginative storytelling, whereas EPPS and PRF use self-report formats.
