# **David C. McClelland – Theory of Needs and Achievement Motivation**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology | Quick Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **David Clarence McClelland (1917–1998):** American psychologist known for research on **achievement motivation** and human needs.
- Associated with Harvard University and the study of motivation, personality and organisational behaviour.
- Major works:
  - _The Achievement Motive_ (1953), with John W. Atkinson, Russell A. Clark and Edgar L. Lowell.
  - _The Achieving Society_ (1961).
  - _Power: The Inner Experience_ (1975).

**NET association:** McClelland → Achievement Motivation + Three Needs Theory.

## **2. Three Needs Theory**

McClelland proposed three important learned psychological needs that influence human behaviour.

| Need                            | Meaning                                | Behaviour                                  |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Need for Achievement (nAch)** | Desire to accomplish challenging goals | Seeks personal responsibility and feedback |
| **Need for Affiliation (nAff)** | Desire for friendly relationships      | Prefers cooperation and social acceptance  |
| **Need for Power (nPow)**       | Desire to influence or control others  | Seeks influence, authority and impact      |

**Important:** These needs are learned through experience and socialisation, rather than being fixed biological instincts.

## **3. Need for Achievement (nAch)**

This is McClelland's most famous contribution.

High nAch individuals generally:

- Prefer moderately challenging tasks.
- Take personal responsibility for outcomes.
- Seek feedback about performance.
- Prefer tasks where success depends on their own effort.
- Focus on accomplishing realistic but challenging goals.

**NET trap:** High-achievement individuals generally prefer moderate risk, not extremely easy or extremely difficult tasks.

## **4. Need for Affiliation (nAff)**

- Desire for friendly interpersonal relationships.
- Need for acceptance and belongingness.
- Preference for cooperation and harmonious relationships.
- May avoid conflict or situations that threaten social approval.

## **5. Need for Power (nPow)**

- Desire to influence, control or have an impact on others.
- Interest in authority, leadership and status.

Two forms:

| Type                   | Meaning                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Personalised power** | Power used for personal dominance and self-interest        |
| **Socialised power**   | Power used for collective goals and organisational welfare |

**NET point:** Socialised power is generally associated with more constructive leadership.

## **6. Measurement of Achievement Motivation**

McClelland and colleagues used the **Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)** to assess implicit achievement motivation.

- Participants create stories based on ambiguous pictures.
- Stories are scored for motivational imagery, including achievement-related themes.
- This approach is associated with **projective assessment**.

**Important:** McClelland's research on achievement motivation was strongly influenced by Henry Murray's need theory and TAT.

## **7. Comparison with Henry Murray**

| Henry Murray                                     | David McClelland                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Developed Personology and a broad need framework | Focused on learned motivational needs                      |
| Proposed numerous psychogenic needs              | Emphasised achievement, affiliation and power              |
| Introduced concepts of alpha and beta press      | Studied achievement motivation and its social consequences |
| Co-developed TAT with Christiana Morgan          | Used TAT-based methods to assess motivation                |
