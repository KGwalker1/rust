# **Medard Boss – Daseinsanalysis (Heideggerian Existential Analysis)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Medard Boss (1903–1990):** Swiss psychiatrist and psychotherapist; foremost exponent of **pure Heideggerian Daseinsanalysis**.
- Received his MD from the University of Zurich in 1928.
- Trained under **Eugen Bleuler** at Burghölzli.
- Underwent psychoanalytic training and was analysed by Freud and Karen Horney.
- Collaborated with **Carl Jung** for over a decade.
- Later developed a close intellectual relationship with **Martin Heidegger**.

### **Collaboration with Heidegger**

- Boss read Heidegger's _Being and Time_ during World War II.
- Their collaboration led to philosophical seminars at Boss's home in **Zollikon, Switzerland**.
- These became known as the **Zollikon Seminars**.

**Important works:**

- _Psychoanalysis and Daseinsanalysis_ (1957/1963).
- _The Analysis of Dreams_ (1958).
- _Existential Foundations of Medicine and Psychology_ (1979).

**Institutional contributions:**

- Founded the Daseinsanalytic Institute in Zürich.
- Established the International Federation for Daseinsanalysis.

**NET identity:** Boss = Pure Heideggerian Daseinsanalysis + Dream Analysis + Zollikon Seminars.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Pure Heideggerian Daseinsanalysis**

Boss argued that psychotherapy should be grounded in Heidegger's existential philosophy rather than Freudian metapsychology.

### **Key propositions**

1. **Rejection of Freudian metapsychology:** Accepted Freud's clinical investigative practice but rejected the theoretical model of id, ego, superego and psychic drives.
2. **Human as Dasein:** Humans are not isolated subjects confronting external objects; they are fundamentally **being-in-the-world**.
3. **Psychosomatic unity:** Mind and body cannot be treated as separate entities. Human existence is indivisible.
4. **Freedom and openness:** Psychological disturbance occurs when individuals lose openness to their possibilities of existing.

**NET focus:** Boss retained aspects of psychoanalytic clinical practice but rejected Freudian metapsychology.

## **3. Dream Analysis – Boss's Signature Contribution**

Dream analysis is one of Boss's most distinctive contributions.

### **Core principles**

- Dreams are **direct manifestations of being-in-the-world**.
- Dreams are not disguised wish-fulfilments or concealed unconscious desires, as in Freud's interpretation.
- Dream content reflects the individual's waking existence.
- Dream analysis helps make explicit aspects of existence that the individual cannot clearly recognize while awake.

### **Analysis of 823 Dreams**

- Boss analysed **823 dreams of a single patient**.
- He concluded that changes in dream content parallel changes in the patient's waking experiences.

### **Example: Teeth-Loss Dreams**

| Freud                                                                                              | Boss                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Interpreted teeth-loss dreams through unconscious sexual symbolism, including masturbation anxiety | Interpreted them as giving up old ways of grasping or relating to the world |

- Teeth represent ways of catching hold of, grasping and comprehending the world.
- Such dreams may occur during changes in an individual's worldview.

**NET focus:** Boss = Dreams as direct disclosures of being-in-the-world, not disguised wish-fulfilment.

## **4. Psychopathology as Restricted Existence**

Boss viewed psychological suffering as a **restriction of the possibilities of existing**.

- Mental disturbance is not simply a malfunctioning psychological mechanism.
- It represents a narrowing of an individual's openness to the world.
- The person becomes unable to realize certain possibilities of existence.

### **Conversion Hysteria**

- Not merely a bodily expression of hidden psychic mechanisms.
- Understood as a way of realizing existential possibilities without openly assuming them.

### **Goal of Therapy**

- Restore the individual's openness and freedom.
- Expand restricted possibilities of existence.
- Understand the patient's particular way of being-in-the-world.
- Therapy is a phenomenological encounter rather than simply the removal of symptoms.

**NET focus:** Psychological suffering = Restriction of possibilities of existing.

## **5. Boss vs. Binswanger**

| Feature                     | Ludwig Binswanger                       | Medard Boss                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Relationship with Heidegger | More independent                        | **Direct, sustained collaboration** |
| Philosophical approach      | Heideggerian with Husserlian influences | **Strictly Heideggerian**           |
| Psychoanalysis              | Retained some psychoanalytic elements   | Rejected Freudian metapsychology    |
| Signature concept           | World-design (_Weltentwurf_)            | Pure Heideggerian Daseinsanalysis   |
| Dream analysis              | Less central                            | **Major clinical contribution**     |
| Key association             | Umwelt, Mitwelt, Eigenwelt              | Zollikon Seminars                   |
| Institutional contribution  | Bellevue Sanatorium                     | Daseinsanalytic Institute, Zürich   |

**Important distinction:**

- Binswanger → Originator of the systematic existential application to psychiatry; world-design.
- Boss → Purest Heideggerian exponent; dream analysis and direct collaboration with Heidegger.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed the most strictly Heideggerian form of Daseinsanalysis.
2. Established a phenomenological approach to dream interpretation.
3. Collaborated with Heidegger through the **Zollikon Seminars**.
4. Rejected the separation of mind and body in understanding human existence.
5. Founded the Daseinsanalytic Institute in Zürich and the International Federation for Daseinsanalysis.
6. Reinterpreted psychological suffering as restricted possibilities of existence.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. **Limited empirical basis:** Strongly grounded in philosophy rather than experimental research.
2. **Operationalization difficulties:** Heideggerian concepts are difficult to measure scientifically.
3. **Uncritical appropriation:** Some scholars argue that Boss's clinical interpretation of Heidegger does not always fully correspond with Heidegger's original philosophy.
4. **Limited generalizability:** Individualized phenomenological analysis is difficult to standardize.
5. **Limited textbook recognition:** Binswanger is often more prominent in conventional personality theory textbooks.
