# **Rollo May – Existential Theory of Personality**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Rollo Reese May (1909–1994):** American psychologist and psychoanalyst; a leading figure in existential psychology and humanistic psychology (Third Force).
- Known as a founding figure of **American existential psychology**.
- Influences:
  - **Kierkegaard:** Existential philosophy.
  - **Paul Tillich:** Existential theology and friendship.
  - **Alfred Adler:** Studied briefly with him.
  - **Harry Stack Sullivan and Erich Fromm:** Psychoanalytic influences.
- His experience with tuberculosis and confrontation with death influenced his existential outlook.
- Received Columbia University's first PhD in clinical psychology (1949).

**Important works:**

- _The Meaning of Anxiety_ (1950)
- _Man's Search for Himself_ (1953)
- _Existence_ (1958, edited)
- _Love and Will_ (1969)
- _The Courage to Create_ (1975)
- _The Discovery of Being_ (1983)

**Key identity:** May = Authentic Being + Ontological Anxiety + Ontological Guilt + Daimonic.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Authentic Being**

- May viewed the central tendency of personality as the achievement of **authentic being**.
- Humans are both:
  - **Subjects:** Active individuals capable of choosing and influencing their lives.
  - **Objects:** Influenced by biology, society, environment and destiny.
- This creates the **human dilemma**: humans are determined by circumstances but retain the capacity to choose their response.

### **Important Concepts**

| Concept                     | Meaning                                                    |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Being-in-the-world (Dasein) | Unity of the individual and environment                    |
| Umwelt                      | Biological or physical world                               |
| Mitwelt                     | Social world and relationships                             |
| Eigenwelt                   | Subjective or psychological world                          |
| Intentionality              | Structure that gives meaning to experience                 |
| Freedom                     | Capacity to pause between stimulus and response and choose |
| Destiny                     | Given conditions, limitations and circumstances of life    |

**NET focus:** Remember the three modes of being-in-the-world: **Umwelt, Mitwelt and Eigenwelt**.

## **3. Ontological Anxiety and Ontological Guilt**

May's most distinctive concepts concern anxiety and guilt as fundamental aspects of human existence.

### **A. Ontological Anxiety**

- Fear of **non-being** or the possibility of losing one's existence.
- Arises when individuals confront uncertainty, freedom and the unknown future.
- Associated with choosing growth, risk and authenticity.
- May distinguished:

| Normal anxiety                   | Neurotic anxiety                  |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Proportionate to the threat      | Disproportionate to the threat    |
| Can be confronted constructively | Often repressed or avoided        |
| Can encourage growth             | Leads to avoidance and conformity |

### **B. Ontological Guilt**

- Guilt arising from failing to realize one's potentialities.
- Associated with missed opportunities and an unlived life.
- Occurs when individuals choose the safe status quo instead of pursuing possibilities.

### **Core Relationship**

| Choice                             | Existential consequence |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Choosing the uncertain future      | Ontological anxiety     |
| Remaining with the safe status quo | Ontological guilt       |

**Authenticity:** Requires courage to confront anxiety, accept responsibility and pursue one's possibilities.

## **4. The Daimonic**

- **Daimonic:** Any natural human function that has the power to take over the whole person.
- Examples: sex, anger and power.
- These impulses are neither inherently good nor evil.
- When integrated into personality, they can be creative and constructive.
- When they dominate without integration, they can become destructive.
- May associated violence with feelings of impotence and apathy.

**NET focus:** The daimonic should be integrated, not simply suppressed.

## **5. Four Developmental Phases**

May proposed four characteristic phases of development. They are **not rigid or fixed age-based stages**.

| Phase            | Main characteristics                                                                   |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Innocence** | Pre-egoic, pre-self-conscious; basic needs and a primitive form of will                |
| **2. Rebellion** | Development of ego and self-consciousness; seeks freedom but lacks full responsibility |
| **3. Ordinary**  | Adult conformity, conventional values and acceptance of responsibility                 |
| **4. Creative**  | Authentic adult; accepts destiny, confronts anxiety and integrates the daimonic        |

### **Important Points**

- Innocence: associated with infancy.
- Rebellion: commonly associated with childhood and adolescence.
- Ordinary: conventional adult life.
- Creative: authentic, courageous and existential living.
- These phases may overlap and recur throughout life.

**NET sequence:** Innocence → Rebellion → Ordinary → Creative.

## **6. Comparison with Other Humanistic / Existential Theorists**

| Feature         | Rollo May                          | Maslow                                  | Rogers                        | Viktor Frankl                           |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Approach        | Existential                        | Humanistic                              | Humanistic                    | Existential                             |
| Core concept    | Authentic being, anxiety, daimonic | Hierarchy of needs                      | Self-concept, congruence, UPR | Will to meaning                         |
| Central concern | Freedom and responsibility         | Self-actualization                      | Personal growth               | Meaning in life                         |
| View of anxiety | Normal anxiety can promote growth  | Obstacle to growth when needs are unmet | Associated with incongruence  | Associated with existential frustration |
| Main emphasis   | Courage to be                      | Growth and fulfilment                   | Fully functioning person      | Meaning and purpose                     |

**Important distinction:** May emphasizes ontological anxiety, guilt and the daimonic; Frankl emphasizes the will to meaning and logotherapy.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Introduced existential psychology to American psychology through _Existence_ (1958).
2. Developed an existential-humanistic approach to personality and psychotherapy.
3. Explained ontological anxiety and guilt as fundamental aspects of existence.
4. Introduced the concept of the **daimonic** to explain both human creativity and destructiveness.
5. Emphasized freedom, responsibility, authenticity and courage in psychotherapy.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. **Abstract concepts:** Being-in-the-world and intentionality are difficult to operationalize.
2. **Limited empirical research:** Theory relies heavily on philosophical and clinical discussions.
3. **Overemphasis on anxiety:** May underemphasize positive aspects of growth and well-being.
4. **Loose developmental phases:** The four phases are not clearly defined stages.
5. **Cultural limitations:** Emphasis on individual freedom and authenticity reflects Western individualistic assumptions.
