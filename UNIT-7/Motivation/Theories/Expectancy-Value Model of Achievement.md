### 🎯 Expectancy-Value Model of Achievement (Eccles et al.)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: A prominent model developed by **Eccles (Parsons) and colleagues (1983)** explaining achievement-related choices (like course selection and career paths). It proposes that behavior is most directly determined by two factors: **Expectancies for Success** and **Subjective Task Values** .
- **Key Distinction**: Unlike Vroom's VIE (work motivation), this model focuses on **developmental and social influences** (family, culture, gender roles) that shape a student's beliefs over time .

**2. The Two Direct Predictors (Must Know for NET)**

- **Expectancy for Success**: Beliefs about how well one will perform on an upcoming task. It is closely tied to **competence beliefs** (self-concept of ability) .
- **Subjective Task Value (STV)**: The perceived value of engaging in the task. This is the core of the model and is divided into four components:

| Component            | Meaning                                                                             |
| :------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attainment Value** | Personal importance of doing well on the task; relevance to one's identity .        |
| **Intrinsic Value**  | The enjoyment or interest derived from doing the task .                             |
| **Utility Value**    | How the task relates to future goals (e.g., career, college) .                      |
| **Cost**             | The negative aspects (effort, anxiety, lost opportunities) that reduce motivation . |

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Multiplicative Effect**: Classic EVT suggests Expectancy and Value combine **multiplicatively**. If either is zero, motivation is zero (high ability belief won't help if you don't value the task) .
- **Socialization Influences**: The model is unique for including a "left side" that shows how **cultural norms, gender stereotypes, and parental beliefs** shape a child's expectancies and values over time .
- **Modern Extension (SEVT)**: Recent updates add an "S" (Situated) to emphasize that choices are highly specific to the **immediate situation and cultural context**, not just stable traits .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Complexity**: The full model contains many variables, making it difficult to test all paths simultaneously.
- **Measurement of "Cost"**: Historically, cost was often neglected or lumped into value. Modern research argues it should be treated as a separate predictor .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Eccles = Expectancy + Value (4 components)**. Link **Attainment** to identity, **Intrinsic** to enjoyment, **Utility** to future goals, and **Cost** to barriers. This model is the "gold standard" for explaining **educational and career choices** (e.g., why girls avoid STEM), unlike Vroom's VIE which is about workplace effort.
