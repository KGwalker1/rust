### 🎯 Expectancy Theory (Vroom's VIE Theory)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Victor Vroom (1964) proposed that motivation is a conscious choice based on three perceptions: **Expectancy**, **Instrumentality**, and **Valence**. Individuals ask: "If I try, will I perform? If I perform, will I get the reward? Do I value that reward?"
- **Key Nature**: It is a **process theory** of motivation, focusing on the cognitive calculation behind effort, not just what drives it .

**2. The Three Components (VIE)**

| Component               | Meaning (The Question Asked)                                                           | Key Influences                                                |
| :---------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Expectancy (E)**      | "If I put in effort, will I achieve the performance?" Belief that effort → performance | Skills, resources, goal difficulty, self-confidence           |
| **Instrumentality (I)** | "If I perform well, will I actually get the reward?" Belief that performance → outcome | Clear reward policies, trust in management, fairness          |
| **Valence (V)**         | "Do I actually care about that reward?" The personal value placed on the outcome       | Personal needs, goals, preferences (e.g., money vs. time off) |

**3. The Formula (Must Know for NET)**

- **Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence** .
- **Multiplicative Logic**: Because the factors are multiplied, if **any one is zero**, motivation is zero—no matter how high the others are. This explains why a capable employee with a great reward system might still be unmotivated (e.g., they don't value the reward) .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Overly Rational**: Assumes people make complex, conscious calculations before acting, which may not reflect impulsive or habitual behavior .
- **Measurement Difficulty**: The components are subjective perceptions that are hard to measure reliably .
- **Individualistic**: Neglects group dynamics and organizational culture that can override individual calculations .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Vroom = VIE (Valence, Instrumentality, Expectancy)**. Link the **multiplicative formula** to the idea that motivation is a "chain" that breaks if any link fails. Know that **Valence is personal**—a bonus may motivate one employee but not another.
