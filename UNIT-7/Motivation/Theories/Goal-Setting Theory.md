### 🎯 Goal-Setting Theory (Locke & Latham)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Edwin Locke (1968) proposed that **specific and challenging goals**, when accepted by the individual, lead to higher performance than vague or easy goals. Goals serve as the immediate regulator of behavior, directing attention and mobilizing effort .
- **Key Insight**: Goal-setting is one of the most empirically supported theories in organizational psychology, with over 1,000 studies confirming its effectiveness .

**2. Key Goal Characteristics (Must Know for NET)**

| Characteristic           | Meaning                                                                                                                 |
| :----------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Specificity**          | Clear, measurable targets (e.g., "unpack 10 boxes by noon" vs. "do your best")                                          |
| **Difficulty/Challenge** | Goals should be difficult but attainable; harder goals yield higher performance when ability and commitment are present |
| **Goal Commitment**      | The person must accept and be determined to achieve the goal; influenced by importance and self-efficacy                |
| **Feedback**             | Ongoing information about progress is essential for maintaining motivation and adjusting effort                         |

**3. How Goals Motivate (4 Mechanisms)**

1. **Direction**: Goals focus attention on goal-relevant activities and away from irrelevant ones.
2. **Effort**: Difficult goals energize and mobilize greater effort.
3. **Persistence**: Goals encourage sustained effort over time until achieved.
4. **Strategy Development**: Challenging goals prompt people to devise new, creative ways of working rather than just working harder.

**4. Moderators (When Goals Work Best)**

- **Ability**: The person must possess the skills and knowledge to achieve the goal. When ability is lacking, **learning goals** (focusing on acquiring skills) are more effective than performance goals .
- **Self-Efficacy**: Belief in one's capability strengthens goal commitment and persistence .
- **Task Complexity**: Complex tasks may require breaking goals into smaller, manageable sub-goals .

**5. Criticisms & Limitations (Quick Points)**

- **Goal Conflict**: Employee goals may conflict with organizational goals, harming performance .
- **Risk-Taking**: Overly difficult goals can stimulate risky behavior .
- **Time-Consuming**: Goal-setting processes can be effortful and lose motivational power if tasks become routine .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Locke & Latham = Goal-Setting Theory**. Link the **4 mechanisms** (Direction, Effort, Persistence, Strategy) to _why_ goals work. Know the **key moderators**: Ability, Feedback, and Commitment. Distinguish **performance goals** (outcome-focused) from **learning goals** (skill-focused)—the latter are better when ability is low.
