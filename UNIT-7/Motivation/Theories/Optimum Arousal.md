### 🎯 Optimum Arousal Theory

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: The theory states that performance is maximized when an individual experiences a **moderate level of arousal** .
- **Key Mechanism**: The relationship between arousal and performance is an **inverted-U shaped curve**. Too little or too much arousal impairs performance .
- **Contrast**: Unlike Drive-Reduction Theory (which focuses on reducing tension), this theory argues that organisms are also motivated to _seek_ an optimal level of stimulation .

**2. The Yerkes-Dodson Law (The Foundation)**

- **Origin**: Formulated by Robert Yerkes and John Dodson in 1908 based on research with mice .
- **The Curve**:
  - **Low Arousal**: Under-stimulation leads to boredom, apathy, and poor focus .
  - **Moderate Arousal (The "Sweet Spot")**: Optimal performance, focus, and cognitive efficiency .
  - **High Arousal**: Over-stimulation causes anxiety, cognitive overwhelm, and "choking" under pressure .

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Task Difficulty Matters**: The Yerkes-Dodson law specifies that **easy tasks** are performed best with **high arousal**, while **complex/difficult tasks** require **lower arousal** for peak performance .
- **Individual Differences**: The optimal level varies by person. **Sensation Seekers** (a concept linked to this theory) crave high arousal and complex experiences, while others prefer calm .
- **IZOF (Individual Zone of Optimal Functioning)**: A modern extension suggesting each performer has a unique inverted-U curve specific to their personality and skill level .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Lack of Empirical Support**: Recent critiques argue the original Yerkes-Dodson law lacks substantial empirical evidence and has been inconsistently applied, particularly in areas like music performance anxiety .
- **Overly Simplistic**: The inverted-U is often criticized as being too vague and difficult to falsify scientifically .
- **Measurement Issues**: "Arousal" is a broad term encompassing physiological, cognitive, and emotional states, making it hard to measure precisely.

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Yerkes-Dodson = Inverted U**. Link the curve to **task difficulty** (easy = high arousal; hard = low arousal). Know that **Sensation Seeking** is an individual difference that shifts the optimal point on the curve.
