### 🎯 Two-Factor Theory (Herzberg, 1959)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Frederick Herzberg's theory states that **job satisfaction and job dissatisfaction are not opposites** but two separate dimensions influenced by different sets of factors .
- **Key Insight**: Removing factors that cause dissatisfaction does **not** automatically create satisfaction. True motivation comes from a separate set of intrinsic factors .

**2. The Two Factors (Must Know for NET)**

| Factor Type      | **Hygiene Factors** (Dissatisfiers)                                                            | **Motivators** (Satisfiers)                                                         |
| :--------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nature**       | Extrinsic to the job (environment)                                                             | Intrinsic to the job (content)                                                      |
| **Examples**     | Salary, company policy, working conditions, supervision, job security, interpersonal relations | Achievement, recognition, work itself, responsibility, advancement, personal growth |
| **When Present** | Prevents dissatisfaction (but does NOT motivate)                                               | Creates satisfaction and motivation                                                 |
| **When Absent**  | Causes dissatisfaction                                                                         | Causes lack of satisfaction (but not dissatisfaction)                               |

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Separate Dimensions**: The opposite of "Job Satisfaction" is "No Job Satisfaction" (not dissatisfaction). The opposite of "Job Dissatisfaction" is "No Job Dissatisfaction" (not satisfaction) .
- **Job Enrichment**: Herzberg advocated **vertical job loading**—adding meaningful tasks, responsibility, and autonomy to a role to activate motivators, rather than just adding more tasks (horizontal loading) .
- **KITA (Kick In The Ass)**: Herzberg criticized purely external incentives or threats as short-term "KITA" factors that do not create genuine motivation .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Method Bias**: The original research relied on critical incident recall, which may lead people to take credit for satisfaction (motivators) and blame external factors for dissatisfaction (hygiene) .
- **Limited Generalizability**: Subsequent studies have not always supported the strict two-factor split, and the theory may not apply equally across all occupations or cultures .
- **Satisfaction ≠ Productivity**: The theory assumes satisfaction leads to performance, but the causal link is debated .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Herzberg = Two-Factor**. Link **Hygiene = Environment (prevents dissatisfaction)** and **Motivators = Job Content (creates satisfaction)**. Use the phrase "**satisfaction and dissatisfaction are separate dimensions**" to distinguish it from theories that treat them as opposite ends of one scale.
