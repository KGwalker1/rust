### 🎯 Acquired / Learned Needs Theory (McClelland, 1961)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: David McClelland's theory states that motivation is driven by three **socially acquired needs**—Achievement, Affiliation, and Power—that are learned through life experiences and socialization, not innate .
- **Key Insight**: The dominant need varies among individuals and shapes their behavior, choices, and job performance. Needs can be developed over time through upbringing and cultural influences .

**2. The Three Needs (Must Know for NET)**

| Need                   | Meaning                                                        | Typical Behaviors                                                               |
| :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **nAch** (Achievement) | Desire to excel, solve problems, and achieve challenging goals | Seeks success, takes calculated risks, wants feedback, driven by accomplishment |
| **nAff** (Affiliation) | Desire for positive relationships, approval, and harmony       | Seeks acceptance, avoids conflict, values cooperation and collaboration         |
| **nPow** (Power)       | Desire to influence, control, or lead others                   | Seeks leadership, enjoys being in control, wants to impact situations           |

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Two Types of Power Need**: McClelland distinguished **Personal Power** (desire to control others) from **Institutional Power** (desire to influence organizational systems and bring out the best in teams) .
- **Measurement**: McClelland used the **Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)** to assess individuals' dominant needs .
- **nAch and Entrepreneurship**: High nAch individuals are drawn to moderately difficult tasks and entrepreneurial roles where success depends on personal effort .

**4. Strengths & Weaknesses (Quick Points)**

- **Strengths**: Easy to understand and apply; recognizes individual differences; based on extensive research across contexts .
- **Weaknesses**: Oversimplifies human motivation; people often have combinations of needs; limited evidence that these three are the universal main drivers; ignores lower-order needs and cultural influences .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **McClelland = nAch, nAff, nPow**. Link **nAch** to entrepreneurship and challenging tasks, **nAff** to teamwork and harmony, and **nPow** to leadership. Know that needs are **learned**, not innate—this is the key difference from Maslow and Alderfer.
