### 🎯 Drive-Reduction Theory (Clark Hull)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: A theory proposed by **Clark L. Hull** (1943) stating that behavior is driven by the need to **reduce internal tension (drive)** caused by unmet biological needs .
- **Key Mechanism**: Physiological need → Drive (tension) → Behavior → Need satisfied → Drive reduced (Homeostasis restored) .
- **Contrast**: Often called the **"Push Theory"** of motivation because internal tension pushes the organism to act .

**2. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Homeostasis**: The body's tendency to maintain a stable internal balance. Drive disrupts this balance, and behavior aims to restore it .
- **Primary Drives**: Innate, biological needs (e.g., hunger, thirst, sleep, pain avoidance) .
- **Secondary Drives**: Learned through experience/conditioning (e.g., money, social approval). They gain motivating power by association with primary drive reduction .
- **Reinforcement**: The **reduction of the drive** itself acts as the reinforcement, strengthening the S-R habit .

**3. Hull's Formula (Important for NET)**
Hull proposed that **Reaction Potential (sER)** is a product of Drive, Habit, and Incentive:

- **Formula**: **sER = D × K × sHR** .
  - **D (Drive)**: Motivation/need intensity.
  - **K (Incentive)**: The value of the goal object.
  - **sHR (Habit Strength)**: The learned association between stimulus and response.
- **Implication**: If any factor is zero, no response occurs .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Overly Biological**: Struggles to explain behaviors that _increase_ tension (e.g., thrill-seeking, curiosity) or behaviors independent of biological deficits .
- **Incomplete**: Does not fully account for secondary reinforcers (like money) or intrinsic motivation in humans .
- **Decline**: Research found drive reduction does not explain all learning, especially in cases of positive incentives or learned fear .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember Hull = **Drive-Reduction**. Link the formula **(sER = D × K × sHR)** to the idea that both motivation (D) and learning (Habit) are necessary for behavior. Know the difference between **Primary** (innate) and **Secondary** (learned) drives.
