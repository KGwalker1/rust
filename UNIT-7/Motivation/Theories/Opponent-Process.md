### 🎯 Opponent-Process Theory (Solomon & Corbit, 1974)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: A theory of acquired motivation stating that any strong emotional stimulus triggers an initial **primary reaction (a-process)**, which is automatically countered by an **opposite compensatory reaction (b-process)** to maintain emotional balance .
- **Key Mechanism**: The observable emotional state is the **sum of the a-process and b-process**. After repeated exposure, the primary reaction weakens (habituation) while the opponent reaction strengthens (sensitization) .

**2. The Two Processes (Must Know for NET)**

| Feature              | **a-process** (Primary)                                      | **b-process** (Opponent)                         |
| :------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Nature**           | Direct emotional reaction to stimulus (e.g., fear, pleasure) | Opposite emotional state (e.g., relief, craving) |
| **Speed**            | Fast onset, fast decay                                       | Slow onset, slow decay                           |
| **Change over time** | **Weakens** with repeated exposure                           | **Strengthens** with repeated exposure           |
| **Function**         | Direct response to stimulus                                  | Restores homeostasis/balance                     |

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Affective Contrast**: The aftereffect (b-process) is opposite in valence to the initial reaction. Example: The "runner's high" or relief after fear .
- **Addiction Model**: This theory explains drug addiction. Initial pleasure (a-process) weakens, while the compensatory withdrawal/craving (b-process) strengthens. The addict now takes the drug to **avoid the withdrawal** (b-process), not for pleasure .
- **Non-Associative**: Solomon argued that the strengthening of the b-process does **not** require classical conditioning; it is an innate CNS mechanism .

**4. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **Limited Empirical Support**: Research found that withdrawal responses did not always strengthen as predicted after habituation to affective stimuli .
- **Overly General**: While powerful for explaining addiction and thrill-seeking, its application to all emotional phenomena remains debated .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Solomon & Corbit = Opponent Process**. Link the **a-process** (initial, fast, weakens) to the **b-process** (opposite, slow, strengthens). Use **drug addiction** as the classic example: pleasure weakens, withdrawal strengthens.
