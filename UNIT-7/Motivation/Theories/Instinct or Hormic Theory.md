### 🧠 Instinct / Hormic Theory (William McDougall)

**1. Core Idea (Hormic Psychology)**

- **Definition**: A school of psychology (1920s) emphasizing **goal-seeking, striving, and purpose** as the primary drivers of behavior .
- **Key Term**: "Hormic" comes from the Greek _horme_ (urge/impulse). Behavior is driven by innate, purposive instincts .
- **Contrast**: Directly opposed **Behaviorism** (Watson). McDougall argued internal purpose cannot be ignored .

**2. The Nature of Instinct (3 Components)**
Every instinct has three inseparable parts :

- **Cognitive**: Recognizing the object/goal.
- **Affective**: The specific **emotion** aroused (core of instinct).
- **Conative**: The **striving/urge** to act toward the goal.

**3. Key Concepts to Remember**

- **Primary Emotions**: Each instinct is linked to a specific emotion (e.g., Flight → Fear; Curiosity → Wonder) .
- **Sentiments**: Instincts are rarely isolated. They organize around objects/ideas to form sentiments (e.g., love for country). This is how society shapes instinctive behavior .
- **Purposeful Behavior**: Behavior is **persistent** (continues until goal met), **variable** (adapts methods), and improves with repetition .

**4. Four Stages of Conduct (McDougall’s Hierarchy)**
NET often asks about the progression from instinct to morality :

1.  **Instinctive**: Modified only by pain/pleasure.
2.  **Reward/Punishment**: Social environment shapes behavior.
3.  **Social Praise/Blame**: Anticipation of approval controls conduct.
4.  **Ideal Conduct**: Acting on internal moral ideals regardless of external reward.

**5. Criticisms (Quick Points)**

- **List Controversy**: The number and definition of instincts were vague and changed over time.
- **Teleological**: Explaining behavior by its "purpose" was seen as unscientific by strict behaviorists.
- **Decline**: Faded with the rise of behaviorism and difficulties in empirical testing .
