## Ethological Approach to Motivation (Detailed)

### Core Idea

The ethological approach views motivation as rooted in **innate, species-specific behavior patterns** that have evolved through natural selection because they promote survival and reproductive fitness. Motives are not learned; they are **biologically programmed** and triggered by specific environmental cues.

### Key Concepts for NET

**1. Fixed Action Patterns (FAPs)**

A **Fixed Action Pattern** is a **stereotyped, innate, and species-specific behavioral sequence** that is triggered by a specific environmental stimulus (a "sign stimulus" or "releaser") and runs to completion once initiated, even if the stimulus is removed .

- **Example**: The male stickleback fish's aggressive attack on anything with a red underside; the grey lag goose's egg-retrieval behavior (rolling a displaced egg back to the nest with its beak).
- **Key Characteristic**: Once triggered, the FAP is **ballistic**—it cannot be interrupted or modified mid-execution.

**2. Sign Stimuli (Releasers)**

A **sign stimulus** is a specific feature of the environment that triggers a Fixed Action Pattern. It is a simplified cue that the organism is evolutionarily prepared to detect.

- **Example**: The red belly of a rival male triggers aggression in the male stickleback; the red spot on the parent gull's beak triggers begging behavior in the chick.

**3. Imprinting**

**Imprinting** is a form of **rapid, irreversible learning** that occurs during a **critical period** early in development. It is a motivational process in which a young animal forms an attachment to the first moving object it sees (the "mother figure").

- **Key Theorist**: **Konrad Lorenz**, who demonstrated imprinting in goslings.
- **Key Characteristics**: Occurs only within a **critical period** (e.g., 13–16 hours in some birds); is **irreversible**; and has **evolutionary significance** (ensures the young stay near the parent for protection).

**4. The Ethological View of Motivation**

In this approach, motivation is not a state of internal tension (as in drive-reduction theory) but rather the **readiness of a Fixed Action Pattern to be elicited by its appropriate sign stimulus**. Behavior is energized by **evolved mechanisms**, not by learned reinforcement or conscious goals.

### Comparison with Other Approaches

- **vs. Psychoanalytical**: Both agree motivation is innate and biologically based, but ethology focuses on **species-wide evolved patterns** (observable behavior), whereas psychoanalysis focuses on **individual unconscious conflicts**.
- **vs. Behavioral**: Ethology rejects the behavioral view that behavior is shaped primarily by reinforcement; it argues that many behavior patterns are **innate and unlearned**, triggered by sign stimuli without prior conditioning.
- **vs. Humanistic**: Ethology is **mechanistic** (behavior is triggered by stimuli); humanistic psychology is **holistic and growth-oriented** (behavior is driven by self-actualization).

### Criticisms (High-Yield for NET)

- **Limited Generalizability**: Ethological concepts (FAPs, imprinting) are based primarily on **animal research** and are difficult to apply directly to complex human motivation.
- **Rigid View of Behavior**: The concept of the Fixed Action Pattern is considered too rigid; human behavior is far more **flexible and modifiable** than the ethological model suggests.
- **Neglect of Learning**: The approach **underestimates the role of learning and experience** in shaping human motivation.
