Here is a concise, exam-oriented note on the **Psychoanalytical Approach to Motivation** for your UGC-NET JRF preparation.

### Core Idea

The psychoanalytical approach, rooted in **Freud's theory**, views motivation as the result of **unconscious, biologically based instincts (drives)** that create psychic energy seeking discharge. The ultimate aim of all behavior is to reduce tension and return the organism to a state of equilibrium .

### Key Concepts for NET

**1. The Four Components of an Instinct (Drive)**
Freud proposed that every instinct has four characteristics. This is a high-yield sequence for MCQs :

- **Source**: The bodily need or physiological condition (e.g., a state of hunger).
- **Impetus**: The force or intensity of the drive; the amount of energy pushing for satisfaction.
- **Aim**: The goal, which is always **satisfaction**—the discharge of tension and reduction of the need.
- **Object**: The entity or condition that allows the aim to be achieved (e.g., food).

**2. The Two Primary Instincts**
Freud later grouped instincts into two broad categories:

- **Life Instincts (Eros)**: Associated with survival, growth, and sexual reproduction (libido).
- **Death Instincts (Thanatos)**: Associated with aggression, destruction, and the return to an inorganic state.

**3. The Unconscious Nature of Motivation**
A defining feature of the psychoanalytical approach is that the true motives for behavior are **unconscious**. We are often unaware of the underlying drives and conflicts that energize our actions; behavior is a compromise between unconscious impulses and the defenses against them .

### Criticisms (High-Yield for NET)

- **Lack of Empirical Testability**: Many core concepts (libido, Thanatos) are abstract and difficult, if not impossible, to test scientifically. Some propositions are "beyond empirical test" .
- **Overemphasis on Biology and Sex**: The theory's heavy reliance on biological instincts and sexual drives is considered outdated and reductionist by many modern psychologists .
- **Conceptual Weakness**: Critics argue that the foundational idea of "psychic energy" and the drive-reduction model are conceptually flawed and no longer tenable as a complete explanation of motivation .
