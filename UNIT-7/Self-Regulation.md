# **Self-Regulation – Quick Revision Notes**

_UGC-NET Psychology | Unit 7_

## **1. Core Definition**

**Self-regulation:** The process of monitoring, evaluating and modifying one's thoughts, emotions and behaviour to achieve personal goals.

## **2. Major Theorists and Models**

| Theorist                      | Theory / Model                   | Key NET Points                                       |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Albert Bandura (1986, 1991)   | Social Cognitive Self-Regulation | Self-observation, judgment and self-reaction         |
| Carver & Scheier (1981, 1998) | Control Theory                   | Feedback loop and discrepancy reduction              |
| Roy Baumeister (1998)         | Strength Model                   | Ego depletion and limited self-control resources     |

## **3. Important Models**

**A. Bandura – Social Cognitive Model**

Three subprocesses:

1. **Self-observation:** Monitoring one's behaviour.
2. **Judgment:** Comparing behaviour with personal or social standards.
3. **Self-reaction:** Self-reinforcement or self-punishment.

**B. Carver and Scheier – Control Theory**

Based on the TOTE model by Miller, Galanter and Pribram (1960).

- Test → Operate → Test → Exit.
- Behaviour is regulated through negative feedback.
- Discrepancy between current performance and goal produces corrective action.

Sequence:

**Input → Comparison with standard → Output/action → Feedback**

**C. Baumeister – Strength Model**

- Self-control resembles a limited resource.
- **Ego depletion:** Previous self-control exertion may reduce subsequent self-control performance.
- Four ingredients:
  1. Standards
  2. Monitoring
  3. Willpower/strength
  4. Motivation

**D. Zimmerman – Self-Regulated Learning (SRL)**

Three cyclical phases:

| Phase           | Components                                         |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Forethought     | Goal setting, strategic planning, self-efficacy    |
| Performance     | Self-control, attention focusing, self-observation |
| Self-reflection | Self-evaluation, causal attribution, self-reaction |

**E. Mischel – Hot and Cool Systems**

- **Hot system:** Emotional, impulsive, immediate gratification.
- **Cool system:** Cognitive, reflective, delayed gratification.
- Associated with the Stanford Marshmallow Experiment.

**F. Kanfer – Behavioural Self-Regulation**

Three stages:

1. Self-monitoring
2. Self-evaluation
3. Self-reinforcement

**G. Higgins – Regulatory Focus Theory (1997)**

| Promotion Focus        | Prevention Focus          |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Ideal self             | Ought self                |
| Growth and advancement | Safety and responsibility |
| Focus on gains         | Focus on avoiding losses  |
| Eager strategies       | Vigilant strategies       |

## **4. Self-Regulation vs. Self-Control**

| Self-Regulation                      | Self-Control                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Broad process                        | Specific component of regulation           |
| Includes goal setting and monitoring | Mainly impulse inhibition                  |
| Initiates and modifies behaviour     | Resists immediate temptation               |
| Continuous process                   | Often involves resisting an immediate urge |

## **5. Important Related Concepts**

- **Under-regulation:** Failure to exercise sufficient control.
- **Misregulation:** Using control strategies in a counterproductive manner.
- **Ironic Process Theory – Daniel Wegner (1994):** Suppressing unwanted thoughts can produce a rebound effect.
- **Process Model – Michael Inzlicht and colleagues:** Self-control failure may involve motivational and attentional shifts rather than simply depleted energy.

## **6. Neurobiological Basis**

Most important for NET

Remember these sequences:

- **Bandura:** Observation → Judgment → Reaction.
- **Kanfer:** Monitoring → Evaluation → Reinforcement.
- **Zimmerman:** Forethought → Performance → Self-reflection.
- **TOTE:** Test → Operate → Test → Exit.
- **Higgins:** Promotion = Ideal; Prevention = Ought.

**MCQ caution:** Ego depletion is Baumeister's strength-model explanation, but it is not the only explanation of self-control failure. Inzlicht's process model offers an alternative motivational account.
