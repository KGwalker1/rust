## 1. Introduction & Background

- **Martin E. P. Seligman (b. 1942)**: American psychologist; the **founder of positive psychology** and the originator of the **learned helplessness** theory of depression.
- **Key discovery (1967)**: With **J. Bruce Overmier**, Seligman demonstrated that dogs exposed to **inescapable electric shocks** later failed to escape when escape became possible — a phenomenon they termed **learned helplessness** .
- **Theoretical trajectory**: Seligman began in the **behaviorist/clinical** tradition (learned helplessness as a model of depression), then **reversed course** in the 1990s — arguing that psychology had focused too much on pathology and too little on **well-being** . This culminated in **positive psychology** (formally launched in 2000 with Csikszentmihalyi).
- **Key works**: _Helplessness_ (1975); _Learned Optimism_ (1990); _Authentic Happiness_ (2002); _Flourish_ (2011).

> **NET Pointer**: Seligman is the **founder of positive psychology** and the **originator of learned helplessness**. A common MCQ asks: "Who first described learned helplessness?" → **Seligman and Overmier (1967)** . Do not confuse with **learned optimism** (Seligman's later cognitive-behavioral intervention) or **PERMA** (his well-being model).

## 2. Learned Helplessness (1967)

### Definition

**Learned helplessness** is a phenomenon in which **repeated exposure to uncontrollable stressors** results in individuals **failing to use control options that later become available**. Individuals learn that they lack behavioral control over environmental events, which **undermines motivation** to attempt changes .

### The Classic Experiment

- Dogs were exposed to **inescapable electric shocks** in a harness.
- Later, placed in a shuttle box where escape was possible, these dogs **failed to attempt escape** — they passively endured the shocks.
- Dogs that had received **escapable shocks** did not show this impairment .

### The Three Deficits (High-Yield)

The syndrome produced **three features** :

1. **Motivational deficit**: Failure to respond when challenged with further aversive events.
2. **Associative deficit**: Impairment of learning from successful coping.
3. **Emotional deficit**: Apparent underreactivity to painful events (though later research showed the animals were physiologically very stressed).

### Extension to Depression

In the 1970s, Seligman extended the model to **clinical depression** in humans: people repeatedly exposed to stressful situations beyond their control develop an **inability to make decisions or engage effectively in purposeful behavior** .

> **NET Pointer**:
>
> - The **three deficits** are high-yield: **motivational, associative, emotional** .
> - The original experiment used **dogs** and **inescapable shocks**.
> - Learned helplessness was proposed as a **model of depression** .

## 3. Explanatory Style (Attributional Style)

Seligman's **reformulation** of learned helplessness centered on **how people explain negative events** — the **explanatory style** .

### The Three Dimensions (High-Yield)

| Dimension           | **Pessimistic Style**                                      | **Optimistic Style**                                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Permanence**      | Negative events are **permanent** ("It will never change") | Negative events are **temporary** ("It will pass")         |
| **Pervasiveness**   | Negative events are **global** ("Everything is ruined")    | Negative events are **specific** ("This one thing is bad") |
| **Personalization** | Negative events are **personal** ("It's my fault")         | Negative events have **external** causes                   |

- **Pessimists**: Explain bad events as **internal, stable, and global** .
- **Optimists**: Explain bad events as **external, variable, and specific** .
- For **good events**, the pattern reverses: optimists attribute success to **internal, stable, global** causes; pessimists to **external, variable, specific** causes .

> **NET Pointer**: The **3-P explanatory style** (Permanent, Pervasive, Personal) is the core of Seligman's learned optimism framework . A common MCQ asks: "According to Seligman, pessimists explain negative events as..." → **internal, stable, and global** .

## 4. Learned Optimism & the ABCDE Model

### Learned Optimism

Seligman argued that **pessimistic thinking is escapable** — optimism can be **learned** through cognitive skills . Learned optimism is not merely "looking on the sunny side" — it is a set of **cognitive skills that promote resilience and flexibility** .

### The ABCDE Model (High-Yield)

Seligman expanded **Albert Ellis's ABC model** into the **ABCDE technique** :

| Letter | Component        | Description                                                        |
| ------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A**  | **Adversity**    | The negative event (objective description)                         |
| **B**  | **Beliefs**      | How you interpret the adversity                                    |
| **C**  | **Consequences** | Your feelings and actions                                          |
| **D**  | **Disputation**  | Questioning the pessimistic beliefs; finding evidence against them |
| **E**  | **Energization** | The outcome — feeling more optimistic and energized                |

- **Disputation** is the key skill: treat your pessimistic beliefs as if someone else said them to you, then argue against them .

> **NET Pointer**: The **ABCDE model** is high-yield. A common MCQ asks: "What does the 'D' in Seligman's ABCDE model stand for?" → **Disputation** . The model was adapted from **Ellis's ABC model** .

## 5. Positive Psychology & the PERMA Model

### The Shift to Positive Psychology

Seligman argued that psychology had focused on **eliminating misery** but not on **building well-being**. Happiness is **not the absence of misery** — building positive emotion, engagement, and meaning are **different processes** from getting rid of sadness and anxiety .

### PERMA (2011) — High-Yield

Seligman's model of well-being comprises **five measurable, teachable elements** :

| Element                      | Description                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **P** – **Positive Emotion** | Hedonic feelings of happiness, joy, contentment                    |
| **E** – **Engagement**       | Psychological connection to activities; flow, absorption, interest |
| **R** – **Relationships**    | Feeling socially integrated, cared about, supported                |
| **M** – **Meaning**          | Believing one's life is valuable; connection to something greater  |
| **A** – **Accomplishment**   | Making progress toward goals; sense of achievement                 |

- Each element is **measurable and teachable** .
- **"Three Good Things"** exercise: Write down three things that went well each evening — shown to reduce depression and increase life satisfaction .

> **NET Pointer**:
>
> - **PERMA** = Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment .
> - The model was proposed in **2011** (_Flourish_).
> - A common MCQ asks: "Which of the following is NOT one of Seligman's PERMA elements?" → e.g., "Resilience" / "Optimism"

## 6. Comparison with Other Theorists

|                  | **Seligman**                                      | **Beck**                                 | **Ellis**                                    | **Kahneman**                            |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Core concept     | **Learned helplessness, learned optimism, PERMA** | Cognitive therapy, cognitive distortions | Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT)     | Heuristics and biases, prospect theory  |
| Focus            | **Depression → well-being**                       | Depression, cognitive distortions        | Irrational beliefs                           | Judgment under uncertainty              |
| Key contribution | **Positive psychology**                           | Cognitive triad                          | ABC model (Seligman's ABCDE is an extension) | Loss aversion                           |
| Relationship     | —                                                 | Used insights from learned helplessness  | ABC model extended by Seligman               | Parallel researcher in cognitive biases |

> **NET Pointer**: Seligman's **ABCDE** is an extension of **Ellis's ABC** model . A sophisticated MCQ might ask: "Seligman's ABCDE model was adapted from..." → **Ellis's ABC model**.
