# **Ernst Kretschmer – Constitutional Typology (Body Build and Personality)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Ernst Kretschmer (1888–1964):** German psychiatrist who proposed a constitutional theory linking **body build, temperament and mental disorders**.
- His approach belongs to the **Type Theory of Personality**.
- **Key work:** _Körperbau und Charakter_ (1921), translated as _Physique and Character_ (1925).
- He attempted to explain the relationship between physical constitution and the predisposition to schizophrenia and manic-depressive illness.

**NET point:** Kretschmer = Body constitution + temperament + mental disorder.

## **2. Three Body Types (Habituses)**

Important NET matching topic

| Body type                | Physical characteristics                                  |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leptosome (Asthenic)** | Thin, lean, narrow shoulders, flat chest, slender limbs   |
| **Athletic**             | Muscular, strong, broad shoulders, broad chest            |
| **Pyknic**               | Short, stocky, rounded body, protruding belly, round face |

- **Leptosome/Asthenic** = Thin.
- **Athletic** = Muscular.
- **Pyknic** = Short and stocky.
- **Dysplastic** = Mixed or atypical body type, also acknowledged by Kretschmer.

## **3. Two Temperament Groups**

Kretschmer associated the three body types with two broad temperament groups.

### **A. Cycloid temperament**

- Associated with **Pyknic** body type.
- Linked with manic-depressive illness.
- Temperament varies between **jolly (cheerful) and sad**.
- Characteristics: sociable, warm, practical and natural.

### **B. Schizoid temperament**

- Associated with **Leptosome/Asthenic and Athletic** body types.
- Linked with schizophrenia.
- Temperament varies between **sensitive and cool**.
- Characteristics: reserved, withdrawn, sensitive and idealistic.

### **Important matching table**

| Body type          | Temperament  | Associated disorder      |
| ------------------ | ------------ | ------------------------ |
| Pyknic             | **Cycloid**  | Manic-depressive illness |
| Leptosome/Asthenic | **Schizoid** | Schizophrenia            |
| Athletic           | **Schizoid** | Schizophrenia            |

**Important terminology:**

- **Cyclothyme:** Normal temperament associated with the cycloid group.
- **Cycloid:** Pathological extreme.
- **Schizothyme:** Normal temperament associated with the schizoid group.
- **Schizoid:** Pathological extreme.

## **4. Core Philosophy – Constitution and Psychosis**

Kretschmer proposed that physique, personality and mental disorders share an underlying **constitutional basis**.

His central argument:

- Body build is not simply a symptom of mental illness.
- Physical constitution and psychological characteristics are related manifestations of an underlying biological constitution.
- Pyknic physique, cycloid temperament and manic-depressive illness form one associated group.
- Leptosome/athletic physique, schizoid temperament and schizophrenia form another.

**NET point:** Kretschmer's approach is biological and constitutional, rather than purely descriptive.

## **5. Criticisms and Empirical Status**

Kretschmer's typology has been largely rejected by modern research.

### **Major criticisms**

1. **Subjective classification:** Different investigators may classify the same person differently.
2. **Absence of discrete types:** Physical characteristics appear to vary continuously rather than forming three distinct categories.
3. **Weak empirical support:** Studies failed to consistently establish the proposed relationship between physique and temperament.
4. **Limited generalisability:** Associations between body types and psychiatric disorders were not consistently replicated.

### **Important research findings**

- **Klineberg, Asch and Block:** Studied over 300 non-psychotic individuals. They identified comparable physical types but failed to establish the predicted physique–temperament relationship.
- **Wertheimer and Hesketh:** Reported some support for associations between body build and disorders but could not establish distinct morphological types.

**NET trap:** The major criticism is that Kretschmer's classification was subjective and physical types were not clearly discrete.

## **6. Comparison with Hippocrates/Galen and Sheldon**

| Feature          | Hippocrates/Galen                           | Kretschmer                  | Sheldon                                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Basis            | Bodily humors                               | Body constitution           | Somatotypes                             |
| Number           | 4 temperaments                              | 3 main body types           | 3 somatotypes                           |
| Types            | Sanguine, Phlegmatic, Choleric, Melancholic | Pyknic, Leptosome, Athletic | Endomorph, Mesomorph, Ectomorph         |
| Temperament link | Humor predominance                          | Cycloid and Schizoid        | Viscerotonia, Somatotonia, Cerebrotonia |
| Approach         | Type theory                                 | Constitutional type theory  | Constitutional type theory              |

**Remember the distinction:**

- Kretschmer → Clinical and descriptive classification.
- Sheldon → Quantitative somatotype classification based on embryological development.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed an influential constitutional typology linking physique and temperament.
2. Connected personality classification with psychiatric disorders.
3. Influenced later somatotype research, particularly Sheldon's work.
4. Stimulated research into biological and physical correlates of personality.
