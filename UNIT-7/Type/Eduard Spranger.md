# **Eduard Spranger – Typology of Human Values (Life-Forms)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Eduard Spranger (1882–1963):** German philosopher, psychologist and educator.
- Associated with **humanities-oriented psychology (Geisteswissenschaftliche psychology)**.
- Influenced by **Wilhelm Dilthey's Verstehen (understanding) psychology** and Baden Neo-Kantian value philosophy.
- Major work: _Lebensformen_ (1914), translated as _Types of Men_ (1928).
- Proposed a **value-based typology**, unlike the biological typologies of Kretschmer and Sheldon.

## **2. Core Theory: Values and Personality**

Spranger argued that personality should be understood through **meaning, values and culture**, rather than biological factors alone.

Key propositions:

1. Personality is a meaningful whole, not merely a collection of isolated traits.
2. **Values organise personality** and influence how individuals interpret life.
3. Six fundamental value orientations represent six cultural domains.
4. The types are **ideal types or abstractions**, not fixed categories.
5. Most individuals are mixed types, although one value orientation may dominate.

NET focus

&#x20;Spranger's typology is based on **cultural values**, not bodily constitution or biological characteristics.

## **3. The Six Value Types**

| Type            | Dominant value          | Main characteristics                                          |
| --------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Theoretical** | Truth / Knowledge       | Seeks knowledge, analysis, logic and systematic understanding |
| **Economic**    | Utility / Usefulness    | Values practical benefits, wealth, production and efficiency  |
| **Aesthetic**   | Beauty / Harmony        | Values beauty, form, harmony and self-expression              |
| **Social**      | Love / People           | Values kindness, sympathy, service and selflessness           |
| **Political**   | Power / Influence       | Seeks authority, leadership, influence and control            |
| **Religious**   | Unity / Highest meaning | Seeks spiritual meaning, unity and the highest value          |

**Important distinctions:**

- Theoretical = pursuit of truth.
- Economic = practical usefulness.
- Aesthetic = beauty and harmony.
- Social = love and service to others.
- Political = power and influence.
- Religious = ultimate meaning and unity.

## **4. Study of Values**

Spranger's six value types were operationalised by **Gordon Allport, Philip Vernon and Gardner Lindzey** through the **Allport-Vernon-Lindzey Study of Values**.

- Measures the relative importance of six value orientations.
- Based on Spranger's _Types of Men_.
- Individuals can possess several value orientations, with one potentially dominant.

**NET connection:** Spranger proposed the six types; Allport, Vernon and Lindzey developed the inventory based on them.

## **5. Comparison with Other Type Theorists**

| Theorist            | Basis                          | Types                                                              |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Hippocrates & Galen | Bodily humors                  | Sanguine, Phlegmatic, Choleric, Melancholic                        |
| Kretschmer          | Body constitution              | Pyknic, Leptosome, Athletic                                        |
| Sheldon             | Somatotypes / embryonic layers | Endomorph, Mesomorph, Ectomorph                                    |
| **Spranger**        | **Cultural values**            | **Theoretical, Economic, Aesthetic, Social, Political, Religious** |

Spranger is distinctive because his typology is **cultural and value-based**, rather than biological or constitutional.

## **6. Contributions**

1. Developed a value-based approach to personality.
2. Connected personality with culture, meaning and human values.
3. Provided the theoretical foundation for the **Allport-Vernon-Lindzey Study of Values**.
4. Promoted the humanities-based **Verstehen** approach in psychology.
5. Influenced later personality research on values and individual differences.

## **7. Criticisms**

- **Limited empirical support:** Six types are ideal abstractions, not established discrete personality categories.
- **Descriptive rather than explanatory:** Does not adequately explain how values develop.
- **Overlap:** Individuals commonly display multiple value orientations.
- **Cultural specificity:** The six values may reflect early twentieth-century European culture.
- **Limited research:** Less empirical investigation than major trait theories.
