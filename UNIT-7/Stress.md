### ⚔️ Conflicts: Sources and Types (Berlyne's Framework)

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Conflict arises when competing response tendencies are simultaneously activated. Berlyne's analysis is the classic framework for types of conflict .
- **Key Insight**: Degree of conflict increases with (1) nearness to equality in strength of competing tendencies, (2) absolute strength of tendencies, and (3) number of competing tendencies .

**2. Types of Conflict (Must Know for NET)**

| Type                          | Description                                          | Example                                       |
| :---------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| **Approach-Approach**         | Two desirable goals, only one can be chosen          | Choosing between two good job offers          |
| **Avoidance-Avoidance**       | Two undesirable options, must choose one             | Dentist vs. paying bills                      |
| **Approach-Avoidance**        | One goal with both desirable and undesirable aspects | Wanting promotion but dreading responsibility |
| **Double Approach-Avoidance** | Two goals, each with positive and negative aspects   | Two job offers with different pros/cons       |

**3. Distinction: Frustration vs. Conflict**

- **Primary Frustration**: Aroused response tendency blocked by **absence** of goal object .
- **Secondary Frustration**: Goal object is available but some factor prevents utilization; conflict necessarily produces **internal secondary frustration** .

### 😰 Stress and Coping: Concept and Models

**1. Concept of Stress**

- **Lazarus & Folkman (1984)**: Stress occurs when an individual faces demands but lacks the resources to manage them, AND appraises the situation as **personally relevant**. It is a **transactional process** between person and environment .
- **Sources**: External (life events, daily hassles, work stressors) and Internal (faulty thinking, maladaptive beliefs, health issues) .

**2. Coping (Lazarus & Folkman)**

- **Problem-Focused Coping**: Aimed at the source of stress; attempts to remove or avoid the threat (e.g., quitting smoking after cancer risk appraisal) .
- **Emotion-Focused Coping**: Aimed at managing emotions associated with stress (e.g., reframing: "we all have to die of something") .
- **Key Caveat**: Coping is not necessarily positive or effective; adopted strategies may fail to reduce the stressor .

### 🛠️ Stress Management Strategies

**1. Biofeedback**

- Electronic devices provide information about biologic functions (heart rate, muscle activity, skin temperature) to teach patients to **control these functions** through mental exercises .
- **Uses**: Stress, anxiety, insomnia, headaches, pain .

**2. Music Therapy**

- Rhythmic and melodic elements engage and modulate neural circuits associated with **emotion and reward** .
- Can alleviate stress, enhance emotional regulation, and improve well-being.

**3. Breathing Exercises (Belly Breathing)**

- Slow, deep breath in through nose; out through pursed lips for **3–5 minutes** .
- Goal: Move tummy, not chest. Activates relaxation response.

**4. Progressive Muscular Relaxation (PMR)**

- Alternately **tense (5 sec) and relax (5–10 sec)** each muscle group, progressing from toes upward .
- Shifts body from **sympathetic** (fight-or-flight) to **parasympathetic** (rest-and-digest) mode .
- Introduced by Edmund Jacobson (1920s) .

**5. Guided Imagery**

- Mentally transport yourself to a **peaceful place**, engaging all senses .
- Decreases breaths per minute and heart rate; improves sleep, reduces pain and anxiety .

**6. Mindfulness (MBSR)**

- Purposefully pay attention to the **present moment** without judgment .
- Techniques: Body scan, noting thoughts without dwelling, yoga breathing .
- May reduce stress, anxiety, depression; improve sleep and quality of life .

**7. Meditation**

- Calms nervous system, reduces stress hormones, lowers blood pressure .
- Increases gray matter in regions tied to memory, learning, and emotional control; reduces default mode network activity (curbing worry/rumination) .

**8. Yogasana**

- Combines muscle stretch with **slow breath control** and meditative movements .
- Improves **cardio-autonomic regulation** (Heart Rate Variability), suggesting better brain-heart coupling and stress adaptation .

**9. Stress Inoculation Training (SIT)**

- **Conceptualization**: Understand the nature of the stressor.
- **Skill Acquisition**: Learn coping skills (relaxation, cognitive restructuring).
- **Application**: Practice skills in increasingly stressful situations.
- **Key**: Builds "psychological immunity" through graded exposure.

### 🎯 Theories of Stress

**1. Response-Based Model (Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome)**

- **Core Idea**: Stress is a **non-specific physiological response** of the body to any demand placed upon it. Selye is considered the "father of stress research" .
- **Key Concept**: The body responds to all stressors (physical or psychological) with the same generalized defense pattern, regardless of the specific stressor .
- **The Three Stages (Must Know for NET)**:
  1.  **Alarm Reaction**: Initial mobilization of the body's defenses (fight-or-flight); homeostatic processes are disrupted .
  2.  **Resistance**: The body adapts and attempts to cope with the continued stressor; physiological arousal remains elevated .
  3.  **Exhaustion**: The body's adaptive energy is depleted, leading to collapse, illness, or even death if the stressor persists .
- **Key Distinction**: Selye distinguished between **Eustress** (good/beneficial stress) and **Distress** (bad/harmful stress) .

**2. Stimulus-Based Model (Life Events Approach)**

- **Core Idea**: Stress is defined as a **stimulus**—a significant life event or change that requires readjustment. This approach focuses on identifying and measuring the environmental events that cause stress .
- **Key Tool**: The **Social Readjustment Rating Scale (SRRS)** by Holmes and Rahe (1967). It lists 43 life events, each assigned a **Life Change Unit (LCU)** value based on the amount of readjustment required (e.g., death of a spouse = highest LCU) .
- **Key Insight**: Even positive events (e.g., marriage, outstanding achievement) carry stress because they demand readjustment .

**3. Transactional Model (Lazarus & Folkman)**

- **Core Idea**: Stress is a **transaction** between the person and the environment. It is not the event itself, but the individual's **cognitive appraisal** of the event and their perceived ability to cope that determines whether stress is experienced .
- **The Two Appraisals (Must Know for NET)**:
  1.  **Primary Appraisal**: The person evaluates whether the event is relevant to their well-being and, if so, whether it is **irrelevant, benign-positive, or stressful** (threat, harm/loss, or challenge) .
  2.  **Secondary Appraisal**: The person evaluates whether they have the **resources and coping options** to deal with the stressful event .
- **Coping (Linked to Appraisal)**: If resources are deemed sufficient, the person engages in coping—either **Problem-Focused** (directly addressing the stressor) or **Emotion-Focused** (managing the emotional response) .

**4. Additional Theories (Brief Points)**

- **Conservation of Resources (COR) Theory (Hobfoll)**: Stress occurs when there is a **threat of resource loss, actual loss, or failure to gain resources** after investment. Resources include objects, conditions, personal traits, and energies .
- **Allostatic Load (McEwen)**: Extends Selye's model by focusing on the **cumulative wear and tear** on the body from repeated or chronic activation of stress systems .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember the **three core models**: **Response** (Selye = GAS: Alarm-Resistance-Exhaustion), **Stimulus** (Holmes & Rahe = SRRS/Life Events), and **Transactional** (Lazarus & Folkman = Primary/Secondary Appraisal). The key distinction: Selye says stress is **in the body**, Holmes & Rahe say it's **in the event**, and Lazarus says it's **in the appraisal**.
