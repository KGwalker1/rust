# **Abraham Maslow – Humanistic Theory of Personality**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Abraham Harold Maslow (1908–1970):** American psychologist and a major founder of **Humanistic Psychology**, known as the **Third Force**.
- Humanistic psychology emerged as an alternative to behaviourism and psychoanalysis.
- Maslow shifted attention from psychological disorders to healthy functioning, human potential and personal growth.
- Important works:
  - _A Theory of Human Motivation_ (1943).
  - _Motivation and Personality_ (1954).
  - _Toward a Psychology of Being_ (1962).
  - _The Farther Reaches of Human Nature_ (1971, posthumous).

**NET focus:** Maslow and Carl Rogers are the two major humanistic personality theorists.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Humanistic Psychology**

Maslow believed that human beings possess an inherent tendency towards growth, fulfilment and psychological development.

Key propositions:

1. **Human nature is inherently good:** People have positive and constructive potentials.
2. **Focus on healthy individuals:** Psychology should study psychologically mature and self-actualising individuals, not only pathology.
3. **Growth motivation:** Human beings seek personal development, meaning and fulfilment.
4. **Holistic approach:** Personality should be understood as an integrated whole.
5. **Hierarchy of needs:** Lower unmet needs generally take priority over higher needs.

**NET keyword:** Third Force = Humanistic Psychology, an alternative to behaviourism and psychoanalysis.

## **3. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs**

Maslow proposed five major levels of human needs, arranged from basic survival needs to personal fulfilment.

| Level | Need                   | Main features                                                          |
| ----- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | **Physiological**      | Food, water, sleep, air and warmth                                     |
| 2     | **Safety**             | Security, stability, protection and freedom from fear                  |
| 3     | **Love and Belonging** | Friendship, family, affection and acceptance                           |
| 4     | **Esteem**             | Self-respect, achievement, recognition and confidence                  |
| 5     | **Self-Actualization** | Realising one's potential and becoming what one is capable of becoming |

Remember the sequence

**Physiological → Safety → Love/Belonging → Esteem → Self-Actualization**

### **Important Clarifications**

- Lower needs do not have to be completely satisfied before higher needs emerge.
- Unmet lower needs generally become more dominant.
- Maslow did **not** create the familiar pyramid diagram; it was introduced later by textbook authors.
- Cognition and aesthetic needs were discussed but were not included in the original five-level hierarchy.

## **4. Self-Actualization**

**Definition:** The desire to fulfil one's potential and become everything one is capable of becoming.

Characteristics of self-actualising individuals:

- Creativity and spontaneity.
- Self-acceptance and acceptance of others.
- Compassion and concern for others.
- Independence and fairness.
- Service-oriented behaviour.
- Commitment to universal values.

Maslow studied individuals such as:

- Albert Einstein
- Eleanor Roosevelt
- Abraham Lincoln
- Thomas Jefferson
- Jane Addams
- Frederick Douglass
- Harriet Tubman

### **Self-Transcendence: Later Development**

Maslow later discussed development beyond self-actualisation.

- **Self-transcendence:** Going beyond personal interests towards higher values and meaning.
- **B-values (Being-values):** Truth, goodness, beauty, justice, perfection, excellence and simplicity.
- Individuals motivated by these values seek something beyond personal fulfilment.

**NET trap:** Self-actualisation is the highest level in the original five-level hierarchy; **self-transcendence** represents Maslow's later development.

## **5. Deficiency Needs vs. Being Needs**

Maslow divided motivation into two broad categories.

| D-Needs (Deficiency Needs)               | B-Needs (Being Needs)                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| First four levels                        | Self-actualisation and beyond          |
| Motivated by lack or deficiency          | Motivated by growth and fulfilment     |
| Reduce tension by satisfying unmet needs | Pursue higher values and meaning       |
| Physiological, safety, love, esteem      | Self-actualisation, self-transcendence |

**Remember:**

- D-needs = Deficiency motivation.
- B-needs = Growth motivation / metamotivation.

## **6. Peak Experiences**

- **Peak experiences** are moments of intense joy, wonder, awe and unity.
- They may involve mystical or transcendent feelings.
- Maslow associated these experiences particularly with self-actualising individuals.
- He used the term **peakers** for self-actualised individuals who frequently experience peak experiences.

**NET focus:** Peak experiences = Maslow.

## **7. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Dimension        | Maslow              | Rogers                        | Freud                 | Bandura                |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Approach         | Humanistic          | Humanistic                    | Psychoanalytic        | Social-cognitive       |
| Main concept     | Hierarchy of needs  | Actualizing tendency          | Unconscious conflict  | Self-efficacy          |
| Motivation       | Self-actualisation  | Personal growth               | Instinctual drives    | Human agency           |
| Focus            | Healthy individuals | Healthy development           | Neurosis and conflict | Learning and cognition |
| Key contribution | Peak experiences    | Unconditional positive regard | Psychoanalysis        | Reciprocal determinism |

**Important distinction:**

- Maslow = Hierarchy of needs, self-actualisation, peak experiences.
- Rogers = Actualizing tendency, congruence, unconditional positive regard and client-centred therapy.

## **8. Contributions**

1. **Hierarchy of needs:** Influenced psychology, education and management.
2. **Self-actualisation:** Emphasised human potential and personal growth.
3. **Humanistic psychology:** Helped establish the Third Force.
4. **Transpersonal psychology:** Contributed to the development of self-transcendence and B-values.
5. **Positive psychology:** Helped prepare the foundation for studying well-being and healthy functioning.
6. **Peak experiences:** Expanded the understanding of human fulfilment and spirituality.

## **9. Criticisms**

- **Limited empirical support:** Little strong evidence that people progress through needs in a fixed sequence.
- **Cultural bias:** The hierarchy reflects Western individualistic values.
- **Vague concepts:** Self-actualisation and peak experiences are difficult to measure objectively.
- **Elitism:** The idea of self-actualised individuals may imply a hierarchy of human worth.
- **Oversimplification:** The rigid pyramid interpretation does not reflect Maslow's more flexible original theory.
