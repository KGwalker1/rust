# **Carl Rogers – Humanistic Theory of Personality (Person-Centered Theory)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Carl Ransom Rogers (1902–1987):** American psychologist and major founder of **Humanistic Psychology (Third Force)** alongside Abraham Maslow.
- President of the American Psychological Association in **1947**.
- Developed his personality theory through clinical practice and psychotherapy.
- Emphasised the individual's **subjective and phenomenological experience**.

**Important works:**

| Year | Work                           |
| ---- | ------------------------------ |
| 1942 | _Counseling and Psychotherapy_ |
| 1951 | _Client-Centered Therapy_      |
| 1961 | _On Becoming a Person_         |

**NET distinction:** Maslow = hierarchy of needs and peak experiences; Rogers = self-concept, congruence, unconditional positive regard and fully functioning person.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Actualizing Tendency**

The **actualizing tendency** is the innate and fundamental drive present in all living organisms to maintain, enhance and fulfil their potential.

- It is the **sole source of motivation** in Rogers's theory.
- It promotes growth, development and adaptation.
- All other motives are expressions of this basic tendency.

### **Actualizing Tendency vs. Self-Actualization**

| Actualizing Tendency                      | Self-Actualization                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Fundamental drive in all living organisms | Fulfilment of the conscious self-concept |
| Innate and universal                      | Develops as the self-concept emerges     |
| Broader motivational force                | Part of the actualizing tendency         |

**NET trap:** Do not confuse Rogers's actualizing tendency with Maslow's concept of self-actualisation.

## **3. Self-Concept – Structure of the Self**

The **self-concept** is an organised and consistent set of perceptions and beliefs about oneself.

### **Three Components**

| Component                    | Meaning                                    |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Self-image**               | How an individual perceives themselves     |
| **Ideal self**               | The person one wishes or aspires to become |
| **Self-worth / Self-esteem** | Evaluation of one's own value as a person  |

- The perceived self influences both how individuals interpret the world and how they behave.
- The self-concept develops through experiences and interactions with others.

## **4. Congruence and Incongruence**

### **Congruence**

- Alignment between the **self-concept and actual experiences**.
- Real self and ideal self are in harmony.
- Associated with psychological health and healthy functioning.
- Individuals can accept experiences without defensiveness.

### **Incongruence**

- A gap between the self-concept and actual organismic experiences.
- Often develops through conditions of worth.
- Produces anxiety and psychological disturbance.
- Individuals may use denial and distortion to protect their self-concept.

| Congruence               | Incongruence              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Alignment                | Mismatch                  |
| Psychological health     | Psychological disturbance |
| Acceptance of experience | Defensiveness             |
| Lower anxiety            | Greater anxiety           |

**NET focus:** Congruence = alignment; incongruence = mismatch.

## **5. Unconditional Positive Regard and Conditions of Worth**

### **Unconditional Positive Regard (UPR)**

- Accepting and valuing a person **without conditions**.
- Acceptance is not withdrawn when someone makes mistakes.
- Helps develop self-worth and reduces defensive behaviour.
- Particularly important during childhood.

### **Conditions of Worth**

- Expectations internalised from parents or society.
- Individuals believe they are worthy only when they meet particular conditions.
- Example: "I am worthy only if I achieve good academic results."
- Leads individuals to deny or distort genuine experiences to gain approval.
- Creates incongruence and restricts personal growth.

### **Three Core Therapeutic Conditions**

Rogers identified three necessary and sufficient conditions for therapeutic growth:

| Condition                         | Meaning                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Genuineness / Congruence**      | Therapist is authentic and transparent                         |
| **Unconditional Positive Regard** | Therapist accepts the client without judgement or conditions   |
| **Empathic Understanding**        | Therapist understands the client's internal frame of reference |

Remember: G–UPR–E

Genuineness, Unconditional Positive Regard and Empathy.

## **6. Fully Functioning Person**

A person with high congruence and minimal conditions of worth moves towards becoming a **fully functioning person**.

### **Five Characteristics**

| Characteristic                      | Meaning                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Openness to Experience**          | Accepts emotions and experiences without distortion         |
| **Existential Living**              | Lives fully in the present moment                           |
| **Organismic Trust**                | Trusts personal feelings and the organismic valuing process |
| **Experiential Freedom**            | Experiences personal choice and responsibility              |
| **Constructiveness and Creativity** | Behaves constructively and adapts creatively                |

Important points:

- The fully functioning person is a **process, not a final state**.
- Rogers described it as a direction rather than a destination.
- Such individuals remain open to growth and change.

## **7. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Dimension          | Maslow                       | Rogers                   |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Approach           | Humanistic                   | Humanistic               |
| Main motivation    | Hierarchy of needs           | Actualizing tendency     |
| Central concept    | Self-actualisation           | Self-concept             |
| Key ideas          | Peak experiences, B-values   | Congruence, UPR          |
| Healthy individual | Self-actualising person      | Fully functioning person |
| Therapy            | Not his primary contribution | Person-centred therapy   |

**Important NET distinction:**

- Maslow: Hierarchy of needs, self-actualisation, peak experiences.
- Rogers: Actualizing tendency, self-concept, congruence, UPR and fully functioning person.

## **8. Contributions**

1. **Person-centred therapy:** Shifted the focus from the therapist as an expert to the client as an active agent of change.
2. **Self-concept theory:** Explained how self-perception influences experience and behaviour.
3. **Unconditional positive regard:** Influenced psychotherapy, parenting and education.
4. **Humanistic psychology:** Helped establish the Third Force with Maslow.
5. **Positive psychology:** Emphasised healthy functioning, growth and human potential.

## **9. Criticisms**

- **Empirical limitations:** Actualizing tendency and congruence are difficult to measure and test.
- **Overly optimistic:** Assumes an inherently constructive human nature.
- **Cultural bias:** Emphasises individual growth and self-development, reflecting Western individualistic values.
- **Theoretical tension:** If the actualizing tendency is the sole motivation, it is difficult to explain self-defeating behaviour.
- **Neglect of unconscious processes:** Gives less attention to unconscious influences than psychoanalytic theories.
