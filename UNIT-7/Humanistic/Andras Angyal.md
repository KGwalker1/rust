# **Andras Angyal – Holistic Theory of Personality (Biospheric Model)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Andras Angyal (1902–1960):** Hungarian-American psychiatrist and psychologist.
- PhD, University of Vienna (1927); MD, University of Turin (1932).
- Developed the **Biospheric Model**, a holistic/organismic theory of personality.
- Major work: _Foundations for a Science of Personality_ (1941).
- Belonged to the holistic tradition alongside **Kurt Goldstein**.
- Influenced **Carl Rogers**, particularly through the concepts of symbolic self and autonomy.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Holistic Organismic Theory**

- Personality is an **organized whole**, not an aggregate of separate parts.
- The whole is primary; individual components must be understood within the total system.
- Organism and environment cannot be separated without artificially dividing a single reality.
- Angyal's holistic approach bridges the psychological and physiological aspects of human functioning.

### **The Biosphere**

**Biosphere** = the inseparable unity of the organism and its environment.

It is not simply two independent parts interacting; they are aspects of one reality.

NET key term

**Biosphere = Organism + Environment as a single reality.**

## **3. Structure and Dynamics of the Biosphere**

### **Two Poles**

| Pole             | Meaning                                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Subject pole** | Organism; attitudes, drives and cravings    |
| **Object pole**  | Environment; relevance, valence and demands |

### **Two Fundamental Tendencies**

| Autonomy                  | Homonomy                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Egoistic pole             | Selfless pole                      |
| Mastering the environment | Fitting oneself to the environment |
| Self-assertion            | Self-subordination                 |
| Self-determination        | Self-surrender                     |

- **Healthy personality:** Balance between autonomy and homonomy.
- **Neurosis:** Rigid overemphasis on one tendency at the expense of the other.

## **4. The Symbolic Self**

- **Definition:** The collection of ideas, images and conscious representations a person has about themselves.
- It includes processes and states about oneself that have become conscious.
- The symbolic self may develop excessive autonomy within the organism—a **“state within a state.”**
- It can attempt to control the entire personality despite representing only a limited and subjective picture of the person.
- When it dominates the organism, it may become destructive.

**Important:** The concept of the **symbolic self influenced Carl Rogers's self-concept theory**.

## **5. Dimensions of Personality and Psychopathology**

Angyal proposed three dimensions:

| Dimension       | Meaning                                                   | Disturbance                                     |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Vertical**    | Surface behaviour vs. deeper attitudes                    | Deceptive behaviour                             |
| **Progressive** | Sequence of behaviours directed toward a goal             | Frustration                                     |
| **Transverse**  | Coordination of simultaneous behaviours into complex acts | Behavioural dissociation/incoherence; psychosis |

- Psychopathology results from the **segregation of the two poles** within one or more personality dimensions.
- Angyal also discussed **vicarious living** as a prototype of neurotic patterns.

## **6. Comparison with Goldstein, Rogers and Maslow**

| Feature              | Goldstein                        | Angyal                                      | Rogers                             | Maslow                             |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Main concept         | Self-actualization, equalization | Biosphere, autonomy–homonomy, symbolic self | Actualizing tendency, self-concept | Hierarchy of needs                 |
| Organism–environment | Holistic relationship            | **Inseparable unity**                       | Phenomenological field             | Organism distinct from environment |
| Influence on Rogers  | Indirect                         | Symbolic self and autonomy                  | —                                  | Parallel humanistic influence      |
| Approach             | Organismic/holistic              | Biospheric/holistic                         | Person-centred                     | Humanistic                         |

**Important distinction:**

- **Goldstein:** Self-actualization and equalization.
- **Angyal:** Biosphere and autonomy–homonomy.
- **Rogers:** Actualizing tendency, congruence and UPR.
- **Maslow:** Hierarchy of needs and peak experiences.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed the **biospheric model**, treating organism and environment as a single reality.
2. Introduced the autonomy–homonomy polarity.
3. Developed the concept of the symbolic self, influencing Rogers.
4. Explained psychopathology through the segregation of personality dimensions.
5. Connected psychiatry with holistic personality theory.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. Concepts such as biosphere and symbolic self are difficult to operationalize.
2. Limited empirical research and assessment instruments.
3. Overemphasis on holism makes causal separation of organism and environment difficult.
4. Clinical origins limit generalization.
5. Relatively little recognition in mainstream personality psychology.
