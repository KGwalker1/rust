# **Sidney Jourard – Humanistic Theory of Self-Disclosure (The Transparent Self)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Sidney M. Jourard (1926–1974):** American humanistic psychologist known for his work on self-disclosure and psychological health.
- Major work: _The Transparent Self_ (1964; revised 1971).
- Developed the **Self-Disclosure Questionnaire (SDQ)** with Lasakow (1958).
- Connected self-disclosure with authenticity, self-knowledge and healthy personality.

**Key identity:** Jourard = Self-disclosure + Transparent Self.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Self-Disclosure and Psychological Health**

- **Self-disclosure:** Revealing personal thoughts, feelings and experiences to others.
- **Transparent self:** A healthy personality is open, authentic and willing to be known by others.
- Disclosure helps individuals understand themselves and communicate their real identity.
- **Low disclosure** may indicate repression of the self and difficulty with personal growth.
- Disclosure can have a therapeutic quality, promoting self-renewal.

## **3. The Dyadic Effect (Reciprocity of Disclosure)**

- **Dyadic effect:** One person's self-disclosure tends to encourage the other person to disclose at a similar level.
- Disclosure is therefore reciprocal.
- Builds trust, intimacy and balance in relationships.
- Important during the early stages of relationship development.

**NET distinction:** Jourard's dyadic effect is reciprocity of disclosure. Do not confuse it with Altman and Taylor's Social Penetration Theory (1973), which developed the study of relationship disclosure further.

## **4. Self-Disclosure and Mental Health – Curvilinear Hypothesis**

Jourard proposed that the relationship between self-disclosure and mental health is **curvilinear**.

| Level of disclosure | Consequence                                                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Too little          | Repression, difficulty forming close relationships, maladjustment                      |
| Too much            | Indiscriminate disclosure; difficulty maintaining appropriate interpersonal boundaries |
| Optimal             | High disclosure to a few significant others and medium disclosure to others            |

### **Research Findings**

- Jourard (1962) found that nursing students with higher SDQ scores were later rated as better able to establish communicative relationships with patients.
- Students who disclosed least to female target persons were also less open with nursing faculty.

**NET focus:** Optimal disclosure does not mean disclosing everything to everyone.

## **5. Self-Disclosure Questionnaire (SDQ)**

- Developed by **Jourard and Lasakow (1958)**.
- Considered the first standardized instrument for measuring self-disclosure.
- Measures how much personal information individuals reveal to different target persons.

### **Structure**

| Component      | Examples                                           |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Target persons | Mother, father, male friend, female friend, spouse |
| Topic areas    | Attitudes, interests, personality, body            |

### **Important Findings**

- Disclosure varies according to the target person's role, familiarity and likability.
- Differences may occur according to sex, age, religion, nationality, race and personality.
- Age trends reported:
  - Disclosure to parents and same-sex friends gradually decreases.
  - Disclosure to opposite-sex friends and spouse increases from around 17 to 50, then declines.

## **6. Comparison with Other Humanistic Theorists**

| Feature             | Maslow                 | Rogers                        | Jourard                                    |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Core concept        | Hierarchy of needs     | Self-concept, congruence, UPR | Self-disclosure, transparent self          |
| Healthy personality | Self-actualized person | Fully functioning person      | Transparent, disclosing person             |
| Main mechanism      | Need gratification     | Therapeutic relationship      | Reciprocal disclosure                      |
| Key instrument      | —                      | —                             | SDQ                                        |
| Health relationship | Growth and fulfilment  | Congruence                    | Curvilinear disclosure–health relationship |

**Important distinction:**

- **Rogers:** Emphasizes congruence between self-concept and experience.
- **Jourard:** Emphasizes openness and disclosure to others.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Established self-disclosure as an important concept in personality and clinical psychology.
2. Developed the **SDQ**, a standardized self-disclosure measure.
3. Identified the **dyadic effect** of reciprocal disclosure.
4. Connected self-disclosure with psychological health.
5. Influenced Altman and Taylor's **Social Penetration Theory (1973)**.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. SDQ relies on self-report, which may not reflect actual disclosure.
2. The curvilinear hypothesis is difficult to operationalize.
3. Cultural bias: emphasis on disclosure reflects Western individualistic values.
4. Overemphasis on intimacy and disclosure may undervalue privacy and discretion.
5. Provides a focused research framework rather than a comprehensive personality theory.
