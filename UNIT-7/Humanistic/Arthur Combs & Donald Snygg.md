# **Arthur Combs & Donald Snygg – Perceptual (Phenomenal Field) Theory of Personality**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Donald Snygg (1904–1967)** and **Arthur W. Combs (1912–1999):** American psychologists who developed Perceptual or Phenomenal Field Theory.
- A humanistic and phenomenological approach emphasizing subjective perception.
- Key work: _Individual Behavior: A New Frame of Reference for Psychology_ (1949).
- Revised edition: _Individual Behavior: A Perceptual Approach to Behavior_ (1959).
- Third edition: _Perceptual Psychology: A Humanistic Approach to the Study of Persons_ (1976).

**Key identity:** Snygg and Combs = Phenomenal Field + Phenomenal Self + Differentiation.

## **2. Core Philosophy – The Phenomenal Field**

Their central proposition:

**All behaviour is determined by the phenomenal field of the behaving individual.**

- **Phenomenal field:** The total subjective world of experiences of which a person is aware at a particular moment.
- Includes perceptions, thoughts, feelings, memories, fantasies, ideas and interpretations of the environment.
- Behaviour depends on how a person perceives reality, not necessarily on objective reality.
- The same situation may produce different behaviours because individuals interpret it differently.

**NET focus:** Behaviour is determined by subjective perception, not objective reality.

## **3. The Phenomenal Self – The Single Basic Need**

Snygg and Combs proposed one fundamental human motive:

**The need to preserve and enhance the phenomenal self.**

- **Phenomenal self:** Those parts of the phenomenal field that an individual experiences as belonging to or characteristic of themselves.
- It includes the person's self-image and self-perceptions.
- It is both an object of experience and an ongoing process influencing behaviour.

### **Important Implications**

1. **Preservation:** Individuals protect their existing self-concept.
2. **Enhancement:** Individuals strive to develop and become more than they currently are.
3. **Resistance to change:** Experiences inconsistent with the self-concept may be ignored or distorted.
4. **Beyond physical survival:** Even self-sacrificing behaviour may serve the preservation or enhancement of one's perceived identity.

**NET distinction:** Unlike Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Snygg and Combs proposed one basic need.

## **4. Differentiation – Mechanism of Growth**

- **Differentiation:** The process of separating meaningful details or figures from the background of the phenomenal field.
- Growth and learning occur through improved perception and organization of experience.
- Individuals identify previously unnoticed aspects of their environment and give them meaning.
- Similarity: George Kelly also emphasized the individual's active interpretation and organization of experience through personal constructs.

**NET focus:** Differentiation = extracting meaningful figures from the phenomenal field.

## **5. Self-Concept Change**

Combs and Snygg (1959) described three conditions associated with self-concept change:

1. Acquisition of new concepts through experiences inconsistent with existing self-perceptions.
2. Perception of a discrepancy between the self-concept and the self required by a situation.
3. Differentiation of new self-concepts where none existed previously.

### **Why Is Change Difficult?**

- Individuals ignore experiences inconsistent with their self-concept.
- They selectively perceive experiences that confirm existing beliefs.
- Change is generally gradual, although traumatic experiences may produce rapid change.

## **6. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Feature          | Snygg & Combs                        | Rogers                      | Maslow             | Kelly                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Core concept     | Phenomenal field and self            | Self-concept                | Hierarchy of needs | Personal constructs    |
| Basic motive     | Preserve and enhance phenomenal self | Actualizing tendency        | Multiple needs     | Anticipation of events |
| Behaviour        | Subjective perception                | Self-concept and experience | Need gratification | Construct system       |
| Growth mechanism | Differentiation                      | Congruence                  | Need satisfaction  | Construct revision     |

**Important distinction:** Snygg and Combs emphasize one basic need and differentiation; Rogers emphasizes actualizing tendency, congruence and unconditional positive regard.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed the **Phenomenal Field Theory** of personality.
2. Emphasized subjective perception as the basis of behaviour.
3. Proposed a single-motive theory centred on preservation and enhancement of the phenomenal self.
4. Applied the theory to education, counselling, nursing and social work.
5. Combs emphasized that effective teachers differ from ineffective teachers in their perceptions, beliefs, values and purposes.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. The phenomenal field is subjective and difficult to measure directly.
2. Overemphasizes perception while neglecting unconscious, biological and environmental influences.
3. Does not provide a systematic developmental-stage theory.
4. Limited systematic empirical research.
5. Its simplicity and practical orientation received less attention in mainstream academic psychology.
