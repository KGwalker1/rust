# **Walter Mischel’s Cognitive-Affective Personality System (CAPS)**

**UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Walter Mischel (1930–2018)**: Social-cognitive personality theorist.
- Criticised traditional trait theory for ignoring situations and cognitive processes.
- Triggered the **Person–Situation Debate**.
- Three major areas:
  1. Cognitive Social Learning Theory.
  2. Cognitive-Affective Personality System (CAPS).
  3. Delay of gratification (Marshmallow Test).

**Important works:**

- _Personality and Assessment_ (1968).
- _Toward a Cognitive Social Learning Reconceptualization of Personality_ (1973).
- _A Cognitive-Affective System Theory of Personality_ (1995, with Yuichi Shoda).

## **2. Person–Situation Debate**

Mischel argued that broad personality traits do not consistently predict behaviour across different situations.

- Trait–behaviour correlations rarely exceeded **.30**, according to his critique.
- Behaviour changes depending on the situation.
- Individuals actively process and interpret situations.
- Behaviour results from the interaction of **situational factors and cognitive processes**.

| Traditional Trait Theory   | Mischel                       |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Stable traits              | Situation-dependent behaviour |
| Broad dispositions         | Cognitive processing          |
| Person-centred explanation | Person–situation interaction  |

## **3. Five Cognitive Person Variables (1973)**

| Variable                              | Simple meaning                             |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Competencies**                      | What a person can do: skills and abilities |
| **Encoding strategies**               | How a person interprets situations         |
| **Expectancies**                      | Expected outcomes of behaviour             |
| **Subjective values**                 | Importance given to outcomes               |
| **Self-regulatory systems and plans** | Goals, standards and self-control          |

**Important:** These variables explain individual differences in how people process information.

## **4. Cognitive-Affective Personality System (CAPS), 1995**

Proposed by **Walter Mischel and Yuichi Shoda**.

- Personality is a stable cognitive-affective system.
- Different situations activate different cognitive-affective units.
- Behaviour may vary, but the individual's pattern of responding remains stable.

### **Five Cognitive-Affective Units (CAUs)**

1. **Encodings:** How situations and people are categorised.
2. **Expectancies and beliefs:** Expected outcomes and beliefs about abilities.
3. **Goals and values:** Desired outcomes.
4. **Competencies and self-regulatory plans:** Skills and strategies.
5. **Affects:** Emotional reactions and feelings.

### **If–Then Behavioural Signatures**

- Personality is expressed through consistent situation–behaviour patterns.
- Example: **If criticised → aggressive; if praised → warm.**
- Behaviour changes across situations, but the pattern remains stable.

**Functional Equivalent Classes:** Different situations with similar psychological meanings that activate similar cognitive-affective units.

## **5. Marshmallow Test — Delay of Gratification**

High-yield NET topic

- Conducted by Walter Mischel at Stanford during the 1960s–1970s.
- Preschool children (approximately 3–5 years) were offered one treat immediately or two treats if they waited.
- It examined **delay of gratification and self-control**.

**Strategies used by children who waited:**

- Distracting themselves.
- Avoiding looking at the reward.
- Mentally transforming the reward (e.g., imagining a marshmallow as a cloud).

**Follow-up findings in the original research:**

- Greater waiting time was associated with better academic and social competence.
- It was also associated with better attention and stress coping.

**Theoretical significance:** Self-control involves cognitive strategies, expectancies and self-regulatory plans rather than simply being a fixed trait.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Triggered the person–situation debate.
2. Introduced five cognitive person variables.
3. Developed CAPS with Yuichi Shoda.
4. Explained stable personality patterns despite situational variation.
5. Advanced research on self-control and delay of gratification.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. The .30 correlation argument may have underestimated trait consistency.
2. CAPS is complex and difficult to test.
3. Gives insufficient attention to biological and genetic factors.
4. Later Marshmallow Test replications found weaker predictive effects.
5. Provides a limited account of personality development.
6. Situational explanations may become difficult to falsify.
