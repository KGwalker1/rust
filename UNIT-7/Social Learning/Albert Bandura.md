# **Albert Bandura: Social Cognitive Theory**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Albert Bandura (1925–2021):** Canadian-American psychologist.
- **Theory:** Social Learning Theory, later renamed **Social Cognitive Theory**.
- **Major books:**
  - _Social Learning Theory_ (1977).
  - _Social Foundations of Thought and Action_ (1986).
  - _Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control_ (1997).
- Emphasized cognition, observational learning and human agency.

## **2. Triadic Reciprocal Determinism**

\- Behaviour is influenced by the continuous interaction of three factors.

| Factor              | Meaning                                       |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Person (P)**      | Thoughts, beliefs, emotions and expectations. |
| **Behaviour (B)**   | Individual actions and responses.             |
| **Environment (E)** | Social and physical surroundings.             |

- All three factors influence one another.
- Humans are both products and producers of their environment.

**NET point:** P–B–E = Bandura's Triadic Reciprocal Determinism.

## **3. Self-Efficacy**

- **Meaning:** Belief in one's ability to perform a particular task successfully.
- It is domain-specific, not a general personality trait.

**High self-efficacy:** Confidence, persistence and willingness to face challenges.

**Low self-efficacy:** Avoidance, self-doubt and giving up easily.

### **Four Sources of Self-Efficacy**

1. **Mastery experiences:** Learning through personal success (most powerful source).
2. **Vicarious experiences:** Observing others succeed.
3. **Verbal persuasion:** Encouragement from others.
4. **Physiological and emotional states:** Anxiety, stress and physical feelings influence confidence.

**NET trap:** Self-efficacy = Bandura; Locus of Control = Rotter.

## **4. Observational Learning (Modeling)**

\- Learning by observing and imitating others without necessarily experiencing direct reinforcement.

### **Four Processes**

1. **Attention:** Observing the model.
2. **Retention:** Remembering the behaviour.
3. **Production:** Reproducing the behaviour.
4. **Motivation:** Having a reason to perform it.

### **Vicarious Reinforcement**

- Observing someone being rewarded increases the likelihood of imitation.
- Observing someone being punished may reduce imitation.

### **Bobo Doll Experiment (1961)**

- Children observed adults behaving aggressively towards a Bobo doll.
- Children later imitated the aggressive behaviour.
- **Conclusion:** Learning can occur through observation without direct reinforcement.

## **5. Human Agency**

\- Humans are active agents who can influence their own behaviour and environment.

### **Four Properties**

1. **Intentionality:** Forming plans and intentions.
2. **Forethought:** Anticipating future outcomes.
3. **Self-reactiveness:** Regulating and controlling one's behaviour.
4. **Self-reflectiveness:** Evaluating one's own thoughts and abilities.

## **6. Self-Regulation**

- Controlling behaviour through internal standards and self-evaluation.
- Individuals monitor their actions and reward or criticize themselves.
- Behaviour is controlled by both external consequences and internal standards.

## **7. Personality According to Bandura**

- Personality involves cognitive factors, beliefs, expectations and goals.
- Behaviour may change across situations.
- Individual differences arise from learning experiences, self-efficacy and self-regulation.
- Behaviour can follow predictable **if–then patterns** depending on the situation.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Developed Social Cognitive Theory.
2. Introduced the concept of self-efficacy.
3. Explained observational learning and modeling.
4. Proposed Triadic Reciprocal Determinism.
5. Established human agency as an important concept.
6. Demonstrated observational learning through the Bobo Doll Experiment.

## **9. Criticisms**

- Gives considerable importance to cognitive processes.
- Does not provide a comprehensive developmental stage theory.
- Biological and unconscious influences receive less attention.
- Some concepts are difficult to measure precisely.
- Does not fully explain emotional processes.
