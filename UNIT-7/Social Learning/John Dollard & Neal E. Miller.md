# **Neal Miller and John Dollard’s Psychodynamic Learning Theory**

**UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes**

## **1. Introduction**

- **John Dollard (1900–1980):** Sociologist and social psychologist.
- **Neal E. Miller (1909–2002):** Experimental psychologist; PhD under **Clark L. Hull**.
- Both worked at Yale University's **Institute of Human Relations**.
- Developed an early formal **Social Learning Theory**, integrating psychoanalysis and behaviourism.

**Important works:**

- _Frustration and Aggression_ (1939) — Dollard, Doob, Miller, Mowrer and Sears.
- _Social Learning and Imitation_ (1941).
- _Personality and Psychotherapy_ (1950).

**Also called:** Psychodynamic Learning Theory or Subjective Behaviorism.

## **2. Core Philosophy**

Miller and Dollard integrated **Freud's psychoanalysis with Hull's learning theory**.

| Freud                    | Miller and Dollard            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Unconscious conflicts    | Learned habits                |
| Anxiety                  | Learned fear as a drive       |
| Repression               | Learned responses             |
| Psychoanalytic treatment | Relearning and reconditioning |

- Personality is acquired through learning, particularly during childhood.
- Personality consists of characteristic learned habits.
- Neurosis is learned and can therefore be unlearned.

## **3. Four Fundamentals of Learning**

Very important for NET

Miller and Dollard proposed four basic factors:

| Factor       | Meaning                                         |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Drive**    | Motivation that pushes an organism to act       |
| **Cue**      | Signal directing when, where and how to respond |
| **Response** | Actual behaviour                                |
| **Reward**   | Reinforcement that strengthens the response     |

### **Types of Drives**

- **Primary drives:** Innate biological needs — hunger, thirst, pain and cold.
- **Secondary drives:** Learned drives — anxiety, fear of failure and need for approval.

**Important contribution:** Neal Miller demonstrated that **fear can become a learned drive** that reinforces escape and avoidance behaviour.

### **Habit Formation**

Repeated cycle:

**Drive → Cue → Response → Reward**

- Repeated reinforcement creates habits.
- Habits form the building blocks of personality.

## **4. Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis (1939)**

Proposed by the **Yale group**:

- John Dollard
- Leonard Doob
- Neal Miller
- O. H. Mowrer
- Robert Sears

**Original proposition:**

- Frustration occurs when goal-directed behaviour is blocked.
- Frustration leads to aggression.
- Aggression is caused by frustration.

**Later revision:**

\- Frustration creates an inclination towards aggression, but aggression may be inhibited or displaced.

**Displacement:** Redirecting aggression from the actual source of frustration towards a safer target.

## **5. Miller’s Approach–Avoidance Conflict Theory**

Miller explained motivational conflicts through four types:

| Conflict                      | Meaning                                              | Example                               |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Approach–Approach**         | Choosing between two desirable goals                 | Two attractive jobs                   |
| **Avoidance–Avoidance**       | Choosing between two undesirable options             | Two punishments                       |
| **Approach–Avoidance**        | One goal has positive and negative aspects           | Promotion with greater responsibility |
| **Double Approach–Avoidance** | Two goals, each having positive and negative aspects | Choosing between two colleges         |

### **Gradients of Approach and Avoidance**

- **Avoidance gradient is steeper than the approach gradient.**
- As a person approaches a feared goal, avoidance motivation increases more rapidly.
- This produces **hovering behaviour**, where the person oscillates between approaching and avoiding the goal.
- Explains anxiety, indecision and procrastination.

## **6. Personality Development and Neurosis**

### **Personality as Learned Habits**

- Personality is acquired, not innate.
- Primary drives are biological.
- Secondary drives develop through learning.
- Development involves acquiring habits and secondary drives.

### **Unconscious**

The unconscious consists of drives, cues and responses that an individual cannot verbalise or understand.

### **Neurosis**

- Neurosis is a **learned unconscious conflict**.
- It may develop through childhood experiences and parental practices.
- Examples include excessive punishment and lack of emotional support.

### **Psychotherapy**

- Therapy involves **relearning or reconditioning**.
- The therapist helps the individual identify and verbalise unconscious cues and responses.
- Inadequate learned behaviour is replaced with more appropriate behaviour.

## **7. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Theorist             | Main concept                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Freud                | Instincts, repression, unconscious conflict |
| Hull                 | Drive reduction and habit strength          |
| **Miller & Dollard** | Drive–cue–response–reward; learned neurosis |
| Bandura              | Self-efficacy and observational learning    |
| Rotter               | Locus of control                            |
| Mischel              | CAPS and if–then behavioural signatures     |

**Important distinction:** Miller and Dollard emphasised direct reinforcement and learning; Bandura later highlighted observational learning and vicarious reinforcement.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Developed an early formal social learning theory.
2. Integrated psychoanalysis with learning theory.
3. Proposed the Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis.
4. Developed the Approach–Avoidance Conflict model.
5. Influenced later social-learning and social-cognitive theorists.
6. Miller's later work on biofeedback demonstrated that autonomic responses could be operantly conditioned.

## **9. Criticisms**

1. Heavy reliance on animal research.
2. Drive-reduction theory cannot explain every form of reinforcement.
3. Psychoanalytic concepts are difficult to operationalise.
4. Less emphasis on cognitive processes than Bandura.
5. Limited explanation of developmental stages.
6. Social and cultural influences were acknowledged but not fully developed.
