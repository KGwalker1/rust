# Alfred Adler – Individual Psychology

| Topic                  | Key Points for Memory                                                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Founder                | Individual Psychology                                                     |
| Lifespan               | 1870–1937                                                                 |
| Break with Freud       | 1911                                                                      |
| Fundamental motivation | Striving for superiority / perfection                                     |
| Inferiority feelings   | Arise from childhood helplessness and dependence                          |
| Inferiority complex    | Exaggerated feelings of inferiority                                       |
| Superiority complex    | Overcompensation that masks inferiority                                   |
| Compensation           | Effort to overcome real or perceived inferiority                          |
| Fictional finalism     | Behaviour guided by imagined future goals; associated with Hans Vaihinger |
| Style of life          | Unique pattern of pursuing goals; develops by age 4–5                     |
| Social interest        | Gemeinschaftsgefühl; cooperation, belonging and contribution              |
| Personality            | Holistic, unified, goal-directed and socially oriented                    |

## Birth Order

| Birth Position | Main Characteristics                                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Firstborn      | Responsibility; may experience dethronement after a sibling's birth |
| Second-born    | Competition, ambition and striving to catch up with the eldest      |
| Youngest       | May be pampered or dependent; can also be ambitious                 |
| Only child     | Undivided parental attention; may struggle with peer cooperation    |

## Childhood and Personality Development

| Concept              | Key Points                                                      |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pampering            | May encourage dependence and reduce independence                |
| Neglect              | May lead to insecurity, distrust and low self-worth             |
| Organ inferiority    | Physical weakness may stimulate compensation                    |
| Family constellation | Family relationships and sibling position influence development |
| Early recollections  | Used to understand an individual's style of life                |

## Other Important Concepts

| Topic                   | Key Points                                              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Safeguarding tendencies | Protect self-esteem and avoid perceived failure         |
| Excuses                 | Justifying failure or avoiding action                   |
| Aggression              | Depreciation, accusation and self-accusation            |
| Withdrawal              | Avoidance, hesitation and sidestepping                  |
| Dreams                  | Preparation for dealing with future problems            |
| Neurosis                | Mistaken style of life and insufficient social interest |
| Mental health           | Associated with a high degree of social interest        |
| Therapy                 | Encouragement, cooperation and reorientation            |

## Contributions and Criticisms

| Contributions                                    | Criticisms                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Holistic view of personality                     | Some concepts are difficult to test scientifically |
| Emphasis on social interest                      | Birth-order claims have limited empirical support  |
| Emphasis on goals and purpose                    | Less emphasis on biological determinants           |
| Influence on humanistic and cognitive psychology | Theory lacks some empirical precision              |
