# UGC-NET JRF Psychology: EPQR and NEO PI-R

Here are short, exam-focused memory notes in tables, emphasizing authors, years, dimensions, item counts, key concepts, and common MCQ traps from your source.

## 1. EPQ / EPQR — Eysenck Personality Questionnaire

### A. Development and chronology

| Inventory                               | Year      | Key feature                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| MPI — Maudsley Personality Inventory    | 1959      | Extraversion (E) and Neuroticism (N)         |
| EPI — Eysenck Personality Inventory     | 1964/1965 | E + N + Lie scale                            |
| EPQ — Eysenck Personality Questionnaire | 1975      | Added Psychoticism (P); P, E, N + L          |
| EPQR — Revised EPQ                      | 1985      | Revised and improved psychometric properties |

Developers: Hans J. Eysenck and Sybil B. G. Eysenck.

### B. PEN model

| Dimension        | High score                                                   | Low score                          | Biological association in the source                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| P — Psychoticism | Impulsive, hostile, tough-minded, non-conforming, unempathic | Empathic, cooperative, socialized  | Androgens/testosterone; low serotonin                      |
| E — Extraversion | Sociable, active, lively, stimulation-seeking                | Quiet, reserved, passive           | Cortical arousal; ARAS                                     |
| N — Neuroticism  | Anxious, depressed, emotionally reactive                     | Calm, stable, even-tempered        | Limbic-system reactivity                                   |
| L — Lie scale    | Social desirability, defensiveness or conformity             | Less socially desirable responding | Response-style/validity scale, not a personality dimension |

Hierarchy: Specific acts and habits → traits → supertraits/factors.

High-yield point: Introverts are considered to have higher baseline cortical arousal than extraverts, who seek stimulation.

### C. Eysenck's four temperaments

|           | Low N: Stable | High N: Neurotic |
| --------- | ------------- | ---------------- |
| Extravert | Sanguine      | Choleric         |
| Introvert | Phlegmatic    | Melancholic      |

Memory trick: Match Extraversion–Introversion with Stability–Neuroticism to identify the four temperaments.

### D. EPQR structure and psychometrics

| Feature                 | Key fact                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Adult EPQR              | 100 items                                                          |
| Short version           | EPQR-S: 48 items                                                   |
| Junior version          | EPQR-J: 81 items, ages 7–15                                        |
| Response format         | Yes/No                                                             |
| Main scales             | P, E, N + L                                                        |
| Approximate item counts | P = 25, E = 21, N = 23, L = 21                                     |
| Administration time     | 15–20 minutes                                                      |
| Reliability             | E and N generally stronger; P is comparatively weaker              |
| Main criticism          | P scale has low internal consistency and skewed item distributions |

### E. Criticisms of EPQR

- P-scale weakness: May overlap with low Agreeableness and low Conscientiousness in the Big Five.
- Broad factors: The dimensions may be too broad to explain specific behavior.
- L-scale ambiguity: May measure defensiveness, social conformity, or both.
- Self-report bias: Vulnerable to socially desirable responses and faking.

## 2. NEO PI-R — NEO Personality Inventory–Revised

### A. Development and chronology

| Inventory     | Year      | Key feature                                            |
| ------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| NEO Inventory | 1978      | Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness                    |
| NEO-PI        | 1985      | Five-factor version with facet scales                  |
| NEO-FFI       | 1989/1992 | Short form with 60 items                               |
| NEO PI-R      | 1992      | Revised inventory                                      |
| NEO-PI-3      | 2005      | Improved readability; suitable for younger respondents |

Developers: Paul T. Costa Jr. and Robert R. McCrae.

Important: NEO retains its original three-factor name, but the NEO PI-R measures all five Big Five personality domains.

### B. Big Five personality model — OCEAN

| Factor                | High-score characteristics           | Low-score characteristics        |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| O — Openness          | Curious, imaginative, unconventional | Conventional, practical          |
| C — Conscientiousness | Organized, disciplined, dutiful      | Careless, disorganized           |
| E — Extraversion      | Sociable, assertive, active          | Reserved, quiet                  |
| A — Agreeableness     | Trusting, cooperative, altruistic    | Competitive, antagonistic        |
| N — Neuroticism       | Anxious, depressed, vulnerable       | Calm, secure, emotionally stable |

### E. Research findings associated with the Big Five

| Finding                 | Key point                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Heritability            | Approximately 40–60% in the source                                               |
| Personality stability   | Traits tend to remain relatively stable across adulthood                         |
| Maturity principle      | Agreeableness and Conscientiousness tend to increase with age                    |
| Job performance         | Conscientiousness is the strongest Big Five predictor in the cited meta-analysis |
| Neuroticism             | Associated with anxiety/depression vulnerability                                 |
| Openness                | Associated with creativity                                                       |
| Cross-cultural research | Five-factor structure replicated across many cultures                            |
| Five-Factor Theory      | McCrae and Costa distinguish Basic Tendencies from Characteristic Adaptations    |

### F. Five-Factor Theory: key distinction

| Concept                    | Meaning                                                                  |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Basic Tendencies           | Biologically based personality traits                                    |
| Characteristic Adaptations | Skills, habits, attitudes and values shaped partly by life circumstances |
| Self-Concept               | A person's beliefs and understanding about themselves                    |

### G. Criticisms of NEO PI-R / Big Five

- Descriptive, not explanatory: The Big Five organizes personality traits but does not fully explain their causes.
- Openness debate: Its meaning and cross-language replication are debated.
- Two higher-order factors: Some researchers propose broader factors such as Stability and Plasticity.
- Self-report bias: Responses can be affected by social desirability.
- Missing traits: The Big Five does not explicitly include Honesty–Humility, a key factor in HEXACO.

## 6. Common NET/JRF MCQ traps

- 100 vs 240: 100 = EPQR; 240 = NEO PI-R.
- 48 vs 60: 48 = EPQR-S; 60 = NEO-FFI.
- Three vs five: EPQR measures three personality dimensions; NEO PI-R measures five domains.
- Lie scale: EPQR has an L scale; NEO PI-R has no formal Lie scale.
- Psychoticism: Eysenck's dimension, not a Big Five domain.
- Competence: A Conscientiousness facet, not an Openness facet.
- Positive Emotions: An Extraversion facet.
- Vulnerability: A Neuroticism facet.
- Honesty–Humility: Associated with HEXACO, not one of the original Big Five.
- Conscientiousness: The key answer when asked which Big Five domain best predicts job performance, according to the cited research.

Memory shortcut: EPQR = 3 dimensions + Lie scale; NEO PI-R = 5 domains + 30 facets + 240 items.
