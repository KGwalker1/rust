# **Paul Costa & Robert McCrae – Five-Factor Theory (Big Five)**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Paul T. Costa Jr. (1941–)** and **Robert R. McCrae (1949–)**: American personality psychologists.
- Developed the **Five-Factor Model (FFM)**, commonly called the **Big Five**.
- Created the **NEO Personality Inventory** and its revisions.
- Worked extensively at the **National Institute on Aging (NIA)**.
- Important works:
  - _The NEO Personality Inventory_ (1985).
  - _Revised NEO Personality Inventory_ (1992).
  - _Personality Trait Structure as a Human Universal_ (1997).
  - _The Five-Factor Theory of Personality_ (1996, 2008).

**NET point:** Costa and McCrae are major factor-analytic trait theorists.

## **2. Historical Development of the Big Five**

| Researcher                   | Contribution                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |

| Goldberg (1981, 1990)        | Popularised the term **Big Five**                 |

## **3. Five-Factor Model (FFM) – OCEAN**

The Big Five describes personality through five broad dimensions.

| Factor                    | High score                           | Low score                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| **O – Openness**          | Creative, curious, imaginative       | Conventional, practical           |
| **C – Conscientiousness** | Organised, disciplined, dutiful      | Careless, impulsive, disorganised |
| **E – Extraversion**      | Sociable, energetic, assertive       | Quiet, reserved, solitary         |
| **A – Agreeableness**     | Trusting, cooperative, altruistic    | Suspicious, selfish, antagonistic |
| **N – Neuroticism**       | Anxious, moody, emotionally unstable | Calm, relaxed, emotionally stable |

**Remember:** OCEAN = Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.

**Important distinction:**

- **FFM:** Descriptive classification of the five personality dimensions.
- **FFT:** Theoretical explanation of how personality traits function and influence behaviour.

## **4. Hierarchical Structure – Domains and Facets**

Costa and McCrae organised personality into:

- **5 broad domains**
- **6 facets under each domain**
- **30 facets in total**

### **The 30 Facets**

| Domain                | Six facets                                                                             |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Neuroticism**       | Anxiety, Angry Hostility, Depression, Self-Consciousness, Impulsiveness, Vulnerability |
| **Extraversion**      | Warmth, Gregariousness, Assertiveness, Activity, Excitement-Seeking, Positive Emotions |
| **Openness**          | Fantasy, Aesthetics, Feelings, Actions, Ideas, Values                                  |
| **Agreeableness**     | Trust, Straightforwardness, Altruism, Compliance, Modesty, Tender-Mindedness           |
| **Conscientiousness** | Competence, Order, Dutifulness, Achievement Striving, Self-Discipline, Deliberation    |

**NET point:** NEO-PI-R measures both broad domains and their narrower facets. Two people can have similar domain scores but different facet profiles.

## **5. Five-Factor Theory (FFT)**

Costa and McCrae developed the Five-Factor Theory to explain the functioning of personality traits.

**Core postulates:**

1. **Biological basis:** Personality traits have genetic and biological foundations.
2. **Causal status:** Traits influence characteristic adaptations, such as habits, attitudes and relationships.
3. **Stability:** Traits are relatively stable throughout adulthood.
4. **Universality:** The five-factor structure appears across different cultures.
5. **Objective biography:** Traits influence life outcomes, including career, health and relationships.

**NET distinction:** FFM describes _what_ the five factors are; FFT explains _how and why_ they function.

## **6. Cross-Cultural Universality**

- McCrae and Costa (1997) examined the FFM across different cultural and language groups.
- Included German, Portuguese, Hebrew, Chinese, Korean and Japanese translations.
- Reported similar five-factor structures across cultures.
- Supported the idea that personality trait structure is **cross-culturally universal**.

**Important terms:**

- **Etic approach:** Studies universal characteristics across cultures.
- **Emic approach:** Studies culture-specific characteristics.

**NET point:** McCrae and Costa (1997) are associated with the etic perspective and universality of the Big Five.

## **7. NEO Inventories**

| Instrument    | Year      | Features                            |
| ------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **NEO-PI**    | 1985      | Neuroticism, Extraversion, Openness |
| **NEO-FFI**   | 1989/1992 | 5 domains, 60 items; short form     |
| **NEO-PI-R**  | 1992      | 5 domains, 30 facets, 240 items     |
| **NEO-PI-3**  | 2005      | Revised wording, 240 items          |
| **NEO-FFI-3** | 2007      | Revised short form, 60 items        |

**Important points:**

- NEO originally stands for **Neuroticism, Extraversion and Openness**.
- Agreeableness and Conscientiousness were added to the original inventory.
- **NEO-PI-R:** 240 items, 5 domains and 30 facets.
- **NEO-FFI:** 60 items, measures five domains without separate facet scales.

## **8. Comparison with Other Trait Theorists**

| Theorist       | Number                | Main theory/instrument              |
| -------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Allport        | Infinite dispositions | Cardinal, central, secondary traits |
| Cattell        | 16                    | Source traits, 16 PF                |
| Eysenck        | 3                     | PEN model, EPQ                      |
| Costa & McCrae | 5                     | Big Five, NEO-PI-R                  |

**Important progression:** Cattell (16) → Eysenck (3) → Costa and McCrae (5).

## **9. Contributions**

1. Established the Big Five as a major personality taxonomy.
2. Developed the NEO-PI-R.
3. Introduced a domain–facet measurement structure.
4. Provided evidence for cross-cultural universality.
5. Developed the Five-Factor Theory with biological and causal explanations.
6. Helped integrate different personality approaches into a common framework.

## **10. Criticisms**

1. The number of factors is debated; some researchers propose six or seven.
2. The FFM describes personality but does not fully explain its development.
3. Neglects unconscious and psychodynamic processes.
4. Thirty facets can make assessment complex.
5. Some questionnaire items may have culture-specific meanings.
6. The theory's emphasis on stability may understate meaningful personality changes across the lifespan.
7. The **HEXACO model** proposes an additional factor: Honesty–Humility.
