# **Auke Tellegen – Trait Theory & Multidimensional Personality Questionnaire (MPQ)**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Auke Tellegen (1930–2024)**: Dutch-born American personality psychologist associated with trait theory and behavioural genetics.
- Joined the **University of Minnesota in 1963**; retired in 1999 as Professor Emeritus.
- Major contributions: **MPQ**, Tellegen Absorption Scale, twin studies, trait realism and MMPI-2.
- Important collaborators: **G. Atkinson** (Absorption Scale) and **David Lykken** (twin studies).

**Key works:**

- _Brief Manual for the Differential Personality Questionnaire_ (1982) — precursor to MPQ.
- _Personality Similarity in Twins Reared Apart and Together_ (1988).
- _Wherever You Go, There You Are: Genetic Similarity and Mate Selection_ (1993, with Lykken).
- _Happiness Is a Stochastic Phenomenon_ (1996, with Lykken).

## **2. Core Philosophy – Traits as Real Entities**

Tellegen argued that **personality traits are real, latent and biologically based entities**.

Key ideas:

1. **Latent traits:** Trait measures are indicators of traits; they are not the traits themselves.
2. **Hierarchical organisation:** Primary traits combine into broader, higher-order factors.
3. **Behavioural genetics:** Twin and adoption studies help establish the heritability of personality.
4. **Trait realism:** Traits explain behavioural consistency but do not mean that individuals behave identically in every situation.

**NET connection:** Tellegen's position resembles Eysenck's biological approach and Cattell's concept of source traits.

## **3. Multidimensional Personality Questionnaire (MPQ)**

High-yield NET fact

- Developed by **Auke Tellegen**.
- **276 mostly True–False items**.
- Measures **11 primary scales**, organised into **4 higher-order factors**.

### **Four higher-order factors**

| Factor                          | Primary scales                                           | Meaning                                         |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **PEM – Positive Emotionality** | Wellbeing, Social Potency, Achievement, Social Closeness | Positive emotions, energy and social engagement |
| **NEM – Negative Emotionality** | Stress Reaction, Aggression, Alienation                  | Negative emotions, anxiety and anger            |
| **CON – Constraint**            | Control, Harmavoidance, Traditionalism                   | Caution, rule-following and impulse control     |
| **ABS – Absorption**            | Absorption                                               | Imaginative involvement and altered awareness   |

### **All 11 primary scales**

| Factor | Scales                                                   |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| PEM    | Wellbeing, Social Potency, Achievement, Social Closeness |
| NEM    | Stress Reaction, Aggression, Alienation                  |
| CON    | Control, Harmavoidance, Traditionalism                   |
| ABS    | Absorption                                               |

**Remember:** MPQ = 276 items → 11 primary scales → 4 higher-order factors.

## **4. Tellegen Absorption Scale (TAS)**

Developed by **Tellegen and G. Atkinson (1974)**.

**Absorption** refers to the disposition to become deeply immersed in an experience, involving complete attentional engagement of perceptual, imaginative, motor and cognitive resources.

### **Structure**

- **34 items**, originally True–False.
- A five-point Likert version also exists.
- Associated with hypnotic suggestibility, hallucinations and subjective responses to psychedelics.

### **Three interpretations of absorption**

1. **Absorption in experience:** Complete attentional involvement.
2. **Surrender of instrumental set:** Effortless, deep involvement rather than deliberate control.
3. **Restructuring of phenomenal self:** Alteration of one's subjective experience and sense of self.

**NET trap:** Absorption is a distinct fourth MPQ factor, not simply a component of Big Five Openness.

## **5. Landmark Twin Studies**

### **A. Four-part twin design (Tellegen et al., 1988)**

Included four groups:

- Monozygotic (MZ) twins reared together.
- Monozygotic (MZ) twins reared apart.
- Dizygotic (DZ) twins reared together.
- Dizygotic (DZ) twins reared apart.

**Major findings:**

- Estimated personality heritability at approximately **50%**.
- MZ twins showed similar personality resemblance whether reared together or apart.
- Shared family environment did not explain the observed similarity between biological relatives.

**Important qualification:** Tellegen did not conclude that parenting or environment is unimportant.

### **B. Happiness heritability (Lykken & Tellegen, 1996)**

| Component           | Heritability estimate |
| ------------------- | --------------------- |
| Stable happiness    | **80%**               |
| Momentary happiness | **50%**               |

The study used the MPQ Wellbeing scale.

### **C. Mate selection (Lykken & Tellegen, 1993)**

- Investigated genetic similarity and mate selection.
- Proposed that mate selection reflects romantic infatuation operating in a largely random fashion, rather than MZ twins deliberately selecting similar spouses.

## **6. Relationship with the Big Five**

Church (1994) examined the relationship between MPQ dimensions and the Big Five.

| MPQ factor            | Related Big Five dimensions                                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Negative Emotionality | Neuroticism and Agreeableness                               |
| Positive Emotionality | Extraversion and the surgent aspect of Conscientiousness    |
| Constraint            | Controlled aspect of Conscientiousness and much of Openness |
| Absorption            | Not well represented in the Big Five                        |

**Main point:** MPQ dimensions can be related to the Big Five, but **Absorption is its distinctive contribution**.

## **8. Major Contributions**

1. Developed the **Multidimensional Personality Questionnaire**.
2. Developed the **Tellegen Absorption Scale** with G. Atkinson.
3. Advanced trait realism: traits as real, latent and biologically based entities.
4. Conducted landmark twin studies on personality similarity.
5. Investigated the genetic contribution to happiness.
6. Contributed to the development of the **MMPI-2**.

## **9. Criticisms and Limitations**

- **80% stable happiness heritability:** Controversial and potentially dependent on sample and methodology.
- **MPQ usage:** Less widely used than the NEO-PI-R and 16 PF.
- **Absorption measurement:** Some TAS items raise questions about the exact construct being measured.
- **Twin-study assumptions:** Equal-environments assumptions and gene–environment interactions complicate interpretation.
- **Four-factor structure:** Less widely adopted than the Big Five.
