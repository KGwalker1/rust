# **Jerry S. Wiggins – Interpersonal Circumplex Model of Personality**

_UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes_

## **1. Introduction**

- **Jerry S. Wiggins (1931–2006):** American personality psychologist associated with the interpersonal approach to personality assessment.
- Developed the **Interpersonal Circumplex Model** and **Interpersonal Adjective Scales (IAS)**.
- His model organises interpersonal personality traits around two dimensions: **Dominance and Nurturance**.
- Major works:
  - _A Psychological Taxonomy of Trait-Descriptive Terms: The Interpersonal Domain_ (1979).
  - _Circumplex Models of Interpersonal Behavior_ (1980).
  - **IAS-R** (1988, with Trapnell and Phillips).

**NET focus:** Wiggins studied interpersonal traits specifically. His model connects closely with two Big Five dimensions: Extraversion and Agreeableness.

## **2. Core Philosophy – Interpersonal Circumplex**

The Interpersonal Circumplex is a circular model that organises interpersonal behaviour around **two orthogonal dimensions**.

### **The two axes**

| Axis       | Dimension                       | High pole                           | Low pole                |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Vertical   | **Dominance (Agency/Status)**   | Assertive, influential, controlling | Submissive, deferential |
| Horizontal | **Nurturance (Love/Communion)** | Warm, friendly, empathetic          | Cold, hostile, distant  |

- **Agency:** The tendency to assert influence and control in social situations.
- **Communion:** The tendency to seek warmth, closeness and connection with others.
- The axes are **orthogonal**, meaning they are statistically independent.

A person can therefore be:

- High in both: warm and dominant.
- Low in both: cold and submissive.
- High in one and low in the other: combinations of dominance and warmth.

## **3. The Eight Octants**

The circumplex is divided into **eight octants**, each representing a combination of Dominance and Nurturance.

| Octant | Label                  | Personality combination            |
| ------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **PA** | Assured-Dominant       | High dominance, neutral nurturance |
| **BC** | Arrogant-Calculating   | High dominance, low nurturance     |
| **DE** | Cold-Hearted           | Neutral dominance, low nurturance  |
| **FG** | Aloof-Introverted      | Low dominance, low nurturance      |
| **HI** | Unassured-Submissive   | Low dominance, neutral nurturance  |
| **JK** | Unassuming-Ingenuous   | Low dominance, high nurturance     |
| **LM** | Warm-Agreeable         | Neutral dominance, high nurturance |
| **NO** | Gregarious-Extraverted | High dominance, high nurturance    |

**Important examples:**

- Arrogant-Calculating = dominant + cold.
- Warm-Agreeable = warm + moderate dominance.
- Gregarious-Extraverted = dominant + warm.
- Aloof-Introverted = submissive + cold.

## **4. Relationship with the Big Five**

One of the most important findings is the relationship between Wiggins's circumplex and the Big Five.

| Wiggins's dimension             | Big Five factor   |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Dominance (Agency)**          | **Extraversion**  |
| **Nurturance (Communion/Love)** | **Agreeableness** |

- **McCrae and Costa (1989)** examined the relationship between the IAS-R and NEO-PI.
- Their research showed that the interpersonal circumplex is defined primarily by Extraversion and Agreeableness.
- Wiggins's model is therefore complementary to the Big Five, not a competing five-factor model.
- The Big Five describes broad personality dimensions, whereas the circumplex provides a detailed structure of interpersonal traits.

High-yield NET point

**Wiggins = Dominance × Nurturance = Extraversion × Agreeableness.**

## **5. Interpersonal Adjective Scales-Revised (IAS-R)**

### **Structure**

- Developed by Wiggins, Trapnell and Phillips (1988).
- Contains **64 adjectives**.
- Contains 8 octant scales, with 8 adjectives per octant.
- Uses an **8-point Likert scale**.

### **Original IAS vs. IAS-R**

| Instrument   | Year | Number of adjectives |
| ------------ | ---- | -------------------- |
| Original IAS | 1979 | 128                  |
| IAS-R        | 1988 | **64**               |

### **Three Circumplex Properties**

| Property          | Meaning                    | Correlation                |
| ----------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Adjacency**     | Traits next to each other  | Positive                   |
| **Bipolarity**    | Traits opposite each other | Negative                   |
| **Orthogonality** | Traits 90° apart           | Zero or approximately zero |

Examples:

- Assured-Dominant and Arrogant-Calculating: adjacent, positively correlated.
- Assured-Dominant and Unassured-Submissive: opposite, negatively correlated.
- Assured-Dominant and Warm-Agreeable: 90° apart, approximately uncorrelated.

## **7. Contributions**

1. Developed the **Interpersonal Circumplex Model**.
2. Developed the **IAS-R**, a measure of interpersonal traits.
3. Established a systematic structure for interpersonal personality characteristics.
4. Connected interpersonal traits with Extraversion and Agreeableness in the Big Five.
5. Influenced research on personality disorders by helping understand extreme interpersonal behaviours as variations of normal interpersonal traits.

## **8. Criticisms**

1. **Limited scope:** Focuses on interpersonal traits rather than the complete personality structure.
2. **Circularity concerns:** The circular arrangement may partly reflect the analytical method or response patterns.
3. **Cultural variability:** Some cultural adaptations have produced elliptical rather than circular structures.
4. **Descriptive approach:** Describes interpersonal traits but does not fully explain their origins or development.
