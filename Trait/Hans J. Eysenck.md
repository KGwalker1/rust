# **Hans J. Eysenck – Biological Trait Theory (PEN Model)**

UGC-NET JRF Psychology — Short Revision Notes

## **1. Introduction**

- **Hans J. Eysenck (1916–1997):** German-born British psychologist.
- PhD, University of London (1940); worked at Maudsley Hospital.
- Developed a **biological, hierarchical and factor-analytic** theory of personality.
- Major contribution: **PEN Model** and **Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ)**.
- Important works:
  - _Dimensions of Personality_ (1947).
  - _The Biological Basis of Personality_ (1967).
  - _The Structure of Human Personality_ (1970).
  - _Psychoticism as a Dimension of Personality_ (1976).

**Approach:** Nomothetic, factor-analytic and biological.

## **2. Hierarchical Structure of Personality**

Eysenck proposed four levels, from specific behaviours to broad personality dimensions.

| Level                 | Meaning                         | Example            |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 1. Specific responses | Single acts in a situation      | Smiling once       |
| 2. Habitual responses | Repeated behaviours             | Smiling frequently |
| 3. Traits             | Clusters of related habits      | Sociability        |
| 4. Types              | Broad dimensions of personality | Extraversion       |

**Order to remember:**

Specific Response → Habit → Trait → Type

- **Type level** is the highest and broadest level.
- PEN dimensions belong to the type level.

## **3. PEN Model – Three Personality Dimensions**

Eysenck proposed three broad, statistically independent (**orthogonal**) dimensions.

| Dimension            | High score                                            | Low score                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **P – Psychoticism** | Aggressive, cold, impulsive, egocentric, tough-minded | Empathetic, cooperative, warm     |
| **E – Extraversion** | Sociable, active, assertive, sensation-seeking        | Quiet, reserved, introspective    |
| **N – Neuroticism**  | Anxious, tense, moody, emotionally unstable           | Calm, relaxed, emotionally stable |

**Important points:**

- Originally, Eysenck proposed **Extraversion and Neuroticism** (1947).
- **Psychoticism was added later**, during the 1970s.
- PEN = Psychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism.

## **4. Biological Basis of Personality**

Eysenck's distinctive contribution was linking personality dimensions with physiological systems.

### **A. Extraversion – ARAS**

**ARAS = Ascending Reticular Activating System**

- Regulates cortical arousal, wakefulness and attention.
- **Introverts:** Higher basal ARAS arousal; easily overstimulated; prefer less stimulation.
- **Extraverts:** Lower basal ARAS arousal; seek excitement and stimulation.

**Remember this important contrast**

- Introverts = HIGHER arousal.
- Extraverts = LOWER arousal.
- Introverts avoid excessive stimulation; extraverts seek stimulation.

### **B. Neuroticism – Limbic System**

- The limbic system is associated with emotional arousal.
- **High N:** Lower activation threshold, greater autonomic responsivity and emotional reactivity.
- **Low N:** Higher threshold and greater emotional stability.

### **C. Psychoticism**

- Biological explanation was less clearly developed.
- Eysenck broadly associated it with hormonal and genetic factors.

**NET matching:**

- Extraversion ↔ ARAS.
- Neuroticism ↔ Limbic system.
- Psychoticism ↔ Hormonal/genetic factors (broadly).

## **5. Assessment – Eysenck's Questionnaires**

| Instrument                                  | Year     | Dimensions              |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----------------------- |
| MMQ – Maudsley Medical Questionnaire        | 1947     | Neuroticism             |
| MPI – Maudsley Personality Inventory        | 1959     | E and N                 |
| EPI – Eysenck Personality Inventory         | 1964     | E and N                 |
| **EPQ – Eysenck Personality Questionnaire** | **1975** | **P, E, N + Lie scale** |
| EPQ-R                                       | 1985     | Revised P, E, N         |

- **EPQ** measures all three PEN dimensions.
- **Lie (L) scale:** Detects socially desirable responding.
- The Psychoticism scale has faced validity criticisms.

**Do not confuse:**

- EPQ = Eysenck.
- 16 PF = Cattell.
- MMPI = Hathaway and McKinley.

## **6. Heredity and Environment**

- Eysenck was a strong **hereditarian**.
- He argued that personality differences are substantially influenced by genetic and biological factors.
- Twin studies were used to support his position.
- He estimated neuroticism heritability at around **80%**, although this estimate was criticised.
- Biological factors create predispositions; environmental conditions influence their expression.

**NET distinction:** Eysenck emphasised biological/genetic influences, whereas Bandura emphasised reciprocal interactions between person, behaviour and environment.

## **7. Comparison with Other Theorists**

| Theorist       | Approach                     | Number   | Main contribution            |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------- |
| Allport        | Idiographic                  | Infinite | Cardinal, central, secondary |
| Cattell        | Factor-analytic              | 16       | Source traits, 16 PF         |
| Eysenck        | Factor-analytic + biological | 3        | PEN model, EPQ               |
| Costa & McCrae | Factor-analytic              | 5        | Big Five (OCEAN)             |

**Important:** Cattell and Eysenck are both **nomothetic, factor-analytic trait theorists**.

### **Eysenck vs. Big Five**

- Eysenck's Psychoticism dimension has been criticised by McCrae and Costa.
- They argued that it may combine **low Agreeableness and low Conscientiousness**, rather than represent a completely independent dimension.

## **8. Contributions**

1. Developed the PEN model of personality.
2. Established biological explanations of personality differences.
3. Linked Extraversion with ARAS and Neuroticism with the limbic system.
4. Developed the EPQ.
5. Introduced a four-level hierarchical structure of personality.
6. Encouraged research into the genetic basis of personality.

## **9. Criticisms**

1. Psychoticism has questionable psychometric validity.
2. Arousal theory has received mixed empirical support.
3. Overemphasised genetic influences.
4. The three dimensions may not capture the full complexity of personality.
5. Neglected cognitive and social processes in personality development.
6. Basal arousal differences between introverts and extraverts have received limited support.

## **11. Likely NET MCQ Traps**

- **PEN:** Psychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism—not Openness or Conscientiousness.
- **Hierarchy:** Specific response → Habit → Trait → Type.
- **PEN dimensions:** Type level, not trait level.
- **ARAS:** Associated with Extraversion.
- **Limbic system:** Associated with Neuroticism.
- **Arousal:** Introverts have higher basal arousal than extraverts.
- **EPQ:** Measures P, E, N and Lie scale.
- **Psychoticism:** Added after the original E–N model.
- **Cattell vs. Eysenck:** 16 PF vs. PEN.
- **Allport vs. Eysenck:** Idiographic vs. nomothetic.
- **McCrae and Costa:** Criticised the distinctiveness of Psychoticism.
