# **Theoretical Perspective on Thought Processes: Gestalt Psychology**

A concise, exam-oriented note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)**.

## **1. Meaning and Introduction**

- **Gestalt Psychology** explains thinking as an organised, holistic process rather than merely an association of separate ideas.
- The word _Gestalt_ is German for **form, pattern or organised whole**.
- It argues that the whole is different from the sum of its individual parts.
- Thinking involves organising, restructuring and understanding relationships among elements.
- Gestalt psychologists emphasised insight, perception and problem-solving.

**Central assumption:** Thinking involves understanding the overall structure of a problem, not simply connecting isolated experiences.

## **3. Fundamental Principles of Gestalt Psychology**

Gestalt psychology emphasises how the mind organises information into meaningful patterns.

| Principle            | Meaning                                                            |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Prägnanz (Good Form) | The mind organises experiences into the simplest, most stable form |
| Figure–Ground        | Separating the main object from its background                     |
| Proximity            | Objects close together are perceived as a group                    |
| Similarity           | Similar objects are grouped together                               |
| Closure              | The mind fills gaps to perceive a complete pattern                 |
| Continuity           | The mind prefers smooth, continuous patterns                       |
| Common Fate          | Elements moving in the same direction are perceived as a group     |

**NET point:** Prägnanz is also known as the Law of Good Form or Law of Simplicity.

## **4. Köhler's Insight Learning Theory (1925)**

- Wolfgang Köhler developed the concept of **insight learning** through experiments with chimpanzees.
- His famous experiments were conducted at the Tenerife Anthropoid Research Station.
- Chimpanzees solved problems by suddenly understanding the relationships between objects rather than relying only on trial and error.

**Example: Sultan experiment**

- A chimpanzee named Sultan was unable to reach a banana placed outside its cage.
- It suddenly understood that two short sticks could be joined to make a longer stick.
- It used the combined stick to obtain the banana.

### **Characteristics of Insight Learning**

1. Sudden understanding of the problem.
2. Recognition of relationships between elements.
3. Restructuring of the problem situation.
4. Purposeful solution rather than random responses.
5. Solution may be transferred to similar situations.

**Important work:** _The Mentality of Apes_ (1925).

## **5. Wertheimer's Productive Thinking**

- Max Wertheimer distinguished between **productive thinking** and **reproductive thinking**.
- He argued that genuine thinking involves understanding the structure and relationships within a problem.

| Productive Thinking               | Reproductive Thinking             |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Involves new understanding        | Uses previously learned solutions |
| Restructures the problem          | Follows familiar procedures       |
| Focuses on relationships          | Relies on memorised methods       |
| Encourages creativity and insight | Relies on repetition              |

**Example:**

- Productive: Understanding why a mathematical formula works.
- Reproductive: Memorising and applying the formula without understanding it.

## **6. Kurt Lewin's Field Theory**

- Kurt Lewin applied Gestalt principles to motivation and behaviour.
- Behaviour is a function of the person and the environment.

\\[ B=f(P,E) \\]

Where:

- B = Behaviour
- P = Person
- E = Environment

### **Important Concepts**

- **Life Space:** Total psychological environment of an individual at a particular time.
- **Valence:** Attractive or repulsive value of an object or goal.
- **Tension:** Psychological state that motivates behaviour.
- **Psychological Field:** The total situation influencing an individual's behaviour.

**Important:** Lewin is associated with field theory, not the original founding of Gestalt psychology.

## **7. Gestalt Approach to Problem-Solving**

According to Gestalt psychologists, problem-solving involves:

1. Understanding the problem situation.
2. Identifying relationships among elements.
3. Restructuring the problem.
4. Experiencing insight.
5. Applying the solution.

**Important concept – Functional Fixedness:**

- Inability to perceive alternative uses of familiar objects.
- Demonstrated through Karl Duncker's candle problem (1945).
- It acts as an obstacle to restructuring and insight.

## **8. Limitations and Criticism**

- Insight is difficult to measure scientifically.
- Gestalt theory sometimes underestimates the role of trial-and-error learning.
- It gives less attention to reinforcement and past learning.
- It does not fully explain all forms of complex reasoning.
- Some Gestalt principles are more descriptive than explanatory.
