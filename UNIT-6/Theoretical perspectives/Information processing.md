# **Theoretical Perspective on Thought Processes: Information Processing Approach**

A concise, exam-oriented note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)**.

## **1. Meaning and Introduction**

- The **Information Processing Approach (IPA)** explains thinking as a series of mental operations through which information is received, encoded, stored, transformed and retrieved.
- It compares the human mind to an information-processing system, although the mind is more complex than a computer.
- It focuses on how individuals attend to, interpret, remember and use information.
- It became influential during the **Cognitive Revolution of the 1950s and 1960s**.

**Central assumption:** Human thinking involves active processing of information through different cognitive systems.

The approach commonly describes cognition through the following stages:

1. **Input:** Information enters through sensory organs.
2. **Attention:** Relevant information is selected.
3. **Encoding:** Information is converted into a form that can be mentally processed.
4. **Storage:** Information is retained in memory.
5. **Retrieval:** Stored information is brought back when required.
6. **Response:** Processed information guides behaviour or decision-making.

## **6. Broadbent's Filter Model of Attention (1958)**

- Donald Broadbent proposed the **Early Selection Theory**.
- Information enters through sensory channels.
- A selective filter chooses information based on physical characteristics.
- Selected information receives further processing; unattended information is largely filtered out.

**NET connection:** Broadbent = Early Selection; Treisman = Attenuation; Deutsch and Deutsch = Late Selection.

## **7. Information Processing in Problem-Solving**

Newell and Simon (1972) explained human problem-solving using an information-processing framework.

### **Important concepts**

- **Problem Space:** The total set of possible states and actions involved in solving a problem.
- **Initial State:** Starting point of the problem.
- **Goal State:** Desired outcome.
- **Operators:** Actions used to move from one state to another.
- **Means–Ends Analysis:** Reducing the difference between the current state and the goal state.

**Important work:** _Human Problem Solving_ (1972), by Allen Newell and Herbert A. Simon.

## **8. Sternberg's Information-Processing Components**

Robert Sternberg explained intelligence through information-processing components in his Triarchic Theory.

| Component                        | Function                                     |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Metacomponents                   | Planning, monitoring and evaluating thinking |
| Performance Components           | Carrying out tasks and executing strategies  |
| Knowledge-Acquisition Components | Learning and acquiring new information       |

**NET point:** Metacomponents regulate and control cognitive performance.

## **9. Characteristics of the Information Processing Approach**

1. Mind is viewed as an active information-processing system.
2. Processing involves multiple stages.
3. Attention is selective.
4. Memory involves encoding, storage and retrieval.
5. Thinking involves mental representations and transformations.
6. Processing capacity is limited.
7. Prior knowledge influences the interpretation of information.

## **10. Limitations and Criticism**

- Computer analogy may oversimplify human cognition.
- Gives less attention to emotions, motivation and social influences.
- Some models are too linear and fail to explain parallel processing.
- Individual differences and cultural influences may be underemphasised.
- Not all thinking can be explained through conscious, step-by-step processing.
