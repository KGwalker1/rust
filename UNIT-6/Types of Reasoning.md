# **Types of Reasoning**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Reasoning** is a cognitive process through which individuals use available information, facts and knowledge to draw conclusions, make judgements and solve problems.
- It involves logical thinking, inference and evaluation of evidence.
- Reasoning is an important component of intelligence, decision-making and problem-solving.

## **2. Major Types of Reasoning**

### **A. Deductive Reasoning**

- Moves from **general principles to specific conclusions**.
- If the premises are true and the argument is logically valid, the conclusion must be true.
- Associated with formal logic and syllogistic reasoning.

**Example:**

1. All humans are mortal.
2. Ram is a human.
3. Therefore, Ram is mortal.

**Important:** Deduction provides logically necessary conclusions when the premises are true and the argument is valid.

### **B. Inductive Reasoning**

- Moves from **specific observations to general conclusions**.
- Conclusions are probable rather than logically certain.
- Commonly used in scientific research and everyday learning.

**Example:**

1. Iron expands when heated.
2. Copper expands when heated.
3. Aluminium expands when heated.
4. Therefore, metals generally expand when heated.

**Important:** Inductive conclusions can be revised when new evidence becomes available.

### **C. Abductive Reasoning**

- Involves finding the **most plausible explanation** for an observation.
- Also called inference to the best explanation.
- Commonly used in diagnosis and scientific investigation.

**Example:**

- A person has a fever and cough.
- A respiratory infection may be the most plausible explanation, although further investigation is required.

### **D. Analogical Reasoning**

- Involves drawing conclusions by identifying similarities between two situations or objects.
- Knowledge from a familiar situation is applied to a new situation.

**Example:**

\- The functioning of the human brain is sometimes compared to a computer's information-processing system.

### **E. Causal Reasoning**

- Involves identifying cause-and-effect relationships between events.
- Helps understand why something happens.
- Example: Investigating whether sleep deprivation affects memory performance.

**Important:** Correlation does not necessarily establish causation.

### **F. Critical Reasoning**

- Involves evaluating arguments, evidence and assumptions.
- Helps distinguish reliable information from unsupported claims.
- Example: Evaluating the validity of a research conclusion.

### **G. Counterfactual Reasoning**

- Involves imagining alternative outcomes to events that have already occurred.
- Often expressed through _what if_ thinking.
- Example: "If I had studied earlier, I might have scored better."

### **H. Probabilistic Reasoning**

- Involves judging the likelihood of events occurring.
- Uses probability and uncertainty to make conclusions.
- Example: Estimating the likelihood of rain based on weather information.

## **4. Important Theories and Researchers**

### **A. Jean Piaget – Development of Reasoning**

Piaget explained the development of reasoning through four stages of cognitive development.

| Stage                | Age              | Reasoning characteristics                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sensorimotor         | 0–2 years        | Learning through sensory and motor experiences.          |
| Preoperational       | 2–7 years        | Intuitive thinking; limited logical reasoning.           |
| Concrete operational | 7–11 years       | Logical reasoning about concrete objects and situations. |
| Formal operational   | 11 years onwards | Abstract, hypothetical and deductive reasoning.          |

**NET point:** Hypothetical-deductive reasoning develops during the formal operational stage.

### **B. Kohlberg – Moral Reasoning**

Lawrence Kohlberg proposed three levels of moral reasoning:

1. **Preconventional:** Moral decisions based on punishment and reward.
2. **Conventional:** Moral decisions based on social approval, rules and laws.
3. **Postconventional:** Moral decisions based on universal ethical principles and individual rights.

### **C. Dual-Process Theory – Daniel Kahneman**

| System 1             | System 2             |
| -------------------- | -------------------- |
| Fast and automatic   | Slow and deliberate  |
| Intuitive            | Analytical           |
| Requires less effort | Requires more effort |
| Often unconscious    | More conscious       |

## **5. Deductive vs Inductive vs Abductive Reasoning**

| Feature      | Deductive                          | Inductive                  | Abductive                      |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Direction    | General → Specific                 | Specific → General         | Observation → Best explanation |
| Conclusion   | Certain if valid premises are true | Probable                   | Plausible                      |
| Main purpose | Testing logical conclusions        | Developing generalisations | Explaining observations        |
| Example      | Mathematical proof                 | Scientific generalisation  | Medical diagnosis              |

## **6. Common Errors in Reasoning**

| Error                    | Meaning                                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Confirmation bias**    | Favouring information that supports existing beliefs.               |
| **Hasty generalisation** | Drawing a broad conclusion from insufficient evidence.              |
| **False cause**          | Assuming that one event caused another without sufficient evidence. |
| **Circular reasoning**   | Using the conclusion as support for the argument itself.            |
| **Ad hominem**           | Attacking the person instead of addressing the argument.            |
| **False dilemma**        | Presenting only two choices when more alternatives exist.           |

## **8. Common MCQ Traps**

- **Deductive reasoning:** A valid argument with true premises guarantees a true conclusion.
- **Inductive reasoning:** Conclusions are probable, not guaranteed.
- **Abductive reasoning:** Selects the most plausible explanation.
- **Analogical reasoning:** Based on similarities.
- **Causal reasoning:** Focuses on cause-and-effect relationships.
- **Piaget:** Formal operational stage involves abstract and hypothetical-deductive reasoning.
- **Kohlberg:** Associated with moral reasoning, not general intelligence.
