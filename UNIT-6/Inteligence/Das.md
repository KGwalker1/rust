# **J. P. Das's PASS Theory of Intelligence**

## **1. Introduction**

- **J. P. Das (Jagannath Prasad Das)** – Indian-born psychologist associated with the University of Alberta, Canada.

- Developed the **PASS Theory of Intelligence** along with **Jack A. Naglieri and John R. Kirby**.

- Based on the neuropsychological work of **Alexander R. Luria**.

- Early formulation: 1975; further development in 1994.

- Major book: _Assessment of Cognitive Processes: The PASS Theory of Intelligence_ (1994).&#x20;

- PASS stands for **Planning, Attention, Simultaneous and Successive processing**.

## **2. Four Components of PASS Theory**

| Component                      | Meaning                                                                                 | Example                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Planning**                | Selecting strategies, monitoring and evaluating performance to achieve a goal.          | Planning how to solve a difficult problem.                                |
| **2. Attention**               | Focusing and maintaining attention on relevant information while ignoring distractions. | Concentrating on a question during an examination.                        |
| **3. Simultaneous Processing** | Integrating separate pieces of information into a meaningful whole.                     | Understanding a diagram or geometric relationship.                        |
| **4. Successive Processing**   | Processing information in a specific sequence or order.                                 | Remembering a sequence of numbers or following instructions step by step. |

## **3. Explanation of the Four Processes**

### **A. Planning**

- Involves decision-making and problem-solving.
- Includes strategy selection, self-monitoring and self-correction.
- Helps achieve a particular goal.

### **B. Attention**

- Involves selective and sustained attention.
- Helps focus on important stimuli.
- Prevents distraction from irrelevant information.

### **C. Simultaneous Processing**

- Combines different elements into a unified whole.
- Involves spatial and logical relationships.
- Example: Understanding the relationship between parts of a picture.

### **D. Successive Processing**

- Involves arranging information in a particular sequence.
- Important for language and sequential memory.
- Example: Repeating words or numbers in the correct order.

## **4. Theoretical Foundation: Alexander Luria**

PASS theory is based on **Alexander Luria's neuropsychological model of brain functioning**.

Luria proposed three functional units:

| Functional unit | Main function                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| First unit      | Arousal and attention                         |
| Second unit     | Receiving, analysing and storing information  |
| Third unit      | Planning, regulating and monitoring behaviour |

These functional units provide the neurological foundation for the four PASS cognitive processes.

## **5. Cognitive Assessment System (CAS)**

- Developed by **Jack A. Naglieri and J. P. Das (1997)**.
- Based on PASS theory.
- Designed primarily for children aged **5–17 years**.
- Measures all four cognitive processes:
  1. Planning
  2. Attention
  3. Simultaneous processing
  4. Successive processing
- Used to identify cognitive strengths and weaknesses and support educational intervention.&#x20;

## **6. Strengths and Criticisms**

**Strengths**

- Connects intelligence with brain functioning and cognitive processes.
- Goes beyond traditional IQ-based assessment.
- Helps identify individual cognitive strengths and weaknesses.
- Useful in understanding learning difficulties and planning interventions.

**Criticisms**

- Assessment requires specialised testing.
- Cognitive processes may overlap.
- The model does not capture every aspect of intelligence.
- Its assessment and interpretation require trained professionals.

## **8. Common MCQ Traps**

- **Simultaneous processing:** Integrating information into a whole.
- **Successive processing:** Processing information in a sequence.
- **Planning:** Strategy selection, monitoring and self-correction.
- **Attention:** Selective focus on relevant information.
- **Luria:** Neuropsychological foundation of PASS.
- **CAS:** Assessment instrument based on PASS theory.
- Do not confuse PASS theory with Sternberg's Triarchic Theory.

## **9. Last-Minute Revision**

Remember **P–A–S–S**:

- **P** – Planning: How to solve?
- **A** – Attention: What to focus on?
- **S** – Simultaneous: How do parts fit together?
- **S** – Successive: What comes next?

**One-line summary:** J. P. Das's PASS theory explains intelligence through four interrelated cognitive processes: planning, attention, simultaneous processing and successive processing.
