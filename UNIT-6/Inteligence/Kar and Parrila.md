# **Das, Kar and Parrila – PASS Model of Intelligence**

## **1. Introduction**

- **J. P. Das, B. C. Kar and Rauno K. Parrila** contributed to the development and elaboration of the **PASS Theory of Intelligence**.
- PASS stands for **Planning, Attention-Arousal, Simultaneous and Successive Processing**.
- The theory is based on the neuropsychological model of **Alexander R. Luria**.
- It views intelligence as a combination of interrelated cognitive processes rather than simply a single IQ score.
- Important work: _Cognitive Planning_ (1996), by Das, Kar and Parrila.&#x20;

## **2. Four Cognitive Processes**

| Process                     | Meaning                                                      | Example                                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Planning**                | Formulating strategies, monitoring and evaluating actions.   | Planning how to solve a problem.                 |
| **Attention-Arousal**       | Maintaining focus and alertness while ignoring distractions. | Concentrating during an examination.             |
| **Simultaneous Processing** | Integrating separate information into a meaningful whole.    | Understanding a diagram or spatial relationship. |
| **Successive Processing**   | Processing information in a specific sequence.               | Remembering a sequence of words or numbers.      |

## **3. Three Functional Units of the Brain**

Based on Luria's neuropsychological framework:

| Functional unit | Main function                                 | Related PASS processes                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **First unit**  | Arousal and attention                         | Attention-Arousal                      |
| **Second unit** | Receiving, analysing and storing information  | Simultaneous and Successive Processing |
| **Third unit**  | Planning, regulating and monitoring behaviour | Planning                               |

**Important:** The four PASS processes are interrelated and work together during cognitive activities.

## **4. Development of PASS Theory**

| Year     | Contribution                                                     |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1975** | Initial formulation by Das, Kirby and Jarman.                    |
| **1979** | Das, Kirby and Jarman further presented the model.               |
| **1994** | Elaborated by Das, Naglieri and Kirby.                           |
| **1996** | Further contribution by Das, Kar and Parrila.                    |
| **1997** | Cognitive Assessment System (CAS) developed by Das and Naglieri. |

## **5. Cognitive Assessment System (CAS)**

- Based on PASS theory.
- Developed by **J. P. Das and Jack A. Naglieri**.
- Published in 1997.
- Assesses four cognitive processes: Planning, Attention, Simultaneous and Successive processing.
- Helps identify cognitive strengths and weaknesses, particularly in children.

## **6. Strengths and Criticisms**

**Strengths**

- Based on neuropsychological research.
- Explains intelligence through cognitive processes.
- Useful in identifying learning difficulties.
- Provides an alternative to conventional IQ-based assessment.

**Criticisms**

- Cognitive processes may overlap.
- Assessment requires specialised instruments.
- Does not cover every dimension of intelligence.
