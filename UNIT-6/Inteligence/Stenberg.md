# **Robert Sternberg’s Triarchic Theory of Intelligence**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Robert J. Sternberg (born 1949)** – American psychologist.
- Proposed the **Triarchic Theory of Intelligence (1985)**.
- Published in _Beyond IQ: A Triarchic Theory of Human Intelligence_ (1985).
- Expanded the understanding of intelligence beyond traditional IQ and academic abilities.
- Intelligence involves adapting to, shaping and selecting environments.

## **2. Three Types of Intelligence**

| Type           | Meaning                                                      | Key abilities                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Analytical** | Ability to analyse, evaluate and solve problems              | Comparing, judging, analysing, logical reasoning |
| **Creative**   | Ability to deal with new situations and generate novel ideas | Innovation, imagination, problem-solving         |
| **Practical**  | Ability to apply knowledge in everyday situations            | Adaptation, application, common sense            |

### **A. Analytical Intelligence**

- Also called **componential intelligence**.
- Involves analysing information and evaluating ideas.
- Important for academic performance and conventional intelligence tests.
- Example: Solving mathematical problems or analysing an argument.

### **B. Creative Intelligence**

- Also called **experiential intelligence**.
- Involves dealing with novelty and automating familiar tasks.
- Example: Developing a new solution to an unfamiliar problem.

### **C. Practical Intelligence**

- Also called **contextual intelligence**.
- Involves adapting to, shaping and selecting environments.
- Example: Managing workplace problems using practical knowledge.

## **3. Three Subtheories**

| Subtheory    | Focus                     | Associated intelligence |
| ------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Componential | Internal mental processes | Analytical              |
| Experiential | Novelty and automation    | Creative                |
| Contextual   | Real-world adaptation     | Practical               |

**Important concepts:**

- **Adaptation:** Adjusting oneself to the environment.
- **Shaping:** Modifying the environment to suit one's needs.
- **Selection:** Choosing a different environment when the existing one is unsuitable.

## **4. Componential Intelligence: Three Components**

Sternberg identified three information-processing components:

1. **Metacomponents:** Higher-order processes used to plan, monitor and evaluate problem-solving.
2. **Performance components:** Processes that actually execute the task.
3. **Knowledge-acquisition components:** Processes involved in learning and acquiring new information.

## **5. Successful Intelligence**

- Sternberg later developed the idea of **Successful Intelligence**.
- It involves balancing analytical, creative and practical abilities to achieve personal goals.
- A successful person recognises strengths, compensates for weaknesses and adapts to the environment.

## **6. Strengths and Criticisms**

**Strengths**

- Goes beyond traditional IQ-based approaches.
- Recognises practical and creative abilities.
- Connects intelligence with everyday functioning.
- Has influenced educational approaches.

**Criticisms**

- Difficult to measure all three abilities reliably.
- Considerable overlap exists among the three types.
- Practical and creative intelligence lack universally accepted assessment methods.
- Some research questions whether these abilities are truly independent of general intelligence.

## **8. Common MCQ Traps**

- **Sternberg vs Gardner:** Sternberg proposed three types of intelligence; Gardner proposed Multiple Intelligences.
- **Analytical ≠ Practical:** Analytical involves evaluating and analysing; practical involves real-world application.
- **Creative intelligence:** Associated with novelty and automation.
- **Metacomponents:** Responsible for planning, monitoring and evaluating, not directly executing tasks.
- **Practical intelligence:** Also associated with the idea of _tacit knowledge_ — knowledge gained through experience that may not be explicitly taught.
