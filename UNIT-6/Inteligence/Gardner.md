# **Howard Gardner's Theory of Multiple Intelligences (1983)**

A concise, exam-oriented short note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)**, covering all important concepts without unnecessary details or images.

## **1. Introduction**

- **Howard Gardner (born 1943)** is an American developmental psychologist associated with Harvard University.
- Proposed the **Theory of Multiple Intelligences (MI) in 1983**.
- Published _Frames of Mind: The Theory of Multiple Intelligences_ (1983).
- Challenged the traditional view that intelligence is a single general ability measured through IQ tests.
- According to Gardner, intelligence consists of multiple relatively independent intellectual capacities.
- Individuals possess different combinations and strengths of intelligences.

## **2. Eight Types of Intelligence**

Gardner initially proposed seven intelligences in 1983 and later added naturalistic intelligence.

| No. | Intelligence         | Meaning                                                         | Examples                           |
| --- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 1   | Linguistic           | Ability to use language effectively                             | Writers, poets, journalists        |
| 2   | Logical-Mathematical | Logical reasoning, calculations and problem-solving             | Scientists, mathematicians         |
| 3   | Spatial              | Visualizing and manipulating spatial relationships              | Architects, pilots, artists        |
| 4   | Musical              | Recognizing and creating musical patterns, rhythm and melody    | Singers, composers                 |
| 5   | Bodily-Kinesthetic   | Using the body skillfully for expression and activities         | Dancers, athletes, surgeons        |
| 6   | Interpersonal        | Understanding other people's emotions, intentions and behaviour | Teachers, counsellors, leaders     |
| 7   | Intrapersonal        | Understanding one's own emotions, motives and feelings          | Reflective thinkers, philosophers  |
| 8   | Naturalistic         | Identifying and classifying elements of nature                  | Botanists, zoologists, naturalists |

**Important distinctions:**

- Interpersonal = Understanding others.
- Intrapersonal = Understanding oneself.
- Linguistic = Language ability.
- Logical-mathematical = Logical and numerical reasoning.

## **3. Naturalistic and Existential Intelligence**

**Naturalistic Intelligence:**

- Added in **1995**.
- Ability to recognize, classify and understand plants, animals and other elements of nature.

**Existential Intelligence:**

- Gardner discussed it as a possible ninth intelligence.
- Involves thinking about fundamental questions concerning life, death, existence and the meaning of life.
- It was not established as a fully accepted ninth intelligence in his framework.

**NET trap:** Original theory = 7; commonly recognized later framework = 8; existential = possible ninth.

## **4. Criteria for Identifying an Intelligence**

Gardner proposed several criteria for distinguishing intelligence from ordinary talents and abilities.

1. **Isolation by brain damage:** An ability may be impaired independently of other abilities.
2. **Savant syndrome and prodigies:** Exceptional performance in one area despite limitations in others.
3. **Identifiable core operation:** Each intelligence has distinctive basic operations.
4. **Distinctive developmental history:** Different intelligences follow different developmental patterns.
5. **Evolutionary history:** Each intelligence has a basis in human evolutionary development.
6. **Experimental evidence:** Psychological experiments support distinct abilities.
7. **Psychometric evidence:** Testing findings provide evidence of different abilities.
8. **Symbol systems:** Each intelligence can be expressed through culturally developed symbolic systems.

## **5. Important Concepts**

**A. Savant Syndrome**

- Individuals demonstrate exceptional abilities in particular areas despite limitations in other areas of functioning.
- Examples include extraordinary musical memory, artistic ability and mathematical calculation.
- Gardner considered savants and prodigies important evidence when examining the relative independence of intelligences.

**B. Neuropsychological Evidence**

- Brain damage may selectively affect particular intellectual capacities.
- Different abilities may involve different brain systems.
- Gardner used such evidence to support the idea of multiple intelligences.

## **6. Educational Applications**

Gardner's theory influenced educational practices by emphasizing individual differences.

- **Linguistic:** Reading, writing and storytelling.
- **Logical-mathematical:** Problem-solving and numerical exercises.
- **Spatial:** Diagrams, maps and visual learning.
- **Musical:** Songs, rhythm and musical activities.
- **Bodily-kinesthetic:** Demonstrations and role-play.
- **Interpersonal:** Group discussions and collaborative learning.
- **Intrapersonal:** Self-reflection and personal journals.
- **Naturalistic:** Nature observation and classification.

**Main educational implication:** Teachers should recognize different intellectual strengths and provide diverse learning and assessment opportunities.

However, the claim that matching teaching methods to a student's supposed intelligence type improves learning has not received strong empirical support.

## **7. Strengths and Criticisms**

| Strengths                                       | Criticisms                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Broadens the concept of intelligence beyond IQ. | Limited empirical evidence for eight independent intelligences.                    |
| Recognizes individual differences.              | Some intelligences overlap with existing cognitive abilities.                      |
| Encourages diverse teaching methods.            | May confuse intelligence with talent or specialized skills.                        |
| Considers cultural and developmental factors.   | Lacks universally accepted standardized measurement.                               |
| Recognizes neuropsychological evidence.         | Psychometric research supports substantial correlations among cognitive abilities. |
