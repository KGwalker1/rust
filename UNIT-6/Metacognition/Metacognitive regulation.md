# **Metacognitive Regulation**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Metacognitive regulation** refers to the ability to control, monitor and regulate one's own cognitive processes during learning and problem-solving.
- It is an important component of **metacognition**, a concept developed by **John H. Flavell (1976, 1979)**.
- It helps individuals plan learning activities, monitor understanding and evaluate learning outcomes.
- **Ann Brown (1987)** made important contributions to understanding metacognitive regulation in learning.

## **2. Three Major Components of Metacognitive Regulation**

| Component         | Meaning                                                                                  | Example                                                       |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Planning**   | Deciding learning goals, strategies and resources before starting a task.                | Preparing a study timetable before an examination.            |
| **2. Monitoring** | Checking and observing one's understanding and progress during a task.                   | Identifying which concepts are not understood while studying. |
| **3. Evaluation** | Assessing the effectiveness of strategies and learning outcomes after completing a task. | Checking examination performance and identifying mistakes.    |

### **A. Planning**

- Setting learning objectives.
- Selecting appropriate strategies.
- Allocating time and resources.
- Predicting task difficulty.

### **B. Monitoring**

- Checking comprehension during learning.
- Identifying errors and confusion.
- Maintaining awareness of progress.
- Changing strategies when necessary.

### **C. Evaluation**

- Assessing whether learning goals were achieved.
- Judging the effectiveness of strategies.
- Identifying strengths and weaknesses.
- Planning improvements for future learning.

## **3. Metacognitive Regulation vs Metacognitive Knowledge**

| Metacognitive Knowledge                            | Metacognitive Regulation                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Knowing about one's cognitive processes.           | Controlling cognitive processes.                                          |
| Awareness of learning abilities and strategies.    | Planning, monitoring and evaluating learning.                             |
| What do I know?                                    | How am I managing my learning?                                            |
| Example: Knowing that summarising improves memory. | Example: Using summaries and checking whether they improve understanding. |

## **4. Important Researchers**

| Researcher                       | Contribution                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **John H. Flavell (1976, 1979)** | Developed the concept of metacognition.                              |
| **Ann Brown (1987)**             | Emphasised metacognitive regulation in learning and problem-solving. |
| **Nelson and Narens (1990)**     | Explained metacognition through monitoring and control processes.    |
| **Schraw and Dennison (1994)**   | Developed the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory (MAI).               |

## **5. Importance of Metacognitive Regulation**

- Improves self-directed learning.
- Enhances problem-solving and decision-making.
- Helps identify errors and misunderstandings.
- Improves academic performance.
- Develops independent learning skills.
- Encourages effective use of learning strategies.

## **7. Common MCQ Traps**

- **Planning:** Occurs before or at the beginning of a task.
- **Monitoring:** Occurs during task performance.
- **Evaluation:** Generally occurs after task completion.
- **Flavell:** Associated with the development of metacognition.
- **Ann Brown:** Important contributor to metacognitive regulation.
- **Schraw and Dennison:** Associated with the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory (MAI).
- Metacognitive regulation is different from metacognitive knowledge: regulation involves controlling cognitive activities, not simply knowing about them.

## **8. Last-Minute Revision**

Remember **P–M–E**:

- **P – Planning:** What should I do?
- **M – Monitoring:** Am I doing it correctly?
- **E – Evaluation:** Did it work?

**One-line summary:** Metacognitive regulation is the process of controlling one's learning through planning, monitoring and evaluating cognitive activities.
