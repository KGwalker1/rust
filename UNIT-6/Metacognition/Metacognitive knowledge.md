# **Metacognitive Knowledge**

## **1. Introduction**

- **Metacognition** refers to thinking about one's own thinking.
- The term was introduced and developed by **John H. Flavell (1976, 1979)**.
- It involves awareness, understanding and regulation of one's cognitive processes.
- **Metacognitive knowledge** refers to what an individual knows about their own thinking, learning and cognitive processes.

## **2. Types of Metacognitive Knowledge**

According to Flavell (1979), metacognitive knowledge has three major components:

| Type                      | Meaning                                                               | Example                                                                           |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Person knowledge**   | Knowledge about oneself and other people's cognitive abilities.       | Knowing that you learn better through diagrams.                                   |
| **2. Task knowledge**     | Knowledge about the nature, difficulty and demands of a task.         | Knowing that essay writing requires more preparation than memorising definitions. |
| **3. Strategy knowledge** | Knowledge about strategies that help in learning and problem-solving. | Knowing that summarising and revision improve memory.                             |

### **A. Person Knowledge**

- Awareness of one's strengths and weaknesses.
- Includes knowledge of individual differences in learning.
- Example: Knowing that you have difficulty concentrating in noisy environments.

### **B. Task Knowledge**

- Understanding the nature and requirements of a task.
- Recognising which tasks are easy or difficult.
- Example: Understanding that solving a complex mathematical problem requires more time.

### **C. Strategy Knowledge**

- Knowing different cognitive strategies and when to use them.
- Example: Using mnemonics to remember information and concept maps to understand relationships.

## **3. Metacognitive Experiences**

According to Flavell, metacognitive experiences are conscious experiences related to ongoing cognitive activities.

Examples:

- Feeling that a topic is difficult to understand.
- Realising that you have forgotten an important concept.
- Feeling confident about an answer.

## **4. Metacognitive Knowledge vs Metacognitive Regulation**

| Metacognitive Knowledge           | Metacognitive Regulation                      |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Knowing about one's thinking.     | Controlling one's thinking.                   |
| Awareness of cognitive processes. | Monitoring and modifying cognitive processes. |
| Knowing which strategy is useful. | Deciding when and how to use the strategy.    |

**Three important regulatory processes:**

1. **Planning:** Selecting strategies and setting goals before learning.
2. **Monitoring:** Checking understanding during learning.
3. **Evaluation:** Assessing learning outcomes after completing a task.

## **5. Important Researchers**

| Researcher                       | Contribution                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **John H. Flavell (1976, 1979)** | Developed the concept of metacognition and its knowledge components. |
| **Ann Brown (1987)**             | Emphasised metacognitive regulation in learning and problem-solving. |
| **Nelson and Narens (1990)**     | Explained metacognition through monitoring and control processes.    |

## **6. Importance of Metacognitive Knowledge**

- Improves self-directed learning.
- Helps in effective problem-solving.
- Enhances memory and comprehension.
- Helps identify learning difficulties.
- Encourages independent learning and self-assessment.

- **Task knowledge:** Knowledge about task requirements.
- **Strategy knowledge:** Knowledge about learning strategies.
- **Metacognitive knowledge ≠ Metacognitive regulation:** Knowing versus controlling.
- **Flavell:** Associated with the development of metacognition.
- **Brown:** Particularly associated with metacognitive regulation in learning.
