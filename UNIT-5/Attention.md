# **Attention: Forms of Attention and Models of Attention**

A concise, exam-oriented short note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)**, covering important concepts, psychologists, theoretical models, comparisons, and MCQ traps.

# **PART 1: FORMS OF ATTENTION**

## **1. Meaning of Attention**

Attention is a cognitive process through which an individual selectively concentrates mental resources on particular stimuli or activities while ignoring other competing stimuli.

- Attention has a limited capacity.
- It helps in selecting relevant information.
- It facilitates perception, learning, memory and problem-solving.
- Attention can be voluntary or involuntary.

## **2. Types / Forms of Attention**

### **1. Selective Attention**

The ability to focus on one particular stimulus while ignoring irrelevant or distracting stimuli.

**Example:** Listening to a teacher in a noisy classroom.

- Involves filtering of information.
- Helps prevent information overload.
- Associated with Broadbent's Filter Model, Treisman's Attenuation Model and Deutsch and Deutsch's Late Selection Model.

### **2. Sustained Attention (Vigilance)**

The ability to maintain attention on a particular task or stimulus over an extended period.

**Example:** A security guard monitoring CCTV cameras for several hours.

- Important for continuous monitoring.
- Performance may decline with time due to vigilance decrement.
- Associated with signal detection and vigilance research.

### **3. Divided Attention**

The ability to distribute attention between two or more tasks performed simultaneously.

**Example:** Driving while listening to the radio.

- Depends on task difficulty and cognitive resources.
- Performance decreases when tasks require similar cognitive resources.
- Divided attention is not always equally efficient across tasks.

### **4. Alternating Attention**

The ability to shift attention flexibly between different tasks or activities.

**Example:** Switching between reading a textbook and solving a mathematical problem.

- Requires mental flexibility.
- Involves shifting cognitive focus.
- Different from divided attention because tasks are performed alternately rather than simultaneously.

### **5. Focused Attention**

The ability to respond directly to a specific stimulus.

**Example:** Responding immediately when someone calls your name.

- Involves detecting and responding to particular sensory information.
- Can involve visual, auditory or tactile attention.

### **Comparison of Forms of Attention**

| Form        | Main characteristic                        |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Focused     | Responding to a particular stimulus        |
| Selective   | Choosing relevant information              |
| Sustained   | Maintaining attention over time            |
| Divided     | Attending to multiple tasks simultaneously |
| Alternating | Shifting attention between tasks           |

NET Important

&#x20;Remember the distinction between selective, divided and alternating attention. These are frequently confused in conceptual questions.

# **PART 2: MODELS OF ATTENTION**

Models of attention explain how humans select, process and prioritize information from the environment.

The major models can be classified into:

1. Early Selection Models
2. Attenuation Model
3. Late Selection Models
4. Capacity Models
5. Feature Integration Theory
6. Spotlight and Zoom-Lens Models

## **1. Broadbent's Filter Model (1958)**

EARLY SELECTION THEORY

[Mind Changers – Donald Broadbent remembered](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/pOYaJ_BL2HP_IK7xxV1p_qWXJaC8-WDGdFlSE8OxUuJAsM0T70W2i2l83CIC-n6r5vUrRw0DVXWg8-HyhGx-WvWsLr2VP7Kn0gqE1S1fas7knxwXPaaoEIpT62DjQLL8Dy2a8aamZbWaZIQb-u-gu1djR5Ou1AiEYcfHUka6B9g?purpose=inline)

**Donald E. Broadbent**

Proposed the Filter Model of Attention in 1958.

- Attention acts as an early filter that selects information based on physical characteristics.
- Information initially enters a sensory register.
- The filter selects one channel for further processing.
- Selected information enters the limited-capacity short-term processing system.
- Unattended information is largely prevented from semantic processing.

**Stages:**

Sensory Register → Selective Filter → Limited-Capacity Channel → Short-Term Memory

**Important experiment:** Dichotic listening experiment.

**Limitation:** Cannot adequately explain the Cocktail Party Effect, where a person notices their name even when attending to another conversation.

## **2. Treisman's Attenuation Model (1964)**

MODIFIED EARLY SELECTION THEORY

[In Memoriam: Anne Treisman (1935 - 2018) | Psychology](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/dW3lYxYxswIph0UBu7ckdqpGX_-w-jPCEA70meMg1MhHuVzVv5R0oXGvXfNJcMG5kkRPi53YY1ZGgpYyQe5tBTa9pDZde95iqxYB-3wVw1LSoBnaEhrZgFWh5fEGEbk2wzJZY6MknM07f7uobbMlUCmyiTmZKa6erI6Fb_Z9-oA?purpose=inline)

**Anne Treisman**

Proposed the Attenuation Model in 1964.

- Treisman modified Broadbent's Filter Model.
- Instead of completely blocking unattended information, the filter weakens or attenuates it.
- Attended information receives full processing.
- Unattended information receives weaker processing.
- Important information with a low recognition threshold, such as one's name, may still be detected.

**Key concept: Attenuator**

The attenuator reduces the strength of unattended messages rather than completely eliminating them.

**Important:** Treisman's model explains the Cocktail Party Effect better than Broadbent's original model.

## **3. Deutsch and Deutsch's Late Selection Model (1963)**

LATE SELECTION THEORY

**Alvin and Diana Deutsch**

Proposed the Late Selection Model in 1963.

- All incoming stimuli undergo perceptual and semantic analysis.
- Selection occurs after meaning has been processed.
- Only selected information enters conscious awareness or controls the response.
- Unattended information can be processed for meaning without necessarily reaching consciousness.

**Central assumption:** Selection occurs late, after semantic processing.

## **4. Norman's Pertinence Model (1968)**

LATE SELECTION APPROACH

[Photographs suitable for publication – Don Norman's JND.org](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/QAcDn8Onq2xA6AdpMT691VrHFXXd4hViB6Wy2zdn8uKSJ--TV8QfkYSt-VWDC-mNU0jBX2bmcxsdL6ttb_B5tgR0ipJ-ikTW1-6KmtfRZHQcCvrOGM_PcGSzTKvvbbruVv8P1Dl0X1-yOAv3Ce2dN4xkltFGPBedD9feFVWhUy0?purpose=inline)

**Donald Norman**

Developed the Pertinence Model of Attention.

- All stimuli receive some degree of perceptual processing.
- Selection depends on both physical input and the importance or relevance of information.
- Information relevant to current goals and expectations receives priority.
- Meaning and pertinence influence which information enters awareness.

**Key concept:** Pertinence refers to the importance or relevance of a stimulus to the individual.

## **5. Kahneman's Capacity Model of Attention (1973)**

CAPACITY / RESOURCE MODEL

[Daniel Kahneman - Professor Emeritus @ Princeton University](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/hHXZm5sVY4LItzKyc9YC0YIVs28wAm7q5rJOcrb9gUr7aIJq8L2PIv-XGaBU55Fr4w9XaeZFJgGUwsJKa86DreVTEyHc7aINwOZLEtXdYG7yPDnABjB9ynmyw2MY5GJ8kIZggKTlZQikv5yzAcojCaByr15QM2umAO7siR5ENoY?purpose=inline)

**Daniel Kahneman**

Proposed the Capacity Model in 1973.

- Attention is considered a limited-capacity mental resource.
- Available attentional capacity can be distributed across different activities.
- Allocation depends on arousal, intentions and task demands.
- Difficult tasks consume more attentional resources than easy tasks.
- When task demands exceed available capacity, performance deteriorates.

**Important concepts:**

- Arousal: Determines the available attentional capacity.
- Allocation policy: Determines how attention is distributed.
- Enduring dispositions: Automatic tendencies to attend to certain stimuli.
- Momentary intentions: Temporary goals influencing attention.

**Example:** Driving in heavy traffic requires more attentional resources than driving on an empty road.

## **6. Treisman's Feature Integration Theory (1980)**

VISUAL ATTENTION THEORY

[In Memoriam: Anne Treisman (1935 - 2018) | Psychology](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/dW3lYxYxswIph0UBu7ckdqpGX_-w-jPCEA70meMg1MhHuVzVv5R0oXGvXfNJcMG5kkRPi53YY1ZGgpYyQe5tBTa9pDZde95iqxYB-3wVw1LSoBnaEhrZgFWh5fEGEbk2wzJZY6MknM07f7uobbMlUCmyiTmZKa6erI6Fb_Z9-oA?purpose=inline)

**Anne Treisman and Garry Gelade**

Proposed Feature Integration Theory in 1980.

The theory explains how different visual features are combined to form a unified perception of objects.

**Two stages:**

1\. **Preattentive Stage**

- Occurs automatically and rapidly.
- Features such as colour, shape, orientation and size are processed separately.
- Does not require focused attention.

2\. **Focused Attention Stage**

- Attention combines different features into a unified object representation.
- Helps identify objects accurately.
- Feature binding errors may occur when attention is insufficient.

**Important concepts:**

- Feature maps: Represent individual visual features.
- Master map of locations: Represents where features occur.
- Illusory conjunctions: Incorrect combinations of features.

**Example:** Seeing a red circle and a blue square but mistakenly reporting a blue circle.

## **7. Spotlight Model of Attention**

SPATIAL ATTENTION MODEL

- Attention operates like a spotlight that illuminates a particular region of the visual field.
- Information within the attended region is processed more efficiently.
- Processing outside the spotlight is comparatively weaker.
- Attention can move from one spatial location to another.

**Associated researcher:** Michael Posner.

**Key concept:** Spatial attention.

## **8. Zoom-Lens Model of Attention**

- Proposed by Eriksen and St. James (1986).
- Attention works like a zoom lens.
- The size of the attended region can expand or contract.
- A narrow attentional focus provides more resources to a smaller area.
- A wider focus covers a larger area but distributes resources more broadly.

**Example:** Focusing closely on one word while reading versus scanning an entire paragraph.

## **Early Selection vs Late Selection**

| Early Selection                                                                     | Late Selection                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Selection occurs early in processing.                                               | Selection occurs after substantial perceptual processing.  |
| Based mainly on physical characteristics in Broadbent's model.                      | Based on meaning and relevance in late selection accounts. |
| Unattended information receives limited semantic processing in Broadbent's account. | Unattended information receives semantic processing.       |
| Broadbent, Treisman                                                                 | Deutsch & Deutsch, Norman                                  |

## **Common MCQ Traps**

1. Broadbent proposed an **early selection model**, not a late selection model.
2. Treisman's attenuation model does not completely block unattended information.
3. Deutsch and Deutsch proposed that selection occurs after semantic analysis.
4. Kahneman viewed attention as a limited-capacity resource, not an unlimited resource.
5. Treisman and Gelade introduced Feature Integration Theory, not Broadbent.
6. Illusory conjunctions are associated with Feature Integration Theory.
7. Vigilance refers to sustained attention, not divided attention.
8. Alternating attention involves switching tasks, whereas divided attention involves simultaneous tasks.
9. Norman's Pertinence Model emphasizes the relevance of information.
10. Posner is associated with spatial attention and the spotlight metaphor.
