# **Perceptual Constancy: Size, Shape, and Colour; Illusions**

A concise, exam-oriented short note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)** covering the meaning, types, mechanisms, important theories, examples of perceptual constancy and major visual illusions.

# **PART 1: PERCEPTUAL CONSTANCY**

## **1. Meaning of Perceptual Constancy**

Perceptual constancy refers to the tendency to perceive objects as relatively stable in size, shape, colour and other characteristics despite changes in sensory information caused by variations in viewing conditions.

- Objects may produce different retinal images depending on distance, angle and illumination.
- However, our perception of their physical characteristics generally remains stable.
- It helps individuals recognize objects under different environmental conditions.
- It depends on previous experience, context and perceptual interpretation.

**Example:** A person walking away from us produces a smaller retinal image, but we do not perceive the person as physically shrinking.

## **2. Major Types of Perceptual Constancy**

The three major forms are:

1. Size Constancy
2. Shape Constancy
3. Colour Constancy

Other forms include brightness, lightness and location constancy.

# **PART 2: SIZE CONSTANCY**

## **1. Meaning**

Size constancy is the tendency to perceive an object as maintaining its actual physical size even when its retinal image changes because of variations in viewing distance.

**Example:** A car moving away from us appears smaller on the retina, but we perceive it as approximately the same size.

## **2. Mechanism of Size Constancy**

- The size of an object's retinal image decreases as its distance from the observer increases.
- The brain uses distance information to interpret the retinal image.
- The perceived size remains relatively stable despite changes in retinal image size.

### **Important Formula**

According to the visual angle relationship:

\\[ \theta \approx \frac{S}{D} \\]

Where:

- \\(\theta\\) = Visual angle (in radians, approximately)
- S = Physical size of the object
- D = Distance from the observer

The same physical object produces a smaller visual angle when its distance increases.

### **Emmert's Law (1881)**

**Emmert's Law**

Associated with German psychologist Emil Emmert.

- The perceived size of an afterimage increases with the perceived distance of the surface on which it is projected.
- When an afterimage is projected onto a distant wall, it appears larger.
- When projected onto a nearby surface, it appears smaller.

**NET Point:** Emmert's Law explains the relationship between perceived distance and afterimage size.

# **PART 3: SHAPE CONSTANCY**

## **1. Meaning**

Shape constancy refers to the tendency to perceive an object as maintaining its actual shape despite changes in the shape of its retinal image caused by changes in viewing angle.

**Example:** A circular plate viewed from an angle produces an elliptical retinal image, but we perceive it as circular.

## **2. Characteristics**

- The retinal image changes with the viewing angle.
- The perceived shape remains relatively constant.
- The brain uses information about the object's orientation and viewing conditions.
- Previous experience contributes to shape recognition.

**Example:** An open door viewed from different angles still appears rectangular, although its retinal projection changes.

**Important:** Shape constancy helps us recognize familiar objects from different viewing positions.

# **PART 4: COLOUR CONSTANCY**

## **1. Meaning**

Colour constancy is the tendency to perceive the colour of an object as relatively stable despite changes in the colour or intensity of illumination.

**Example:** A red apple generally appears red whether viewed under sunlight or ordinary indoor lighting.

## **2. Mechanism of Colour Constancy**

- The light reflected from an object changes depending on illumination.
- The visual system processes information about surrounding objects and lighting conditions.
- The brain estimates the illumination and compensates for its effects.
- This allows relatively stable colour perception.

### **Important Concepts**

**1. Chromatic Adaptation**

- The visual system adjusts to changes in the colour of illumination.
- It helps maintain relatively stable colour perception.

**2. Colour Context**

- Surrounding colours influence how an object is perceived.
- The same colour may appear different depending on its background.

**3. Retinex Theory – Edwin Land**

[Edwin Land and Instant Photography - American Chemical Society](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/p2w0vsrm1UhJPc0Qulk0h2feEnENNEZxjdVHwlmqQ9NxJEghVsMPaaAi7xFhX9NVsMnYA4wolCGXZ2Eem4OBGxQb6KTmpL7o8aU2pG2j4pDZFtseS8Mzgt3yT-akOoJc9EdKCVk3dv1V0Gn4zdRdGZpDhsYuJWCXzBeQpaVB_VY?purpose=inline)

**Edwin H. Land**

Developed Retinex theory of colour vision.

- Retinex is derived from the words _retina_ and _cortex_.
- The theory explains colour constancy through comparisons of light reflected from different regions of a visual scene.
- The visual system uses spatial comparisons to estimate surface reflectance rather than relying only on the wavelength reaching the eye.
- It explains why objects maintain relatively stable perceived colours under different lighting conditions.

**NET Point:** Edwin Land is associated with Retinex Theory.

# **PART 5: COMPARISON OF PERCEPTUAL CONSTANCIES**

| Type                 | What remains constant?      | What changes?                    |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Size Constancy       | Physical size               | Retinal image size               |
| Shape Constancy      | Actual shape                | Retinal image shape              |
| Colour Constancy     | Perceived surface colour    | Illumination                     |
| Brightness Constancy | Perceived brightness        | Amount of illumination           |
| Lightness Constancy  | Perceived surface lightness | Lighting and surrounding context |

### **Important Distinction: Brightness vs Lightness Constancy**

- **Brightness:** Perceived intensity of light coming from a source or surface.
- **Lightness:** Perceived reflectance or relative lightness of a surface.

Do not treat these two concepts as identical.

# **PART 6: PERCEPTUAL ILLUSIONS**

## **1. Meaning of Illusion**

A perceptual illusion occurs when the perceived characteristics of a stimulus differ systematically from its actual physical characteristics.

- It involves a misinterpretation of sensory information.
- It demonstrates that perception is not always an accurate representation of physical reality.
- Illusions help psychologists understand the mechanisms and limitations of perception.
- They may arise due to contextual influences, perceptual organization, previous experience and physiological factors.

**Example:** Two lines of equal physical length may appear different in length because of surrounding arrowheads.

### **B. Apparent Motion Illusions**

#### **1. Phi Phenomenon**

- Discovered by Max Wertheimer in 1912.
- Two stationary lights presented successively at an appropriate interval create an illusion of movement.
- It demonstrates apparent motion without actual movement of the stimulus.

#### **2. Beta Movement**

- A sequence of stationary images presented rapidly appears as continuous movement.
- It is the perceptual principle underlying motion pictures and animation.

**Important distinction:**

| Phi Phenomenon                                          | Beta Movement                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Apparent movement between successive stationary stimuli | Perception of an object moving between successive positions |
| Associated with Wertheimer                              | Associated with Wertheimer                                  |
| Important in Gestalt psychology                         | Important in understanding apparent motion and cinema       |

### **D. Illusions of Depth and Size**

#### **Moon Illusion**

- The moon appears larger near the horizon than when it is high in the sky.
- Its visual angle remains approximately the same.
- Explanations include apparent-distance and contextual interpretations, though no single explanation fully accounts for the phenomenon.

**NET Point:** The Moon Illusion is an example of apparent size differences without corresponding changes in the moon's retinal angular size.

# **PART 7: IMPORTANT DIFFERENCES**

## **Perceptual Constancy vs Perceptual Illusion**

| Perceptual Constancy                              | Perceptual Illusion                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Maintains perceptual stability                    | Produces systematic perceptual distortion             |
| Helps recognize objects under changing conditions | Shows how perception can differ from physical reality |
| Example: Size constancy                           | Example: Müller-Lyer illusion                         |
| Generally adaptive                                | Demonstrates limitations of perception                |

## **Illusion vs Hallucination**

| Illusion                                         | Hallucination                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Misinterpretation of an actual external stimulus | Perception-like experience without an appropriate external stimulus |
| External stimulus is present                     | No corresponding external stimulus                                  |
| Example: A rope perceived as a snake             | Hearing a voice when nobody is speaking                             |

## **Quick Revision: Three Core Concepts**

**Perceptual Constancy**

We perceive objects as relatively stable despite changing sensory conditions.

- Size
- Shape
- Colour

**Perceptual Organization**

The brain organizes sensory elements into meaningful patterns.

- Gestalt principles
- Figure-ground
- Laws of grouping

**Perceptual Illusions**

Perception systematically differs from the physical characteristics of a stimulus.

- Geometrical illusions
- Apparent motion
- Ambiguous figures
- Depth and size illusions
