# **Role of Motivation and Learning in Perception**

An exam-oriented short note for **UGC-NET JRF Psychology (December 2026)**.

## **1. Introduction**

Perception is not merely the passive reception of sensory information. It is an **active process** influenced by internal factors such as motivation, needs, expectations, past experiences and learning.

- **Motivation** influences what we attend to and how we interpret sensory information.
- **Learning** influences perception by shaping our expectations, recognition, interpretation and understanding of the environment.

Thus, perception is influenced by both present psychological needs and past experiences.

## **2. Role of Motivation in Perception**

Motivation refers to internal processes that initiate, direct and maintain goal-oriented behaviour. It influences perception by determining which stimuli become important to an individual.

**Major effects of motivation:**

1. **Selective perception:** Individuals tend to notice stimuli related to their current needs and goals.
2. **Perceptual readiness:** A motivated person becomes more prepared to perceive particular stimuli.
3. **Perceptual distortion:** Strong needs and desires may influence how ambiguous stimuli are interpreted.
4. **Attention:** Motivationally relevant stimuli are more likely to attract attention.
5. **Interpretation:** The same stimulus may be interpreted differently depending on an individual's motives.

### **Important research and concepts**

**A. Bruner and Goodman (1947) – Value and Perception**

- Studied the influence of value on the perception of coin sizes.
- Children from different socioeconomic backgrounds estimated the sizes of coins.
- Valuable coins were generally perceived as larger than their actual size.
- Suggested that psychological value and needs can influence perception.

**B. Bruner and Postman (1949) – Perceptual Defense**

- Demonstrated that emotionally threatening or unacceptable stimuli may be difficult to recognize.
- Individuals may unconsciously resist perceiving stimuli that produce anxiety or psychological discomfort.
- This is associated with the concept of **perceptual defense**.

**C. Perceptual Vigilance**

- Individuals are more likely to detect stimuli related to their needs, interests and expectations.
- Example: A hungry person may notice food-related advertisements more readily than other advertisements.

**D. Perceptual Defense**

- Individuals may fail to recognize or may take longer to recognize threatening or unpleasant stimuli.
- It acts as a psychological protective mechanism.

### **Perceptual Vigilance vs Perceptual Defense**

| Perceptual vigilance                                     | Perceptual defense                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Increased readiness to perceive relevant stimuli         | Resistance to perceiving threatening stimuli                    |
| Motivationally important stimuli are noticed more easily | Emotionally threatening stimuli may be avoided or misidentified |
| Facilitates detection                                    | May delay or distort recognition                                |

## **3. Role of Learning in Perception**

Learning refers to a relatively permanent change in knowledge or behaviour resulting from experience. It influences perception by modifying how individuals organize, recognize and interpret sensory information.

**Major roles of learning:**

1. **Recognition:** Previous experience helps individuals identify familiar objects and patterns.
2. **Perceptual interpretation:** Learning provides meaning to sensory information.
3. **Perceptual expectations:** Past experiences create expectations that guide future perception.
4. **Perceptual organization:** Experience influences how individuals organize and interpret stimuli.
5. **Cultural influence:** Language, culture and social experiences influence how people perceive their surroundings.
6. **Perceptual development:** Practice and experience can improve perceptual discrimination and recognition.

### **Important Theories and Research**

**A. Empiricist View – Learning and Experience**

- Associated with philosophers such as John Locke and George Berkeley.
- Emphasizes the importance of experience in the development of perception.
- Perceptual knowledge is acquired through sensory experiences and learning.

**B. Nativist View – Innate Factors**

- Emphasizes the role of inborn abilities and inherited perceptual mechanisms.
- Some basic perceptual capacities are present from birth.
- Associated with thinkers such as Immanuel Kant and later perceptual-development researchers.

**C. Eleanor Gibson and James Gibson – Perceptual Learning**

- Emphasized that perceptual abilities improve through experience and practice.
- Perceptual learning involves becoming increasingly sensitive to important features and differences in the environment.
- It helps individuals distinguish meaningful information from irrelevant details.

**D. Eleanor Gibson (1969) – Principles of Perceptual Learning**

- Perceptual learning involves improved differentiation of sensory information.
- Experience helps individuals detect distinctive features of stimuli.
- It is important in reading, visual recognition and skill development.

**E. Hubel and Wiesel – Feature Detection**

- Demonstrated that neurons in the visual cortex respond selectively to particular stimulus features, such as orientation.
- Their work highlights the biological mechanisms underlying visual perception.
- Their research is important for understanding the interaction between sensory mechanisms and perceptual experience.

## **4. Influence of Motivation and Learning: Comparison**

| Motivation                                            | Learning                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Influences perception through current needs and goals | Influences perception through past experiences |
| Determines what is important to notice                | Helps interpret and recognize stimuli          |
| Can produce perceptual vigilance and defense          | Improves perceptual discrimination             |
| Primarily goal-related                                | Primarily experience-related                   |
| Example: A hungry person notices food                 | Example: Recognizing a familiar face           |

## **5. Important NET/JRF Points**

- **Bruner and Goodman (1947):** Value influences perceived size of coins.
- **Bruner and Postman (1949):** Associated with perceptual defense and emotionally significant stimuli.
- **Perceptual vigilance:** Increased sensitivity to need-relevant stimuli.
- **Perceptual defense:** Resistance or delayed recognition of threatening stimuli.
- **Empiricist approach:** Emphasizes experience and learning in perception.
- **Nativist approach:** Emphasizes innate perceptual capacities.
- **Eleanor Gibson:** Important contributor to perceptual learning.
- **Hubel and Wiesel:** Feature detectors in the visual cortex.

**Central idea:** Motivation influences _what we are prepared to perceive_, whereas learning influences _how we recognize and interpret what we perceive_. Both interact with sensory and biological processes to shape perceptual experience.
