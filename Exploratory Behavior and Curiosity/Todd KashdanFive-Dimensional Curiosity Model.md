Kashdan's Five-Dimensional Curiosity Model breaks down curiosity from a single "how much" trait into five distinct dimensions, each with different emotional tones and motivations. For NET preparation, focus on the core definitions of these five dimensions and the key revisions of the scale (5DC → 5DCR).

### 🎯 Kashdan's Five-Dimensional Curiosity Model

**1. Core Idea**

- **Definition**: Kashdan et al. (2018) proposed that curiosity is not a single dimension, but rather a multidimensional construct composed of **five distinct factors**. A person's curiosity profile is shaped by their combined scores across these dimensions, not by their overall "curiosity level" .
- **Key Insight**: Rather than asking "How curious are you?", the model asks "**How are you curious?**" Different dimensions have completely different emotional tones—some are pleasant (Joyous Exploration), while others are driven by discomfort (Deprivation Sensitivity) .

**2. The Five Dimensions (Must Know for NET)**

| Dimension                   | Core Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| :-------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Joyous Exploration**      | The prototype of curiosity: the **pleasurable** experience of discovering new information, learning, and growing. Driven by interest and enjoyment .                                                         |
| **Deprivation Sensitivity** | Curiosity with an **anxious and tense** emotional tone. Driven by the discomfort of a "knowledge gap," seeking answers to **eliminate** this discomfort, rather than for enjoyment .                         |
| **Stress Tolerance**        | The willingness to **accept** the anxiety, confusion, and distress that novelty and uncertainty bring. Without this dimension, a person may avoid exploration even when curious .                            |
| **Social Curiosity**        | The desire to know what others are thinking and doing. Divided into **Overt** (directly asking, listening, understanding others) and **Covert** (gossiping, snooping, indirect acquisition of information) . |
| **Thrill Seeking**          | The willingness to take physical, social, and financial risks to obtain varied, complex, and intense experiences. Anxiety is not to be reduced, but to be **amplified** for pleasure .                       |

**3. Scale Revision: 5DC → 5DCR (Key Update)**

- **Original 5DC (2018)**: 25 items measuring 5 factors. Social Curiosity was a single dimension .
- **Revised 5DCR (2020)**: Split **Social Curiosity** into two sub-dimensions—**Overt Social Curiosity** (associated with healthy outcomes like agreeableness and interpersonal competence) and **Covert Social Curiosity** (associated with gossip and negative emotionality), while shortening each subscale to 4 items (24 items total) .

**4. Practical Implications (Quick Points)**

- **Well-being**: Joyous Exploration is positively associated with psychological need satisfaction and well-being; Deprivation Sensitivity may interfere with life satisfaction due to its embedded anxiety .
- **Workplace**: Curiosity (especially Joyous Exploration) predicts innovative behavior and engagement, and is a stronger predictor of innovation than mindfulness .

---

**Exam Tip**: Remember **Kashdan = 5DC (Joyous Exploration, Deprivation Sensitivity, Stress Tolerance, Social Curiosity, Thrill Seeking)**. The key distinction is the **emotional tone**: Joyous Exploration = **pleasure-driven**, Deprivation Sensitivity = **discomfort-driven**. Know the revision: **5DCR split Social Curiosity into Overt/Covert**, with Overt being healthy and Covert being problematic.
